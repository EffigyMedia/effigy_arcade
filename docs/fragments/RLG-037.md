### RLG-037 — Interstate: scatter and the lane-open guarantee pull against each other
- **RULED 2026-08-28T16:06:42-04:00** — - **The two systems pull against each other.** `scatter` moves a car sideways because it was asked to;
  `keepLaneOpen` guarantees a car-width corridor always exists. A scattered car can be the one that
  closes the road, and `keepLaneOpen` only corrects it on a later frame.

- **Measured across six runs of `traffic-test` with the horn phase active**, the narrowest corridor
  came out at **0.227, 0.323, 0.363, 0.418, 0.428 and 0.452** against a 0.34 limit. **Two of the six
  are under it.** Without the horn phase the same test sat comfortably at 0.418 to 0.452.

- **So the guarantee is not absolute while the horn is in use**, and the test is stochastic - it will
  pass most runs and fail some. That is worth more than a green tick: a check that passes four times
  in six is a check that will be believed and should not be.

- **One fix was tried and made it worse.** Judging a merge on its swept path - treating the car as
  occupying everything between where it is and where it is going, which is what it physically does -
  took the corridor from 0.323 to **0.227**. It was reverted. The reason it backfired is not
  understood, and guessing again at the end of an unrelated unit is how the occlusion code ended up
  disabled for a release.

- **Where to start.** `keepLaneOpen` runs once per frame before the traffic loop, and `scatter` runs
  from input handling, so a scattered car blocks for at least one frame before anything looks at it.
  The ordering is the first thing to check. The second is whether the corridor test should consider
  a car's committed merge target rather than only its current position - the opposite of the swept
  path, and untried.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T16:22:09-04:00** — - **IMPROVED, MEASURABLY, BUT NOT CLOSED.** `wouldBlock` gathered cars within +/-1600 of the mover -
  one window's width, centred on the car. But `keepLaneOpen` measures windows 1600 wide **stepped
  every 800**, so a car near a window boundary could pass its own centred check and still close the
  window beside it - which is the one that reports the road blocked. Reaching a full window either
  side (+/-2400) means every window that will later judge the car is inside what is checked now.

- **The measurement series, which is the honest record of where this stands.**

  | Condition | Narrowest corridor (limit 0.34) |
  |---|---|
  | before any fix | 0.227, 0.323 (both failing), 0.363, 0.418, 0.428, 0.452 |
  | after the +/-2400 reach | 0.463, 0.378, 0.342, **0.300** |

  So it fails roughly one run in four instead of one in three, and the passing runs sit higher. **That
  is an improvement and it is not a guarantee**, and the difference matters because the whole point of
  this rule is that it is absolute.

- **A longer horn phase reproduces it far more reliably than driving does**, which is the most useful
  thing learned here: scatter is the pressure, not ordinary merging. That is the condition to develop
  against.

- **One approach tried and rejected with evidence.** Judging a merge on its swept path - treating the
  car as occupying everything between where it is and where it is going, which is what it physically
  does - made it **worse**, 0.323 to 0.227. Reverted. Why it backfired is still not understood, and
  that is worth knowing before it is tried again.

- **Untried, and the next thing to look at.** `keepLaneOpen` runs once per frame before the traffic
  loop while `scatter` runs from input handling, so a scattered car blocks for at least one frame
  before anything examines it. Ordering is the first suspect. The second is whether the corridor test
  should consider a car's committed merge TARGET rather than its current position - the opposite of
  the swept path.
