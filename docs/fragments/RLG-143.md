### RLG-143 — A bridge rises over water at ground level; a tunnel drops under ground level. One shape, negated
- **RULED 2026-09-01T13:54:40-04:00** — RULED 2026-09-01 - Owner, having driven the bridge:

"The bridge and the tunnel both have the same design in that the bridge starts as a plane of
water level to the ground and tarmac and the roadway of the bridge starts by going up high
above the water and then levels out for most of the duration of its existence where it's
verticality is capped to 0. Then at the end of the bridge, the roadway goes down to meet with
the next biome where the plane of water is still level with its ground and tarmac.

The tunnel does the opposite when you enter it goes down and then its verticality is capped to
0. And at the end of the tunnel, it moves back up to meet the ground of the next biome."

THIS IS ONE SHAPE WITH A SIGN, AND IT SETTLES A QUESTION THAT WAS LEFT OPEN. [[RLG-112]]
built the authored profile for the bridge and deliberately did NOT give the tunnel one,
because the owner had already capped a bore's form at 0.10 on both axes and one field would
have overruled that ruling. The owner has now stated the tunnel's shape directly and it is the
bridge's, negated: down, flat, up. So the tunnel takes a profile after all, and the earlier
caution was right to wait for this rather than to guess it.

AND THE WATER IS AT GROUND LEVEL, WHICH IS THE PART THAT CHANGES WHAT IS BUILT. v0.11.11 draws
the sea as a plane a stated thirteen camera heights below the deck - a constant, `WATER_DROP`,
chosen by eye. The owner's model has no such constant in it: the water is level with the
ground and tarmac of the place either side, and the DECK is what moves. The height of the
bridge above the sea is therefore not a number anybody picks. It is the integral of the
profile's own rise, and it is correct by construction at every point of the crossing including
the two ends, where it goes to zero because the road has come back down to meet the land.

THAT IS ALSO THE ANSWER TO A CONSTANT THAT WAS ALREADY SUSPECT. This fragment's own record
now names the same fault twice - a value typed in beside a value that is derived, which
drifts - and `WATER_DROP` is exactly that shape. Deriving the drop from the profile removes
it.

WHAT IT NEEDS FROM THE ENGINE, AND IT IS ONE ADDITION. The vertical is integrated in screen
space with no absolute altitude, and the authored profile REPLACES the slope inside an event -
so the road's accumulated height already carries the rise and there is nothing to subtract it
with. A second accumulator, integrating the ordinary rolled grade THROUGH the event as though
no profile were in force, gives the level the land would have been at. The water is drawn at
that, the deck at the road's own, and the gap between them is the bridge's height. The tunnel
needs no second surface, but the same accumulator tells it how far below the surrounding
ground its floor lies, which is what a mouth has to agree with.

TWO THINGS TO WATCH.

  THE MOUTHS ARE WHERE THIS WILL FAIL. Both places must MEET the neighbouring ground exactly,
  and the profile already returns to zero slope at both ends - but zero SLOPE is not zero
  HEIGHT, and it is the height that has to match. The ramp has to bring the road back to the
  level it left, or a bridge hands over to a desert from thirty feet up.

  AND THE CAP IS ALREADY BUILT FOR THE BRIDGE AND NOT FOR THE TUNNEL. "Verticality capped to
  0" is what the flat deck already does. The tunnel's own `hill` and `bend` stay at 0.10 by
  the earlier ruling; the profile overrides the vertical inside the event and the 0.10 goes on
  governing what little the bore does elsewhere. Do not remove it while adding the profile -
  it is not the same number answering the same question.
