### RLG-110 — The car sits in neutral through the count-in, so it can be launched on GO
- **RULED 2026-08-31T10:55:37-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: "During the countdown, we should put the car in neutral so you can rev, which means
the car can launch on go."

THE FREE-REVVING NEUTRAL IS ALREADY BUILT, WHICH CHANGES WHAT THIS COSTS. `engineRpm()` already has a
NEUTRAL branch: with the gear out of range the revs let go of the road entirely, rise toward the
limiter on the throttle, BOUNCE off it rather than pinning against the stop, and fall away under their
own inertia when the throttle is released. Its note records why it was built that way. So the physics
the owner is asking for exists and is tuned; what is missing is the wiring and the payoff.

TWO THINGS STAND IN THE WAY AND BOTH ARE SMALL. The neutral branch is gated on `optManual`, so a
player on the automatic gearbox - which is the default and will be most players - falls into the other
branch, which CLAMPS the gear back to 1 and returns revs derived from road speed. A car at a standstill
in that branch reads idle whatever the throttle does. And `autoGear(dt)` runs every frame when the
gearbox is automatic and would shift straight out of neutral. Both need the count-in to be a state
they respect, not a special case for one gearbox.

THE PAYOFF IS THE DESIGN DECISION AND IT IS THE OWNER'S. "The car can launch on go" can mean two
different games:

  A WINDOW. There is a band of revs that launches well, and above or below it you bog or spin. That is
  the drag-strip version, it rewards a skill that can be learnt in three attempts, and it makes the
  count-in something to DO rather than something to wait through.

  A CURVE. More revs is simply more launch, up to the limiter. Simpler, forgiving, and it turns the
  count-in into "hold the pedal", which is what it already is.

THE WINDOW IS THE ONE WORTH BUILDING, and the engine is already half set up for it: `bogT` exists and
already tracks an engine labouring in too tall a gear, and grip is already a real number that weather
moves through `wetGrip()`. A launch that spins the wheels on a wet road and hooks up on a dry one
falls out of quantities that exist, which is the same shape as everything else that works here. But it
is a decision about how the game plays and it should be made deliberately.

ONE PHYSICS FOR EVERY CAR STILL HOLDS. The launch window must not be a per-class number. A car with
more torque and less grip is harder to launch because of its numbers, not because a branch names it -
which is the standing ruling and is also the more interesting outcome.

IT SHIPS TO BOTH GAMES. `road.js` serves the Interstate and the circuit, and a grid start with a
rev-and-drop is if anything more at home on the circuit than on the highway. Whatever is built has to
be right for both or gated at the `CFG` seam deliberately, not by accident.

AND THE START PROMPT BECOMES WRONG. `drawStartPrompt` currently reads "HOLD THE RIGHT PEDAL" and
"STANDING START - 1ST GEAR". Neither is true of a car in neutral. This project's rule is that a
gesture is taught in play the first time it matters, so the prompt is the right place to teach the
launch - but it has to say what the car is actually doing.

MEASURED, NOT FELT. A harness can hold the throttle through the count-in and read `API` for the revs
at GO and the speed a second later, across a range of held revs. That produces the launch curve as a
table, which is what would say whether a window exists and where it is - and it is the only way to
tune it without the owner driving twenty starts.
- **RULED 2026-08-31T14:49:22-04:00** — BUILT in v0.10.27, as one unit with [[RLG-118]]. The box goes to NEUTRAL for the count-in on either gearbox, the engine revs freely against the throttle, and what the revs buy is a WINDOW.

THE OWNER DECIDED THE PAYOFF, 2026-08-31, and it is the reason the count-in is worth sitting through. Hold the needle inside the band when the gear lands and the car goes; land below it and the car bogs; land above it and the tyres let go and the engine screams while the car sits still. The alternative put to the owner was a plain curve where more revs is always more launch, and it was rejected for the reason that makes it simple: the correct play would always be to hold the limiter, so there would be no decision to make.

THE WINDOW IS A FACT ABOUT THE CAR, and the owner decided that too, in two parts. WHERE it sits comes from power-to-weight: a car with torque to spare launches off lower revs and lights the tyres up if you use more, while a car with very little power has to be wound up before it has anything to give. A COMET launches best at 0.514 of its redline and a van at 0.764. HOW WIDE it is runs the other way from the owner's first assumption, which was put to them with the fleet's numbers: the quicker the car, the TIGHTER the band. A supercar is 0.12 to 0.15 of the dial wide and a road car 0.22 to 0.30, because a perfect launch is worth about 34mph in a COMET and about 6mph in a van - the narrow gate belongs in front of the large reward, not the small one. The body's own `launch` stat still moves the width secondarily, so MATADOR ("FASTEST OFF THE LINE", 1.20) stays more forgiving than STALLION ("SLOWER OFF THE LINE", 0.88) inside the same class. `grip` sets the top edge, because the top edge is the moment the tyres stop holding, and a wet road pulls it down again through `wetGrip()`.

THE TWO THINGS IN THE WAY WERE BOTH SMALL, exactly as the ruling said. `engineRpm()`'s neutral branch was gated on `optManual`; the test is now what the box is IN rather than which box it is, so an automatic reaches it. `autoGear` never needed changing: it is called after the count-in returns from `step()`, so it re-engages first on the GO frame and that landing is what fires the launch.

AND THE COUNT-IN CAN NO LONGER BE SKIPPED WITH THE THROTTLE. [[RLG-088]] let a repeat count be shortened to a third of a second on a press of the gas, because three seconds of waiting is a toll on the player enjoying the game most. The throttle is now how the revs are set, so the button that skipped the count is the button the count exists for, and a third of a second is not long enough to catch a window in. The shortcut was removed rather than moved to another control. The toll RLG-088 was fixing is now paid by the feature instead: the three seconds are something to do rather than something to sit through. THE OWNER MAY WANT THIS BACK on some other control and it is one line.

THE PEDAL IS A BUTTON, NOT A HINGE, and that decided the rev dynamics. A constant throttle opening is not something a thumb can hold, so at the mid-race rates - 0.22 up and 0.045 down - the needle fell out of the middle of a band in about four frames, seventy milliseconds, and the window would have been a coin toss. While a launch is armed the rates are 0.050 and 0.005: about half a second to walk the needle from idle into a band, and about the same again to drift out of the bottom of one. The player blips it up on the last number and rides it into GO. WHETHER THAT IS THE RIGHT WEIGHT IS THE OWNER'S, ON THE DEVICE - a harness can prove the band is reachable and cannot say how it feels under a thumb.

THE WINDOW IS SHOWN ON THE TACHOMETER, at the owner's instruction: a temporary amber section on the dial face, in the same groove as the red zone, with a brighter tick at the middle. It is up only while a launch is live. It is painted on the face rather than beside it because the thing the player has to line up is the NEEDLE - a band anywhere else asks them to read two places at once and work out how the two relate. An earlier version of this drew a second copy of the band across the middle of the screen and it was removed for that reason.

AND `drawStartPrompt` WAS NEVER CALLED. It has been in the file since the fork saying HOLD THE RIGHT PEDAL and STANDING START - 1ST GEAR, and nothing in the draw path referred to it, so neither line was ever on the screen and neither was ever noticed to be wrong. It is drawn now, under the count, and it carries words only: NEUTRAL / REV IT while the count runs, then the verdict - PERFECT LAUNCH, GOOD LAUNCH, BOGGED or WHEELSPIN - with the needle position and the band in numbers under it, so a player who has just been told WHEELSPIN can see by how much.

TWO MALFORMED SKID RECORDS WERE FIXED IN PASSING, and they are the same mechanism. The existing drop-into-gear launch pushed `{ z, x, life, w }` into `skids`, while `stepRubber` ages a mark by `t` and paints it by `heat`. So the launch mark had no heat to draw with and no clock to expire on, and sat on the road until the player drove 2,000 units past it. Both now go through `layRubber`, which is the one place that knows the shape.

PROVED, AND THE PROOF IS NOT VACUOUS. `tools/launch-test.py` is new; it passed as [[EVD-194]]. It drives three starts on the DEFAULT automatic gearbox - never touching the throttle, holding it flat, and walking the needle toward the middle of the band - and asserts the RELATION between where the needle actually was and what the game said happened, which cannot pass by accident. Measured: 0.084 of the redline gave BOGGED, 0.959 gave WHEELSPIN, and 0.739 gave PERFECT LAUNCH, worth 578 speed units a second after GO against 307 for the wheelspin and 0 for the bog. The gate was then put back by hand - `if(!optManual){ gear = 1; return gearRpm(gear, spd); }` ahead of the neutral branch - and the checks failed exactly as they should: the box in neutral FAILED reporting gear 1, the held throttle FAILED at 0.084 of the redline, and all three starts FAILED to reach a verdict at all.

NOT VERIFIED, AND IT CANNOT BE FROM HERE: whether the amber band is legible on a phone at the size the dial is drawn, whether the window can be held with a thumb rather than by a harness sampling the needle every fifty milliseconds, and whether the band's WIDTH is right for each class. All three are the owner's on the device.
