### RLG-110 — The car sits in neutral through the count-in, so it can be launched on GO
- **RULED 2026-08-31T10:55:37-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: "During the countdown, we should put the car in neutral so you can rev, which means
the car can launch on go."

THE FREE-REVVING NEUTRAL IS ALREADY BUILT, WHICH CHANGES WHAT THIS COSTS. `engineRpm()` already has a
NEUTRAL branch: with the gear out of range the revs let go of the road entirely, rise toward the
limiter on the throttle, BOUNCE off it rather than pinning against the stop, and fall away under their
own inertia when the throttle is released. Its note records why it was built that way. So the physics
the owner is asking for exists and is tuned; what is missing is the wiring and the payoff.

TWO THINGS STAND IN THE WAY AND BOTH ARE SMALL. The neutral branch is gated on `optManual`, so a
player on the automatic gearbox - which is the default and will be most players - falls into the other
branch, which CLAMPS the gear back to 1 and returns revs derived from road speed. A car at a standstill
in that branch reads idle whatever the throttle does. And `autoGear(dt)` runs every frame when the
gearbox is automatic and would shift straight out of neutral. Both need the count-in to be a state
they respect, not a special case for one gearbox.

THE PAYOFF IS THE DESIGN DECISION AND IT IS THE OWNER'S. "The car can launch on go" can mean two
different games:

  A WINDOW. There is a band of revs that launches well, and above or below it you bog or spin. That is
  the drag-strip version, it rewards a skill that can be learnt in three attempts, and it makes the
  count-in something to DO rather than something to wait through.

  A CURVE. More revs is simply more launch, up to the limiter. Simpler, forgiving, and it turns the
  count-in into "hold the pedal", which is what it already is.

THE WINDOW IS THE ONE WORTH BUILDING, and the engine is already half set up for it: `bogT` exists and
already tracks an engine labouring in too tall a gear, and grip is already a real number that weather
moves through `wetGrip()`. A launch that spins the wheels on a wet road and hooks up on a dry one
falls out of quantities that exist, which is the same shape as everything else that works here. But it
is a decision about how the game plays and it should be made deliberately.

ONE PHYSICS FOR EVERY CAR STILL HOLDS. The launch window must not be a per-class number. A car with
more torque and less grip is harder to launch because of its numbers, not because a branch names it -
which is the standing ruling and is also the more interesting outcome.

IT SHIPS TO BOTH GAMES. `road.js` serves the Interstate and the circuit, and a grid start with a
rev-and-drop is if anything more at home on the circuit than on the highway. Whatever is built has to
be right for both or gated at the `CFG` seam deliberately, not by accident.

AND THE START PROMPT BECOMES WRONG. `drawStartPrompt` currently reads "HOLD THE RIGHT PEDAL" and
"STANDING START - 1ST GEAR". Neither is true of a car in neutral. This project's rule is that a
gesture is taught in play the first time it matters, so the prompt is the right place to teach the
launch - but it has to say what the car is actually doing.

MEASURED, NOT FELT. A harness can hold the throttle through the count-in and read `API` for the revs
at GO and the speed a second later, across a range of held revs. That produces the launch curve as a
table, which is what would say whether a window exists and where it is - and it is the only way to
tune it without the owner driving twenty starts.
