### RLG-041 — Vehicles disappear and reappear - a rendering pop still present
- **RULED 2026-08-28T17:33:06-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28:** "There is definitely still occasional rendering glitches with the vehicles
where they will disappear and reappear."

Reported from play on a real device. **Not yet reproduced by any harness, and not yet characterised
- this fragment records the report, not a diagnosis.**

**Do not guess at the cause.** "Occasional" and "disappear and reappear" fit at least four distinct
mechanisms already present in this engine, and the record already contains one wrong plan built on a
plausible-sounding rendering cause:

- the draw skipping a car that is out of view, where the view test disagrees with the spawner
  ([[RLG-031]] fixed one of these: things were placed inside the drawn road)
- the rearview level-of-detail fade ([[RLG-020]])
- partial crest occlusion ([[RLG-021]]) - a car behind a rise
- the painter selection for a sprite that has no entry, which renders nothing and raises no error

**The first unit on this is a measurement, not a fix.** It needs to say WHICH car, at what distance,
in what part of the frame, and whether the object still exists in `traffic` or `racers` at the
moment it is not drawn. An object that is still in the array and not painted is a draw fault; an
object that has left the array is a spawn or cull fault. Those are different bugs and the harness
must be able to tell them apart before anybody edits a painter.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T22:49:52-04:00** — **MEASURED in v0.9.11, no fix, and the fragment's own question is answered.** `tools/pop-test.py` is
new. It reads a ledger in the painter behind `API.watchDraw()`: every vehicle offered to
`drawSprite` records the reason it did or did not appear, using the painter's OWN exits rather than
this harness's idea of them. The collector runs on `requestAnimationFrame` so it sees every frame,
and the engine returns a frame number so a missed frame is counted as the collector's fault rather
than reported as a vanished car.

**THE ANSWER: it is a DRAW fault, and it is not a cull fault.** Four invocations, 30-45 seconds each
on the Interstate with a full field and traffic:

| | run 1 | run 2 | run 3 | run 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pops per minute - painted, not painted, painted again, **never leaving the array** | 78.0 | 77.3 | 144.0 | 68.0 |
| **left the array while being painted** - a cull or a spawn | **0** | **0** | **0** | **0** |

Not one vehicle in four runs disappeared because it had been culled or had failed to spawn. Every
disappearance was an object that still existed, in `traffic` or `racers`, that the painter declined
to paint. That is the distinction the ruling demanded before anybody edits anything, and it removes
one of the four candidate mechanisms the fragment listed.

**WHERE THE PAINTER DECLINES, per vehicle-frame, and the whole set of reasons:**

| reason | share | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| `unbucketed` | 68-72% | outside the drawn road. Behind you, mostly - traffic lives to 34,000 back and the road draws 30,000 forward |
| `drawn` | 16-22% | painted |
| `clipped` | 4.7% | across a crest, painted partially. Deliberate, [[RLG-021]] |
| `crest` | 2.2-3.5% | entirely under a hill |
| `offscreen` | 1.7-2.1% | off the top or bottom of the frame |
| `behind` | 0.8-0.9% | nearer than 430 units - behind the camera plane |
| `unemitted` | 0.2-0.6% | bucketed, and the road never painted that slice |

`tiny`, `huge`, `noproj` and `nosprite` did not occur once. **`nosprite` not occurring removes a
second candidate** - the painter-with-no-entry mechanism the fragment listed is not firing here.

**A FLICKER IS NOT AN OCCLUSION, and separating them is what makes this measurement useful.** A car
gone for two seconds while you drive over a rise is a hill doing its job and reads as one. A car gone
for three frames at mid-distance reads as a glitch, which is the word the owner used. Split at five
frames:

| flickers, 5 frames or fewer | per minute | dominant reason | where |
|---|---|---|---|
| run 3 | 42.7 | `unemitted` 22, `unbucketed` 9 | 11,717 and 30,274 units ahead |
| run 4 | 17.3 | `unemitted` 8, `unbucketed` 4 | 11,098 and 30,301 units ahead |

**Two mechanisms, and both are stable across runs even though the rate is not.**

1. **`unemitted` at about 11,000 units.** A bucket that alternates between emitted and not from one
   frame to the next. The road pass emits a sprite bucket when it paints that slice, and an
   unemitted bucket IS the occlusion mechanism - so this is a slice being skipped and unskipped at
   mid-distance, in a place where nothing should be occluding it for three frames.
2. **`unbucketed` at about 30,300 units.** That is the draw distance - `DRAW * SEG` is 30,000. A car
   at the far edge crosses in and out of the bucketed range as `base` advances, and pops with it.

**WHAT THIS RUN CANNOT TELL YOU, and it must not be read as a clean bill.** It is a headless desktop
browser at 480x900 running at 60fps. The owner's report is from a phone. A pop that needs a frame-time
spike, a different device pixel ratio or a mobile GPU will not appear here, and the rate varying by a
factor of two between invocations - 68 to 144 per minute - is a reminder that this is a noisy
measurement of a road that is different every run. The between-session lesson from [[RLG-033]] applies
in full.

**Next, and NOT started:** take the two mechanisms one at a time. The `unemitted` flicker at
mid-distance is the one that matches the report; the draw-distance edge is a second, milder one that
may be acceptable at the horizon. Neither is a fix anybody should attempt from this record alone -
the instrument now exists, so the next unit can change one thing and watch the number move.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T23:19:16-04:00** — **FIXED in v0.9.12, one mechanism, and the number moved.** The engine had TWO occlusion tests for
terrain and the coarse one was doing most of the work.

**How it was found.** The instrument was extended to record, for every vehicle in an unemitted
bucket, HOW NEARLY its slice was painted - the smaller of the two misses that would have emitted it,
in screen pixels. A car behind a hill and a car on the tangent of a rise are the same `unemitted`
event and are not the same thing:

| flickering cars, misses in pixels | median | min | max | under 4px |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| run A | **0.3** | 0.0 | 2.5 | 5 of 5 |
| run B | **0.9** | 0.0 | 1.8 | 8 of 8 |

**Every flickering car in two runs sat on a slice that missed being painted by under a pixel.** Not
one was behind a hill. A slice on a crest tangent crosses that line back and forth as the road
moves, and its cars went with it.

**The fix is to delete the second test rather than to soften it.** The first attempt was a
tolerance - a slice missing by less than the painter's own crest slop still emits its sprites - and
it worked exactly as far as it was aimed: the sub-pixel population vanished and the surviving misses
moved to a median of 5.8 and 5.3 pixels. That is the shape of a continuum being cut at an arbitrary
point, and it said the test itself was wrong rather than badly tuned.

`crestY` is a REAL silhouette test. `buildHillClip` runs at the top of every frame and records the
highest the road reached before each segment, so `crestY(z)` is the brow between you and that point,
computed from geometry rather than from what happened to be painted. Every sprite already passes
through it in `drawSprite`, which draws it whole, cuts it off at the brow, or culls it. The bucket
gate was a second, coarser answer to the same question, and it is the one that flickered.

**So the bucket is always emitted now**, and `crestY` is the only thing that hides a car behind
terrain. The ground fill and `groundMax` are untouched - the terrain is drawn exactly as it was.

**The proof that the coarse test had been doing the work is in `occlusion-test`, and it is stark:**

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| culled - entirely under a crest | **0** | **167** |
| clipped - cut off at the brow | 14 | 160 |
| drawn | 411 | 336 |

Cars fully behind a crest were being lost by the bucket gate before the real test ever saw them,
which is why the engine reported zero culls while occlusion visibly worked.

**And the pop it was built to find:**

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| flickers per minute, 5 frames or fewer | 16.0 / 28.0 | **8.0 / 12.0** |
| where they are | 11,000-23,000 units, mid-road | **all of them at 30,000, the draw edge** |
| `unemitted` share of vehicle-frames | 0.2-0.6% | 0.1% |
| left the array while painted | 0 | **0** |

**The mid-road flicker class is gone.** What remains is the second mechanism, characterized and NOT
fixed: at the draw distance the sprite bucket range runs to `DRAW+1` and the road pass only walks to
`DRAW`, so the last bucket is emitted only as a side effect of the slice in front of it, and beyond
that a car is `unbucketed`. Cars there are about one pixel wide and the absence lasts a median of
ONE frame. It is a real pop and it is not the one that was reported; changing anything at the draw
edge risks a worse pop-in, so it is left recorded rather than tuned.

**Unchanged, and still true: this says nothing about a phone.** Every number here is a headless
desktop browser at 480x900. The mechanism removed was geometric rather than timing-dependent, which
is a reason to expect it to travel - but the owner's report is the only thing that can close this,
and it should be re-tested on the device before anyone marks it done.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T03:43:46-04:00** — **THE OWNER TESTED v0.9.12 ON THE DEVICE, 2026-08-29, and it is better but not gone:**

> "At first there were no flickers and I was excited but then I started seeing them. Usually at the
> precipice of the road dipping down, but it wasn't every car, and sometimes when the cars go up. I
> don't notice it in the rearview, but that may only be because my eyes are more focused down the
> road ahead."

**That report is a diagnosis, and it named the mechanism.** "At the precipice of the road dipping
down" is the crest test, and "not every car" is a test that flips for cars near the brow and for no
others.

**FOUND, and it is an off-by-one between a table and its lookup.** `buildHillClip` fills
`hillClip[n]` for the segment `base + n`, where `base` is `floor(pos/SEG)`. `crestY` looked it up
with `floor((worldZ - pos)/SEG)` - the count of segments AHEAD OF THE PLAYER, which is the same
number only when the car and the player sit at the same offset within their segments. Otherwise it
is one lower, **and which of the two it is flips every time `pos` crosses a segment boundary - about
57 times a second at speed.**

On flat road the two entries hold the same value and nothing shows. At a crest they differ sharply,
so a car near the brow flipped between hidden and drawn several times a second. Cars away from a
crest never flickered, which is the "not every car" in the report.

**Measured before the change:** the two conventions disagreed on **24 vehicle-frames in 45 seconds,
in each of two runs** - roughly one flip every two seconds, and only ever on cars at a crest. Fixed
by using the index the table was filled with. The watch still computes the retired convention, so
the number stays readable rather than being asserted from memory.

**After: flickers 14.7 and 1.3 per minute, and every one of them at the draw edge.** No mid-road
flicker in either run.

**CORRECTION, and it is mine rather than the engine's. The v0.9.12 entry above claims:**

> "The proof that the coarse test had been doing the work is in `occlusion-test`, and it is stark:
> culled 0 -> 167, clipped 14 -> 160."

**WITHDRAWN. That comparison is one run against one run, and `occlusion-test` does not survive
that.** Three runs on a single unchanged build:

| run | drawn | clipped | culled |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 181 | 5 | **0** |
| 2 | 257 | 59 | **40** |
| 3 | 265 | 101 | **147** |

The terrain is generated fresh each run and a 30-second stint either meets hills or does not. `0`
and `167` are both inside the ordinary spread, so the numbers I quoted are evidence of the terrain
and not of the change. **The reasoning behind that change still stands on its own** - a bucket gate
and `crestY` are two answers to one question, and the sub-pixel misses were measured across two runs
each - but the occlusion figures were not proof of it and should not be read as any.

**The lesson is the one this project keeps re-learning, in a new place.** [[RLG-033]] recorded that a
seeded session fixes its grid and between-session variance is the larger kind. `occlusion-test` is
worse than that: it has no seed at all, its run is short, and the single variable it is most
sensitive to - how hilly the road happened to be - is invisible in its output. **A single
`occlusion-test` run cannot be compared with another.** Run 1 above also FAILED its own
`drawn > 200` assertion, which makes it a flaky gate as well as an unrepeatable measurement.
