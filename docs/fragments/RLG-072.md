### RLG-072 — The garage shows both ends of the car
- **RULED 2026-08-29T09:53:18-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-29: the garage shows the front and the back of the selected car, even at the cost of
showing each smaller.

Only the tail was ever built for the player's car, because the tail is the only end you see while
driving. The garage is the one screen where the player looks AT the car instead of following it, and
a car you have never seen the face of is half a car - which mattered more the moment every front in
the fleet was drawn and wired (RLG-053).

`buildSprites` now builds `SP.playerFront` beside `SP.player`, through the same two painters the
fleet sheet uses: `paintRigFront` at the rig's box for a road car, `paintFront` at 230x215 for
everything else. Built once per change of car or paint rather than per frame of a menu.

The card draws both ends into their own half of the canvas, standing on one floor line, so they read
as one car rather than as two pictures. Each fell from 240 pixels wide to about 126, which is the
price the owner accepted. The canvas lost fifty pixels of height with it: two cars side by side are
limited by the width of their half, so a 180-tall card left a band of nothing above them, and on a
phone that band is scroll.

`showUnlock` draws through the same function, so a newly won car is also shown from both ends without
that screen knowing anything about it.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T10:25:11-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-29: the front and back views in the garage are not rendered aligned.

They were bottom-aligned by their sprite BOXES, and the boxes are different shapes - a rear is
220x168, a supercar's front is 230x215, a rig's front is 220x168 - with the car sitting in a
different place inside each. So one end floated above the other and the two were drawn at different
sizes, which is not two views of one car.

The painted CONTENT of each sprite is measured now, once and cached on the sprite, by scanning for
pixels above an alpha of 24. Both ends are then drawn at ONE scale, chosen so the larger of the two
fits its half, with their content bottoms on the same line.

The alpha threshold is not zero on purpose: a sprite's ground shadow fades to nothing at its edge,
and counting the last almost-transparent pixel as part of the car puts the floor line below anything
the car actually reaches.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T11:44:55-04:00** — THE CARS WERE NOT MISALIGNED. THEY WERE DIFFERENT SHAPES, and two attempts to place them better could
not have worked, because placement was never the fault. The owner said it three times and was right
every time; each of my first two fixes measured the wrong thing and then declared victory on it.

WHAT THE MEASUREMENT ACTUALLY SAID, once it was pointed at the sprites rather than at the card. Every
road car draws its front and its rear into the SAME canvas at the SAME top row - a COUPE is rows 37
to 164 from behind and 37 to 167 from the front - and a supercar's two ends are 143 rows tall in both
of their differently-sized canvases. The painters agree with each other exactly, which is what the
owner said when he asked whether the two ends had not been measured to the same dimensions.

THE FAULT WAS A HARDCODED SPRITE BOX. `buildSprites` paints the player's TAIL into that shape's own
canvas - a ROADSTER is 206x150 - and I had written its FACE into a flat 220x168. The same car in two
canvases of different aspect ratio comes out with different proportions, and no amount of aligning
boxes, floors or widths can bring two different shapes into agreement.

The same flat 220x168 was in three other places, put there by the same hand for the same reason: the
rival front cache, the rival REAR cache, and the fleet sheet. So every sports-class rival on the road
has been drawn at the wrong aspect ratio against the traffic version of the same shape.

There is one `rigBox(rig)` now and everything reads it. The table already existed in `buildSprites`
and in the traffic build; the copies that were wrong were the ones written from memory.

MEASURED AFTER: the two ends of a ROADSTER differ by 2 pixels in roof height, 0 in floor line and 2
in width, on a car drawn 126 pixels wide. What remains is the waistline, four pixels apart, because a
bonnet line sits higher than a boot line on the same car - which is the car rather than the drawing.

THE LESSON, and it is about the checking rather than about the sprites. Twice I measured the thing I
had just changed - bounding boxes on the card - and reported it as agreement. Neither measurement
could see the fault, because the fault was upstream of both. WHEN A FIX DOES NOT CONVINCE THE PERSON
LOOKING AT IT, MEASURE SOMETHING THE FIX DOES NOT TOUCH.
