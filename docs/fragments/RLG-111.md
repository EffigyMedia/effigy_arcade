### RLG-111 — Standing water and a scheduled thunderclap survived a reset
- **RULED 2026-08-31T11:01:03-04:00** — Queue item 7: a sweep for run state with the same shape as [[RLG-090]]'s - a value the reset does not
clear, or a counter armed at zero rather than at a full interval. Two were found and both are real.

STANDING WATER SURVIVED A RESET, AND IT TOOK A QUARTER OF THE GRIP WITH IT. `freshWorld` reset
`settle` - settled snow - and did not reset `pool` - standing water. They are the same idea, they are
read by the same two functions, and they sit two lines apart in the source. So a run that ended in a
downpour handed the next one a soaked road.

MEASURED, AND THE NUMBER IS THE ARGUMENT. Soak the road, restart, and ask:

    worldState after the restart:   wet 0, settle 0            - a completely fresh run
    wetGrip() after the restart:    0.740
    wetGrip() on a genuinely dry start: 1.000

A quarter of the car's grip, inherited from a run that is over, on a road that reports itself dry.
That is RLG-090's own complaint in a variable it did not name: the owner's words then were "you start
in a dry biome with snow slipperiness still applied", and this is the same sentence with rain in it.

THE RATES WENT WITH THEM. `poolFall` and `settleFall` are how fast the last fall was depositing, kept
so the thaw can run back down the same slope. Left behind, a new run melts its snow at the previous
run's rate. They are reset with the values they belong to.

AND A CLAP OF THUNDER COULD OUTLIVE ITS OWN RUN. `thunderIn` is a SCHEDULED SOUND - a strike sets it
to between 250ms and 5 seconds and the stepper plays the clap when it runs out. Nothing reset it. A
bolt in the last second of a run made its noise in the next one, over a road that may have no weather
at all. `boltNext` is armed to a full interval rather than to zero, because RLG-090's rule applies to
it as much as to the other three: a run that has just started is not a run that is due to be struck by
lightning.

WHY NO CHECK CAUGHT THIS, AND IT IS THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST. `retry-test` asserts that a fresh
run inherits nothing, and its own docstring says it "asks the engine what a run owns rather than
listing it here" - by reading `API.worldState`. But `worldState` reports exactly the fields
`freshWorld` resets. So the field list was BOTH the reset and the definition of clean, and anything
missing from the reset was missing from the check by construction. A leak could not be seen by the
instrument built to see leaks.

THAT IS FIXED BY MAKING THE LIST WIDER THAN THE RESET. `pool` and `grip` and `thunderIn` and `boltIn`
are published now whether or not they are reset, and the new assertions are separate lines rather than
folded into the existing ones - because the way this was missed is that one line covered a category,
and a category is where the next leak will hide too.

FALSIFIED BY REINTRODUCING THE LEAK, NOT BY REVERTING. Reverting cannot falsify these checks: on the
previous build `worldState` does not report `pool` or `thunderIn`, so the assertions read a missing key
and their defaults make them PASS. `API.leakOn(true)` makes `freshWorld` skip exactly the two lines
this ruling added. With it, a restart from a soaked road starts at pool 1.000 and grip 0.740, and the
check goes red.

WHAT THE SWEEP DID NOT FIND. Everything else `freshWorld` is responsible for was already there. The
remaining un-reset values are cosmetic and were left alone deliberately: `wipePhase`, `lensDrops` and
`winClock` carry across a reset and none of them touches how the car drives. They are named here so
the next sweep does not have to find them again to decide they do not matter.

AND THIS IS NOT A FIX FOR [[RLG-097]]. The owner's fourth report of a pop on GO is still open. This
sweep was on the queue before that report and answers a different question - three mechanisms have
already been found for the pop, all real, none of them what was being seen.
