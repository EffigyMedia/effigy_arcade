### RLG-126 — A crate is always taken, and what it paid is shown where the resource lives
- **RULED 2026-08-31T18:37:09-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31, on the version that left an unusable crate standing: "leaving a crate on the side of the road for later doesn't make any sense cause you'll never see it again. You should just collect it and get nothing. We need a proper way to express how you earn it so it's clean and not obnoxious."

THE FIRST HALF WAS SIMPLY WRONG AND THE REASON IS WORTH KEEPING. The argument for leaving it was that the player might want it later. That is true of a circuit and false of an endless road, where you pass a thing once - so it was preserving a box for a moment that cannot arrive, in front of a player who had already driven over it. A crate is always taken now, and when it has nothing to give it says nothing.

THE SECOND HALF IS THE DESIGN, AND THE OWNER TOOK BOTH HALVES OF WHAT WAS OFFERED: the gauges show it AND a unified short line shows it.

ONE LINE, ONE SHAPE. Every clause is now a PLUS of a named amount - `+25 HEALTH  +25 NOS  +10 SEC`. `REPAIRED -25%` is gone, and it is worth recording that the owner chose to KEEP it a few hours earlier and then changed their mind on seeing it beside two other clauses. Nothing about it was wrong in isolation: `dmg` counts up, so a repair genuinely removes damage. What was wrong was the line - it read "minus, plus, plus" for three things that are all gains, and it was the only clause of three that counted DOWN. A minus sign on a reward is a small lie the eye has to undo, and undoing it three times a run is what "obnoxious" meant here.

AND THE GAUGES LIGHT WHERE THE AWARD WENT. A line in the middle of the screen is read once and gone; the clock and the bottle are what the player watches for the rest of the run, so a gain lights the thing it went INTO. It teaches where to look rather than what to read. The clock already had exactly this mechanism for the opposite state - `.low` turns it red as it runs out - so `.gain` is the same idea with the sign reversed, and the two are on different CSS properties so a top-up inside the last five seconds reads as both at once, which is what it is.

DAMAGE HAS NO GAUGE ON THIS MACHINE AT ALL, which is why the line survives rather than being replaced. `#dmgFill` is styled in both cabinets and matches nothing; damage is only the screen effect. So a repair has nowhere to light and the line is the only place it can be said. THAT IS A GAP WORTH ITS OWN LOOK - a game where health cannot be read as a number is a deliberate choice or an oversight, and nobody has said which.

THE BOTTLE LOST ITS STRIPE AND GAINED ITS NAME. Owner: "I don't wanna have that stripe in it and I want NOS to be written larger on it." The strap band was a dark bar at 52% of the bottle's length, put there so the shape read as a bottle rather than a pill - and it sat directly over the charge gauge, interrupting the one thing the bottle exists to show. The word does that job better and does not cover anything. The label went from 9px to 15px on a 30px bottle: it was a caption, and a real bottle carries its name across the barrel.

BOTH CABINETS CARRY THEIR OWN COPY OF THESE STYLES, and that is a trap this project keeps meeting. `#nitro`, `#clockWrap` and the whole thumb cluster are duplicated in `interstate.html` and `motorsport.html`, so every change here is two changes and a change made once is a change made to one machine. The two copies were written from the SAME source text this time rather than by hand, which makes them identical today and does nothing about tomorrow. Worth a ruling of its own.

PROVED by `tools/crate-test.py`, and ONE OF THE CHECKS WAS VACUOUS UNTIL THE ENGINE WAS CHANGED TO ANSWER IT. "A crate with nothing to give is collected anyway" was first written as "no crates left on the road" - and a crate the car drives PAST is spliced out 1,500 units later either way, so it read zero whether the box was taken or merely passed. It passed with the defect deliberately reintroduced. The engine counts the crates it actually takes now, incremented after the point where a version that declines to take one would have returned, and the check fails properly: "the collected count went 5 to 5, so it was driven past rather than taken."

The gauge checks read the CLASS the element is carrying rather than inferring it from the state that should have set it. Measured: clock `stat c gain`, bottle `gain`. Removing the two lines that light them fails both.
- **RULED 2026-08-31T19:27:47-04:00** — A FLAKE IN THIS RULING'S OWN HARNESS, found and fixed while running the gates for [[RLG-127]]. `crate-test` failed about one run in five on "a crate with nothing to give is collected anyway", and the cause was a race of the kind that is easy to write and hard to see. The drive loop STOPPED as soon as `lastFx()` said something - and the case where a crate pays NOTHING never produces a flash, so that case relied on the loop timing out. A flash still alive from the PREVIOUS case ended it early instead, before the car had reached its own crate, and the check then read "not collected" quite correctly about a car that had not got there. Driving the full distance every time fixed that and broke the opposite way: a flash lives 1.2 seconds and the drive is 2.4, so reading it at the END read an expired flash and every text check failed. IT SAMPLES AS IT GOES NOW, keeping the first thing said and the gauge lights as they were when it was said. NEITHER THE FLASH NOR THE GAUGE LIGHT IS A STATE THAT PERSISTS, and that is the general lesson: a check on something that is briefly true has to watch for it rather than look afterwards. And one gauge check was failing deterministically for a real reason - the cases above it fill the bottle, and a gauge cannot be seen receiving what it already has, so it is drained first.
