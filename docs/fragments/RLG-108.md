### RLG-108 — Checkpoint gantries are missing from the rear-view
- **RULED 2026-08-31T10:50:58-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: "Checkpoint signs aren't shown in the rearview mirror by the way."

IT IS THE SAME GAP [[RLG-079]] CLOSED FOR EVERYTHING ELSE. The mirror was given the sky, the ground,
the tarmac, the biome, the weather, the scenery, the treeline, the cars, their headlights and their
wipers - each time by asking the SAME function the windscreen asks. The checkpoint gantries were never
carried across. They are road furniture standing in the world, and the mirror is a picture of the
world behind the car.

AND A CHECKPOINT IS THE ONE PIECE OF FURNITURE WITH A REASON TO BE IN THERE. Every other thing in the
glass is scenery or traffic. A gantry is the thing the Interstate is played against: a player who is
not sure whether they have crossed one has a real question, and the mirror is where the answer is.
That makes this more than a completeness item.

WHERE IT GOES. `cpGantries` is the list, it is already filtered to `cp.z > pos - 8000` so boards
BEHIND the car are deliberately kept for a while, and the mirror can already see 34,000 units back.
So the data is there and the retention is right - what is missing is a pass. The mirror's own sprite
loop is where it belongs, drawn with the same `rproj` everything else in the glass uses.

TWO THINGS TO GET RIGHT. A gantry seen from behind is not the same picture as one seen from in front -
whatever the forward painter does with a face, the rear of it is a different drawing, and this project
has a standing rule that a machine must not fade to a simplified render. And it is a TALL object: the
mirror's projection pushes near things off the bottom edge, and a gantry spans the road, so it will
be clipped by the pane's top and bottom in a way a car never is.

MEASURE IT THE WAY THE MIRROR'S OTHER GAPS WERE MEASURED. Not by a screenshot: park a board at a known
distance behind the car with `API.gantries()` already reporting how many exist, and assert the glass
gets busier when one is there than when it is not. That is the shape `mirror-rain-test` uses and it
falsifies cleanly.
- **RULED 2026-08-31T17:51:19-04:00** — BUILT in v0.10.33. The checkpoint boards are in the glass.

THE DATA AND THE RETENTION WERE ALREADY RIGHT, exactly as this ruling said: `cpGantries` keeps a board for 8,000 units after it is passed and the mirror sees 34,000 back. What was missing was a pass, and it goes in the mirror's own sorted sprite list rather than underneath it - a gantry stands in the world at a distance like everything else, and the list is what puts near things over far ones.

THE BACK OF A SIGN IS A DIFFERENT PICTURE, which is the first of the two things this ruling said to get right. It is not a simplified render of the front and it is not the front reversed: the green face, the white border and the word are all on the other side. What you see from behind is the back of the panel, its two stiffening rails and the hangers holding it to the truss. The legs and the lattice are the same steel from either side. THERE IS NO LAMP GLOW - the lights on a gantry point at the traffic coming toward it, which is the other way, so from behind you see the fittings and not the light.

AND THE TWO PICTURES CANNOT DRIFT APART. The proportions moved out of the painter into a `GANTRY` table that both faces read - the uprights, the height of the truss, the depth of the lattice, the board's height and width. A literal copied into a second painter is how "the same object from two sides" quietly becomes two objects, which is the same fault `timeFlash` was built to prevent in [[RLG-106]] a few units ago.

THE CLIPPING NEEDED NOTHING, which is the second thing this ruling flagged. The pane already clips to its own rounded rectangle before anything is drawn into it, so a tall object spanning the road is cut off by the glass exactly as it should be.

MEASURED THE WAY THIS RULING ASKED, and not by a screenshot. `API.parkGantry` puts a board on the REAL `cpGantries` array at a chosen distance behind the car - a checkpoint sits two miles out, so driving to one would take most of a minute and measure the placer on the way. The check lives in `tools/mirror-rain-test.py` because that file already owns the machinery the ruling named: the pane's rectangle taken from the engine, a per-pixel grab kept in the page, and a difference between two grabs. Copying that into a fourth file to ask one more question of the same glass would be three chances for the copies to drift.

IT NEEDED A TWO-SIDED DIFFERENCE. The existing measure is deliberately one-sided because precipitation ADDS light. A gantry is dark steel and a panel against a road and a sky, so it darkens about as much of the glass as it lightens and half of it would have scored as nothing.

MEASURED: 1.12 per cent of the pane differs with a board behind the car against 0.01 per cent with nothing, a hundred to one. Removing the pass by hand reads 3.09 against 3.11 - identical, which is the shape of nothing being drawn.

AND THE CHECK HAD TO BE MADE HONEST FIRST, which is worth recording because it nearly was not. A first version compared one grab with one grab, and with the pass removed it read 27.48 per cent with a board against 27.47 without - and a bare "did the glass change" check passes happily on that. It is not evidence of a board; it is evidence that the measurement is worthless. So the control is asserted FIRST - the glass must be still with nothing behind the car - and everything after it depends on that. The stillness is bought by re-asserting the dry empty road every few frames and taking the MEDIAN of nine samples, which is this same file's own idiom for the weather and is there for the same reason.

THAT CHURN IS A REAL AND SEPARATE FAULT, tracked as [[RLG-124]]: three of this harness's own weather checks are red on the shipped build and were red before today's work, confirmed by running them against the last commit. They were NOT changed here - a harness whose numbers are being questioned is not one to edit in passing - and RLG-124 says what to try first.

NOT VERIFIED FROM HERE: whether the back of the sign READS as the back of a sign on a phone, in a pane 60 pixels tall. A harness can say the pixels changed and cannot say what they look like.
