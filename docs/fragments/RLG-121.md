### RLG-121 — The world is frozen behind the count-in, so all of it resumes in one frame on GO
- **RULED 2026-08-31T15:35:27-04:00** — Owner, from the device against v0.10.27, and this is the FOURTH report of a pop on GO: "the fact that it seems like the car in the world gets reloaded on go is really annoying." Asked for the detail rather than guessing - RLG-041's lesson is that every step forward on this came from the owner's own words - the answer was: THEIR OWN CAR, it JUMPS TO A DIFFERENT PLACE, EVERYTHING SHIFTS AT ONCE, and RETRY does it exactly the same as a fresh run.

THE BUG IS THE CONTRADICTION BETWEEN A COMMENT AND THE TWO LINES UNDER IT. `step()` opened with this, from [[RLG-088]]:

    Everything below this runs, so the road, the traffic and the sky are all
    alive behind the numbers. What does not run is the car.

and then RETURNED. So none of it ran. The entire world was frozen for three seconds and every part of it resumed in the same frame. The comment was the correct design all along and the code never implemented it.

MEASURED, AND THE WORST OF IT IS ONE VARIABLE. `viewShift` is the forward view's horizontal offset - it exists so the road is centred in the space left over by the thumb controls rather than sitting under them - and it is assigned in exactly one place, inside `step()`, past the return. So on a touch device it was 0 for the whole count and became minus 8.5 per cent of the screen width on the first driving frame. Measured on a 480-pixel viewport: 0 during the count, -40.8 after. THE ENTIRE PICTURE MOVED SIDEWAYS BY FORTY PIXELS IN ONE FRAME, and the player's own car with it. That is the owner's report exactly, in both halves - the car jumps AND everything shifts, because they are the same event.

IT IS NOT THE ONLY ONE. The bend cache is re-integrated on a timer inside `step()`, and behind the return it was rebuilt zero times during the count and then rebuilt in front of the player. `camX`, `pushK` and every spawn timer were in the same position. RLG-090 had already found three of these one at a time - the biome, the weather and the cloud - and fixed each where it stood. This is the general form of that fault, and fixing it here retires the class rather than the instance.

THE FIX IS THE COMMENT. The return is gone and `held` replaces it: everything below runs, and the CAR is held by name at each place that would move it, score it, hurt it, or spend its clock. Every gate says `held` and can be found by searching for the word.

HELD: the steering input, the road speed, the run clock, the distance, the heat, the corner push, the rivals, the traffic summoned when you dawdle, the police boxing in a stationary car, the crates, the roadblocks, the automatic gearbox, and every collision. THAT LAST ONE IS ONE LINE RATHER THAN TEN: `iframe` is already the engine's own word for "this car cannot be hit right now" and every collision in the function already asks it, so the count re-arms it every frame.

NOT HELD: the sky, the weather, the biome, the lens, the scenery, the spawn timers, the bend cache, the view shift, the camera, and the fx.

THE AUTOMATIC GEARBOX WAS THE ONE THAT BIT. `autoGear` clamps the gear to 1 on its first line, and behind the old return it never ran during the count. The moment the count stopped returning it undid `holdNeutral` every frame, so the box left neutral immediately and [[RLG-110]]'s launch fired on the count's FIRST frame instead of on GO. `tools/launch-test.py` caught it on the first run after the change, which is the harness earning its place - nothing about the picture would have said so.

THE SPEED IS PINNED AFTER THE SPEED BLOCK, NOT BY SKIPPING IT, and that is deliberate. The block declares `offRoad`, `overRun`, `tow`, `decel` and `brakeLamp`, and three hundred lines below read them; jumping over it would leave half of them undefined. And the throttle still has to be READ while the count is up, because it is what sets the revs for the launch.

PROVED, AND THE PROOF IS NOT VACUOUS. `tools/start-test.py` gained two checks: the forward view is in the same place during the count as after it, and the road is re-integrated while the count is up. It also asserts the offset is NOT zero, so the check cannot pass on a desktop viewport where both readings would be 0 - which is the vacuous version of it. Measured with the fix: -40.8 during and -40.8 after, 11 rebuilds during the count. The `return` was then put back by hand and both failed: 0 against -40.8, and 0 rebuilds. smoke, drive, launch, circuit and start all pass.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE. The owner has since asked for a ROLLING start on Interstate - the car pinned at 50mph with the field ahead doing the same - as the main difference between Interstate and Motorsport. That is a design change and it is its own unit. This one is the bug underneath it, and it had to land first: a car pinned at 50mph in front of a frozen world would have been worse than a car pinned at zero.
