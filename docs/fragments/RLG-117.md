### RLG-117 — Race opponents have a chance of wearing stripes
- **RULED 2026-08-31T12:35:58-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: "Actual racers should have a chance to use stripes." And immediately, correcting
what that could have meant: "Not the racer's personality but race opponents."

THE CORRECTION IS THE RULING. [[RLG-054]] had just landed three driving personalities, one of them
called Racer, and a Racer can be an ordinary car in traffic. Stripes on THOSE would have been the
exact failure that fragment spent two paragraphs warning against - a traffic car reading as a rival.
The owner meant the cars on the grid, and said so before it could be built wrong.

IT IS THE CLASS SIGNAL READ FROM THE OTHER END. RLG-044 rules colour says class; RLG-054 has just put
supercars into traffic in muted paint so they do not read as rivals. A striped car is unambiguously an
opponent - which is what makes the muted one unambiguously not. The two changes are one signal, which
is why they were checked in one file.

A CHANCE, NOT A RULE, AND BOTH BOUNDS MATTER. At nothing the signal does not exist; at everything a
grid is a team rather than a field. `RIVAL_STRIPES` is 0.35 and is a tunable with a committed default.
Measured over 30 grids and 330 cars: 121 striped, 37 per cent, spread across all three bodies on the
grid.

ROLLED WITH THE PAINT AND THE BODY, which is a decision about WHEN rather than a detail. It is part of
what this car is for the whole race. Rolled per frame it would flicker; rolled once for the whole
grid it would be a livery.

TWO CACHES, TWO POLICIES, AND EACH KEPT ITS OWN. The rear sprites are built eagerly in a loop at boot,
so the striped variants are built there too, for the six bodies `STRIPE_BODIES` names. The front
sprites are built lazily on demand, so the flag joins their cache key instead. Imposing one policy on
both would have meant either doubling a boot loop for sprites that may never be drawn, or making the
eager cache lazy for no reason.

AND A CAR STRIPED FROM BEHIND AND PLAIN FROM THE FRONT IS TWO CARS. The mirror draws a rival's face
through `rivalFront`, which is why that cache had to take the flag at all. It is easy to miss because
the fault only shows in the mirror.

A FORMULA CAR NEVER WEARS THEM, and there are two guards rather than one. It is not in
`STRIPE_BODIES`, and the painter refuses stripes on a formula body regardless. The check asserts the
EFFECT rather than either guard, so removing one of them would still be caught.

THE CHECK RAN IN THE WRONG MODE FIRST, and it is worth recording because the failure looked like the
feature. Racers exist only in race mode; the first version ran in DRIVE, formed no grid at all, and
reported "0 of 0" as a failure of the stripes rather than of the harness. It sets the mode now.

NOT SEEN ON A DEVICE.
