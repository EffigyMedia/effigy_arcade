### RLG-114 — The pickup moves to production, and utility gains an ambulance and a bus
- **RULED 2026-08-31T11:15:41-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31, to be taken up in the next queue: "We move pickup to production, and in utility we
add an ambulance and a bus. The ambulance is essentially going to be the van with a red only light
bar. Its light bar and siren would function the same as with police in terms of getting cars to move
out of the way."

THE PICKUP MOVE IS ONE LINE AND IT IS ALSO A BALANCE CHANGE. `LOCK` maps each body to the class that
unlocks it: production at 50 miles, utility at 25. Moving PICKUP from utility to production makes it
unlock LATER, not sooner, and it leaves utility with two entries where it had three - so both classes
change size at once. The line is trivial; whether a player who has just earned utility should now find
two vehicles in it rather than three is the part worth a moment.

AND THE PICKUP IS ALREADY DESCRIBED AS A PRODUCTION VEHICLE IN THE WHEEL WORK. The note there splits
the fleet into a sporty group and the ones "the owner named production and utility", and the pickup
sits with a four-speed gearbox and a production-shaped wheel already. So the classification is
catching up with how it is already drawn rather than changing what it is.

THE AMBULANCE IS THE CHEAPEST NEW VEHICLE THIS PROJECT HAS EVER HAD, and the reason is worth stating.
It is the VAN body, which exists front and rear with lamps declared, plus a light bar - and the light
bar is not new either. `barOn` already exists as the player's own bar for the cruiser, the patrol
car's flashing bar is already drawn on top of its sprite in both the forward view and the mirror, and
the siren is already a two-tone built from `snd.sirenPhase`. What is new is one colour choice: the
police bar alternates blue and red, and this one is red only.

THE BEHAVIOUR IS ALREADY BUILT TOO, AND THAT IS THE POINT OF THE REQUEST. RLG-035 made the horn and
the siren actually move traffic, and traffic carries a `heed` value that is spent when a vehicle is
pushed aside and recovers slowly. So "functions the same as with police in terms of getting cars to
move out of the way" is not new behaviour - it is the ambulance being allowed to ask the question the
cruiser already asks. The work is to make that a property of HAVING A BAR rather than of BEING A
POLICE CAR, which is the same shape as three standing owner rulings: the car is capable, the driver
decides, and never write a branch that names a vehicle class to get a behaviour.

SO THE TEST OF THIS BUILD IS WHETHER `barOn` GAINS A COLOUR AND LOSES A SPECIAL CASE. If the ambulance
needs its own branch anywhere in the traffic code, it has been built wrong.

THE BUS IS THE ONE WITH REAL WORK IN IT. There is no bus body: it is a new silhouette front and rear,
with its own lamps, its own mass and its own gearbox, and it is longer than anything except the lorry.
Length is the interesting part - the collider note in [[RLG-058]] says a billboard has no depth and
length has no sprite to be true to, so a bus tests that gap harder than the lorry does because a
player will expect to still be alongside it after passing its nose.

AND ONE PHYSICS FOR EVERY CAR STILL HOLDS. An ambulance is a van with a light bar, so it drives like a
van - it does not get van handling plus an exemption because it is an emergency vehicle. A bus is
heavy and tall because of its numbers. Neither gets a branch.

WHAT IS NOT SAID AND SHOULD BE ASKED. Whether these are DRIVEABLE by the player or only appear as
traffic. The request says "we add an ambulance and a bus" under a class name, and classes are what the
GARAGE unlocks - so it reads as driveable. If they are driveable, both need a full set: front sprite,
rear sprite, lamp declarations, a gearbox, a mass, a paint list and a garage card. If they are traffic
only, most of that is unnecessary. That is the single question that decides how large this is.
- **RULED 2026-08-31T11:26:57-04:00** — OWNER CONFIRMED, 2026-08-31: "Both the ambulance and the bus are driveable as part of the utility
class. The ambulance comes in white only. Maybe we add a red cross centered on the back doors."

THAT ANSWERS THE ONE QUESTION THAT DECIDED THE SIZE OF THIS. Driveable means the full set for both:
a front sprite, a rear sprite, lamp declarations on each, a gearbox, a mass, a paint list and a garage
card. The ambulance gets most of that from the van; the bus gets none of it.

WHITE ONLY IS A SMALLER STATEMENT THAN IT LOOKS, AND IT IS A GOOD ONE. Every driveable body carries a
paint list and the garage offers the choice. An ambulance with one colour is the first vehicle whose
paint is not a choice - which is correct, because a white ambulance is what an ambulance IS, and it
also means the red cross can be drawn at a fixed contrast instead of having to work over six liveries.

THE RED CROSS BELONGS TO THE REAR SPRITE, AND THE REAR SPRITE IS THE ONE THE PLAYER NEVER SEES OF
THEIR OWN CAR. Centred on the back doors means it shows in two places: in another car's mirror, and in
the player's own mirror when an ambulance is behind them. Both are already drawn - every rear sprite
in the game declares its lamps and the mirror paints them - so this is art on an existing surface
rather than a new pass.

AND IT IS THE FIRST MARKING OF ITS KIND. Nothing else in the fleet carries a symbol; liveries are
colour and shape. So the question worth settling while drawing it is whether a marking is a general
capability - something a sprite can declare, the way it declares lamps - or one cross painted into one
sprite. A bus with a route number and a lorry with a livery are both plausible later, which argues for
the first.
- **RULED 2026-09-05T12:29:37-04:00** — THE AMBULANCE IS BUILT at 0.12.3. It is the first half of queue item 7; the bus is the other and
is not started.

THE SPLIT THAT HAD TO COME FIRST. `force` meant two things at once - "this is a police car" AND
"this has a bar, a siren, and the authority to move traffic". An ambulance is the second without
the first, so the field could not answer for it. `force` now means POLICE ONLY, and a new `bar`
field means there is a light bar on the roof, CARRYING THE SCHEME it is painted in. `inCruiser()`
was really asking "have you got a bar", so it became `hasBar()`; `inForce()` was added beside it to
ask the other question, and the old name is kept as a one-line alias because the shell and a
harness both call it. Demonstrated in one table:

    AMBULANCE   bar=medical   police=False  hasBar=True   class=utility   raceLegal=False
    VAN         bar=None      police=False  hasBar=False  class=utility   raceLegal=False
    CRUISER     bar=police    police=True   hasBar=True   class=cruiser   raceLegal=True

THE SIREN AND THE SCATTER CAME FREE, which is what the thread predicted. `barOn` already drives
`scatter(0.90)` and the siren's wail already rides on `barOn`, so both followed the gate without
being touched. What was NOT free was the drawing.

FOUR PAINTERS, NOT ONE, AND THAT IS THE FINDING. The thread called this cheap on the strength of
`force` already being generalised onto the BODY record. That was true of the BEHAVIOUR and false of
the SPRITE. A light bar was drawn in four places - `paintCar` front and rear for the car-shaped
bodies, and `paintRig`/`paintRigFront` for the rigs - and in the rig painters it was inside a
branch gated on `kind === 'cop'`. So the bar was something only a police car could have, twice
over.

AND THE VAN BRANCH RETURNS EARLY. Moving the bar out of the cop branch to the foot of the rig
painter was not enough: `if(kind === 'van')` draws its slab and RETURNS, so the shared block at the
end was never reached. The ambulance's sprite came back carrying a plain van's three lamps and no
bar. It was not a throw and not a scoping fault - the code simply never got there, and nothing
reported anything. The bar is drawn inside the van branch now, on that branch's own `top` roof line
rather than the car-shaped `roofY`, which does not exist there.

IT NEEDED ITS OWN RIG, for a reason worth keeping. `SP` is keyed by RIG, and `SP.van` is the single
sprite every traffic van shares - so bolting a bar to it would have put one on the traffic. So
`rig:'ambulance'` exists purely to own a sprite, and both rig painters remap it to `van` on entry
with `bar:'medical'` implied. One line each, and one place says what an ambulance is made of.

`barY` WAS MEASURED, NOT GUESSED, because the note beside it demands exactly that: the two existing
values were sampled from built sprites and it says "any future force car declares its own". The
guess was 0.070 and the measurement is 0.074. tools/bar-row.py is new and does the measuring.

THAT HARNESS CARRIES ITS OWN CONTROL AND THE CONTROL EARNED ITS KEEP. It measures CRUISER and
SUPERCRUISER alongside whatever it is asked about, and refuses to report numbers unless those come
back at the recorded 0.122 and 0.304. Its FIRST draft failed both, which is how two faults were
caught rather than shipped: the sprite table is built lazily and is empty on the title screen, and
sniffing for "strong blue and strong red" pixels cannot find a bar that is neither - which an
ambulance's is not. It renders the LAMP DECLARATION instead, so it needs no colour knowledge and
works for a scheme nobody has invented yet. Controls now read 0.125 and 0.304 against the recorded
0.122 and 0.304.

THE SHEET'S OWN CLASS MAP WAS UPDATED IN THE SAME CHANGE. `API.fleet` keeps a second copy of the
class table and RLG-114's own note warns that a class moving in one and not the other makes the
sheet print something the garage disagrees with. AMBULANCE is utility in both.

WHAT FOLLOWS FROM RLG-115 WITHOUT A NEW DECISION: an ambulance is a UTILITY vehicle, so it can only
go on TEST DRIVE in Interstate and Motorsport does not list it at all. That is the owner's own rule
applied, not a choice made here, and it is worth their eye because an emergency vehicle absent from
a circuit garage may read as a bug rather than a rule.

VERIFIED. bar-row controls hold; lamp-test 61 vehicles and 188 lamps, up from 59 and 178 by exactly
the ambulance's two faces and ten lamps; smoke-test 6 of 6; class-test 12 of 12; drive-test 39 of
39. The two control measurements are unchanged to three decimals after the split, which is the
evidence the police cars were not disturbed by it.

NOT PROVEN, AND IT NEEDS THE DEVICE. Whether a white van with a red bar reads as an ambulance at
speed, whether red-on-both-ends still reads as a FLASH rather than a steady lamp, and whether the
scatter feels like traffic getting out of the way rather than traffic behaving oddly. A harness can
say the lamps alternate; it cannot say it looks right.
