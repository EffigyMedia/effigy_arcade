### RLG-115 — A production or utility vehicle can only go on TEST DRIVE
- **RULED 2026-08-31T11:44:39-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31, recorded for a later queue and not started: "if you have a production or utility
vehicle selected, the only mode available is test drive."

IT IS A RULE ABOUT WHAT A CAR IS FOR, AND IT MAKES THE CLASSES MEAN SOMETHING. The garage already
divides the fleet - super, formula, cruiser, production, utility - and today those names decide only
when a car unlocks. This gives them a second job: the ordinary cars are for driving around in, and the
events are for the cars built to enter them. A saloon lining up on a starting grid against three
supercars is a race nobody wrote.

AND IT MAKES THE AMBULANCE AND THE BUS COHERENT, which is the thing to notice given [[RLG-114]] was
agreed an hour before this. A driveable bus that could enter a tournament would be a joke asset; a
driveable bus that can only go out on the open road is a thing worth having. This ruling is what makes
that whole class of vehicle sensible rather than novelty, so the two belong in the same queue.

WHERE IT LIVES AND WHAT IT MUST NOT BECOME. The mode buttons are on the title, the car is chosen in
the garage, and the two are separate screens - so the gate has to survive the player choosing a car
AFTER choosing a mode, as well as before. The wrong build is a check inside each mode's start; the
right one is the menu not offering what the car cannot do, so the rule is visible rather than
enforced by refusal. This project's own standard is that a gesture is taught in play the first time it
matters, and a greyed-out button with a reason on it teaches better than a button that does nothing.

THE OPEN QUESTION IS WHAT THE PLAYER SEES, and it should be asked rather than guessed. Three shapes,
and they are not equivalent: the other modes disappear; they grey out with a line saying why; or the
garage refuses the car while a race mode is selected. The second is likeliest to be right because it
explains itself, but it is the owner's call.

AND ONE THING TO CHECK BEFORE BUILDING: what happens to a SAVED selection. If a player leaves the
game with a bus selected and a tournament in progress, the next launch has to resolve that
disagreement somehow. The cleanest answer is that the mode is the thing that persists and the car
follows it, but that is a decision rather than an obvious consequence.
- **RULED 2026-09-05T09:42:01-04:00** — BUILT at 0.12.1. The owner answered the three questions this was blocked on, 2026-09-05, and
the answers were not all the ones the fragment leaned toward.

WHAT THE PLAYER SEES: the race modes GREY OUT WITH THE REASON GIVEN. The MODE control keeps its
place, dims, carries `disabled`, and a line under it reads `PRODUCTION - TEST DRIVE ONLY` with the
car's own class named. That was this fragment's own lean and the owner confirmed it.

MOTORSPORT: production and utility vehicles are NOT OFFERED AT ALL in its garage - the strongest of
the four shapes put to the owner. The reasoning holds up: Interstate lists them and greys the modes
out because it HAS a mode they are legal in, TEST DRIVE. A circuit does not, so a greyed-out control
there would explain a rule about a screen the player can never reach. WHAT IT COSTS IS WORTH
RECORDING - this is the first thing that makes the two games' garages hold different cars, and
REDLINE is one engine with one fleet. It is therefore a `CFG.raceOnlyGarage` flag set by the
cabinet, and the engine never asks which game it is running.

THE SAVED SELECTION: never let the disagreement exist. The owner rejected both of the
resolve-it-at-launch options. `enforceCarRules()` runs as the garage opens and again whenever a car
is chosen, so a car and a mode that disagree are never written to the save at all.

AND THE TOURNAMENT IS SWITCHED OFF, NOT ERASED. `tourOn` goes false so that no race mode is live;
`tourRound`, `tourPts` and the standings are untouched. Taking a van out for a drive must not cost
somebody their ladder. The harness asserts the round survives.

THIS FRAGMENT'S OWN PREMISE WAS STALE AND THE RECORD SHOULD SAY SO. It states that "the mode buttons
are on the title, the car is chosen in the garage, and the two are separate screens", and builds its
worry about the gate surviving a car chosen AFTER a mode on top of that. It is no longer true: MODE
and HOT PURSUIT were moved into the garage, and the title now holds only PLAY, OPTIONS and QUIT. The
car and the mode are adjacent controls on one screen, which is why greying out reads so clearly - the
player sees the cause and the effect together, without changing screens.

HOW IT IS BUILT, and it is not the build this fragment warned against. RLG-115 names the wrong build
explicitly: a check inside each mode's start. Nothing here refuses anything at the moment of driving.
The class table that used to be a local `const LOCK` inside `cycleBody` is now `BODY_CLASS` at module
scope with two readers - the unlock gate that was always there, and `raceLegal()`. The list of cars
the garage will show became `garageBodies()` for the same reason: the arrows used to be the only
thing that could ask.

THERE ARE TWO LOCKS AND THAT IS DELIBERATE. The button carries `disabled`, and the mode action
returns early. Both were removed one at a time and then together while the harness watched.

tools/class-test.py IS NEW AND IT WAS WATCHED FAILING. It drives the real controls rather than
calling `raceLegal` and agreeing with itself. THE FIRST DRAFT OF ONE CHECK WAS VACUOUS and is worth
recording: "pressing it anyway does nothing" read the BUTTON'S LABEL, and the label is hardcoded to
TEST DRIVE on the banned branch, so it cannot move whatever happens underneath. That check passed
with BOTH locks removed. It reads `API.mode()` now, and with both locks gone it reports
`mode race -> endless` and fails. The falsification record: circuit flag off gives "COUPE was offered
and should not have been"; the button lock off gives "disabled/greyed: False"; both locks off gives
the tournament left running as well.

WHAT IS NOT PROVEN HERE. Whether a dimmed control at 0.42 opacity reads as switched-off rather than
broken on a phone in daylight is the owner's eye, not a measurement. So is whether the note under it
is legible at 9px.
