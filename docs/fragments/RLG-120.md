### RLG-120 — The engine is silent through the count-in, and the player is being asked to set the revs by ear
- **RULED 2026-08-31T15:04:16-04:00** — Owner, from the device against v0.10.27: "There's no engine sound during the countdown."

TRUE, AND IT WAS TRUE BEFORE [[RLG-110]] TOO - what RLG-110 did was make it matter. Everything that makes a noise is at the BOTTOM of `step()`, and the count-in returns from the top of it, so the car has always been silent for the three seconds it is held. Nobody noticed while the count was dead time. It is not dead time now: the throttle is how the player sets the revs for the launch, and a rev you cannot hear is half a control. The ear is quicker than the eye at holding a note and the tachometer is 26 pixels across.

THE FIX IS TWO PARTS AND THE SECOND ONE IS THE INTERESTING HALF.

FIRST, the count-in sounds the engine before it returns, with a `still` flag on `snd.drive`. The flag mutes the WIND, and only the wind: wind noise is scaled off the same ratio as the engine note, so a car held on the line would otherwise howl at eight thousand rpm while standing still. It is the only layer that is wrong about a stationary car - the screech is already gated on the car actually shedding speed, and the thruster on the bottle being open.

SECOND, AND THIS IS A FAULT THE REPORT UNCOVERED RATHER THAN THE ONE REPORTED: the free-revving neutral integration lived inside `engineRpm()`, which is a REPORTER. It is called by the dial painter and again by the audio frame, so while driving it advanced the needle TWICE a frame and while the count was up only once - and the rates were written per CALL rather than per second. The needle climbed at a speed that depended on how many things had asked it what it was reading, and on a phone running at 30fps it would have climbed at half the rate it was tuned at.

WORSE, `drawDials` returns early when a cabinet has no gauges element. The engine's own state hung off whether something drew a picture of it, and the moment the SOUND started reading the same state that would have made a silent engine a rendering question. So the integration moved to `stepEngine(dt)`, called once per step of the world, before the count-in's return - and the rates became rates per second. LAUNCH.rise is 3.08 and LAUNCH.fall is 0.30, which are the continuous equivalents of the 0.050 and 0.005 per frame they replace at 60fps.

PROVED, AND THE PROOF IS NOT VACUOUS. `tools/launch-test.py` gained four checks, and they ask the question RLG-065 says to ask. A LEVEL READ OFF A NODE IS NOT EVIDENCE THE NODE IS HEARD - a GainNode on a closed context reports a healthy value quite happily, which cost three attempts once already - so the harness reads the engine oscillator's live frequency AND which context it belongs to. Measured across the count with the throttle held: 71.3 Hz to 436.8 Hz, on the running context, with the highest pitch belonging to the highest needle. The call was then removed by hand and the same checks read 70.0 Hz flat with a gain of 0.0000 - which is silence, and is exactly what the owner heard.

NOT VERIFIED FROM HERE: whether the engine is loud enough in the mix to hold a note by, and whether the pitch spread across the band is wide enough to aim with. Both are the owner's on the device, and both are the same question as the launch window's own feel.
