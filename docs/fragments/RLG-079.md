### RLG-079 — The rear mirror looks down from higher up, and the height is a tunable
- **RULED 2026-08-29T23:07:45-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-29, from the device: "can you please raise the perspective of the rear mirror. It's
still shows too low to the ground."

"STILL" IS THE IMPORTANT WORD. This is the third time the mirror's eye has been raised. It ran at the
forward view's own `CAM_H`, which put the eye at the driver's height in a car whose camera is already
low, and read as a lens lying on the tarmac. It went to 1.55x, then to 2.15x when a per-car height
was replaced by one height for every vehicle. It is 3.00x now.

SO IT IS A NAMED TUNABLE, not a number inside an expression. The value is the one thing about this
view that keeps needing to move, and a session three months from now should find it in one search
rather than reading the projection. `MIRROR_EYE` at the top with the other camera constants,
`API.mirrorEye()` to set it live, and `API.mirrorAt(dz)` to say where a car a given distance behind
lands as a fraction of the glass - so the next adjustment can be measured instead of guessed.

WHAT THE CHANGE DOES, in fractions of the glass below the mirror's own horizon:

  behind     1.55x    2.15x    3.00x
   1,500     0.455    0.631    0.881
   3,000     0.228    0.316    0.441
   6,000     0.114    0.158    0.221
  12,000     0.057    0.079    0.110
  24,000     0.028    0.039    0.055

THE EYE HEIGHT AND THE VERTICAL ZOOM ARE THE SAME KNOB, and that is the part worth recording because
it is not visible from the call site. `y` is `vpy + scale * CAM_H_M * H_M/2` - a product - so raising
the eye does not pitch the camera down while keeping the same depth. It scales the whole vertical
mapping. There is no way to look further down in this projection without also pushing near things off
the bottom edge.

SO THERE IS A COST AND IT IS NOT SMALL. Where the pane's bottom edge falls, in units behind the car:
683 at 1.55x, 947 at 2.15x, 1,322 at 3.00x. A follower closer than about 1,300 units is now BELOW the
glass rather than in it. That limit already existed at 950 and nobody had written it down.

THE FIX FOR THAT IS KNOWN AND NOT BUILT. A real mirror shows a tailgater as the top of a car filling
the frame, not as nothing. Clamping a car whose projected y falls past the bottom edge to the bottom
edge would give that, and it is a small change confined to the mirror's sprite pass. It is left
undone deliberately: the owner asked for the perspective to be raised, and building a second thing in
the same unit would make the device report ambiguous if the mirror still reads wrong.

NOT VERIFIED HERE. A harness cannot say whether a mirror looks right. 3.00x is a judgement from the
geometry above, and the owner has already twice found a value that measured reasonably to be too low
in the hand.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T23:26:14-04:00** — THE EYE HEIGHT WAS NEVER THE FAULT, AND THREE SESSIONS RAISED IT ANYWAY. Owner, 2026-08-29, on the
fourth report: "if it was IRL, I wouldn't be able to look drivers behind me in their eyes... it looks
at the ground and cars bumpers/grill."

That sentence carried what the three before it did not. "Too low to the ground" was read every time
as "the eye is too low, raise it", and the eye went 1.55x, 2.15x, 3.00x. Every one of those made it
WORSE, because raising the eye pushes the road plane further down the glass and fills more of the
pane with tarmac. The report and the fix were pointing in opposite directions and nobody noticed for
three rounds.

A PICTURE SETTLED IT IN ONE LOOK. `tools/mirror-shot.py` renders the pane on its own, enlarged, at
any eye height, horizon, weather and hour. At 3.00x the glass is almost entirely tarmac plunging away
with the car behind a speck at the top - which is exactly "it looks at the ground", said in an image
rather than in words.

THE FAULT WAS `vpy`, THE MIRROR'S OWN HORIZON, at 0.16 of the pane. That put the horizon near the TOP
and left 84 per cent of the glass below it, all of which is road surface and ground. A real mirror
puts its horizon near the middle. No eye height can fix that, because the eye only decides how fast
the road falls away BELOW that line.

It is 0.45 now and the eye came back DOWN to 2.20x, chosen by looking at a grid rather than by
computing one. At those numbers a car behind sits ON the horizon at a size where its body reads, with
a band of sky above it - which is the view the owner asked for.

THE LESSON IS THE INSTRUMENT, NOT THE NUMBER. This project's own guidance says a static check cannot
tell you the game works, and to check the artifact rather than the source. Three rounds of careful
reasoning from the projection got the DIRECTION wrong, and the fourth round looked and was done in
minutes. The geometry table computed in the previous unit was correct in every value and answered a
question that did not matter.

AND THE PREVIOUS UNIT'S REASONING IS WITHDRAWN. UNT-030 recorded that the eye height and the vertical
zoom are the same knob, and used that to justify 3.00x and to record a near-depth cost. The
observation about the knob is true. The conclusion drawn from it was wrong, because it never asked
where the horizon was. The near-depth cost recorded there is also smaller at 2.20x than it was at
3.00x: the pane's bottom edge is the road about 970 units back rather than 1,320.
