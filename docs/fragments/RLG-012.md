### RLG-012 — Hardpoint's title is a chase: two ships, thrusters, a starfield and a turning planet
- **RULED 2026-08-28T12:33:06-04:00** — - **What was asked for, and what is there.** An animated cinematic action pose: two ships in a
  dogfight, one shooting at the other, animated thrusters, an animated starfield, and a moving planet
  or star behind them. All of it is drawn every frame on a canvas behind the menu. Nothing is
  downloaded, which is the product's standing rule.

- **The layers, back to front.** A deep-space gradient; three parallax star layers drifting at 2, 5
  and 11 pixels a second and twinkling on their own clocks; a sun just off the top-right corner
  throwing a warm falloff across the frame; a planet turning under it with drifting cloud bands, a
  terminator on the side away from the sun and a lit limb; then the chase.

- **IT IS A LOOP, AND DELIBERATELY NOT A SIMULATION.** Both ships fly one fixed curve, phase-locked
  so the hunter sits behind and below the runner and the two bank in opposition. A physics chase
  would drift, settle, and eventually put both ships in the same place or off the frame. **What reads
  as an action pose is the MOMENT, and the moment has to be there whenever the player happens to
  look.** Everything with a random look to it is a hash of its own index, so the scene is identical
  on every visit rather than subtly different each time.

- **Two silhouettes, not one mirrored.** The runner is a slim interceptor in cold steel with swept
  wings and a lit canopy; the hunter is heavier and wider in warm brown, with gun pods slung under
  the shoulders that flash when it fires. **A chase where both ships are the same shape reads as one
  ship and its reflection.**

- **The thrust breathes on two sine waves of different periods rather than jittering per frame.** A
  plume driven by a per-frame random reads as a rendering fault, not as combustion.

- **Three things were wrong on the first render and each was fixed by looking, not by reasoning.**
  1. The chase flew across the middle of the frame, which is where the wordmark is. The ships sat
     behind HARDPOINT and read as debris. Moved to the clear band above the menu.
  2. At `scale * 0.052` they were specks. The ships are the subject of the frame, not set dressing
     in it, so they are nearly twice that now.
  3. **The gap between them was shorter than a ship.** Once scaled up the hunter sat on top of the
     runner and the pair read as one object with a confusing outline - and it left the bolts no
     distance to cross, which is the thing the frame is about. The lead went from 0.055 to 0.145.

- **The veil had to become a window.** `#veil` was opaque, which is right over a paused game and
  wrong over title art - the scene would have been painted and then covered up. It gets an `art`
  class only while the title is up, and the menu keeps its own radial falloff so it stays readable
  whatever the dogfight is doing behind it.

- **It stops when the title goes away.** `artStop` is called from CONTROLS, OPTIONS, the station and
  the launch. **A canvas loop still running behind a game is frame budget spent on something nobody
  can see**, and this is a mobile-only product.

- **Proof.** `tools/scene-test.py` is kept: it asserts the canvas has real paint on it (1,092,542 lit
  pixels of a 780x1612 buffer), that the scene is running while the title is up, that it **stops**
  when CONTROLS takes over, and that the page throws nothing. Frames were rendered across the loop
  and looked at. `pack.sh` all gates, 39 files; `smoke-test` 6/6.
