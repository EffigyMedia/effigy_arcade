### RLG-101 — The road itself is carried to the horizon, the way the sea now is
- **RULED 2026-08-31T09:05:09-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31, immediately after the sea was carried to the horizon: "another thing that would be
really cool is if we did the same thing for the highway, so that the highway is interpreted and drawn
to the horizon."

THE SAME MOVE, ON THE THING THE WHOLE VIEW IS BUILT AROUND. The road is drawn for DRAW segments and
stops short of the skyline; the band above it is filled by `groundBase`, which paints the far verge's
colour. So the tarmac ends and the land continues, and the road does not reach the vanishing point it
is drawn toward. The sea had exactly this shape of gap and the answer there was to walk the same
construction on - `proj` and `roadsideAt`, in coarser steps, until the projection converges - rather
than to invent a second one. That is what this asks for.

AND IT IS HARDER THAN THE SEA WAS, FOR ONE REASON THAT SHOULD BE FACED FIRST. The sea is a FILL: one
colour, one edge, and a polygon closed against the screen edge. The road is a ribbon with a centre
line, lane markings, rumble strips and a verge either side, and every one of those is drawn per slice
with its own width. Carrying the tarmac on is a polygon; carrying the MARKINGS on is a strobing
pattern that has to keep its phase, and a marking that arrives out of phase reads worse than no
marking at all. Decide which of those is in scope before starting.

THE STRIPES ARE THE REAL QUESTION AND THEY MAY ANSWER THEMSELVES. Past a certain distance a lane
marking is under a pixel wide and the honest render is none - which is what the draw distance is
implicitly saying today. So the likely shape of this is: the TARMAC and the verge are carried to the
horizon as fills, and the markings are not. That is one body of road in the same sense the sea is one
body of water, and it is a much smaller change than carrying the whole pass.

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN AND SHOULD NOT BE RE-DERIVED. `groundBase` already paints the band and already
takes the far slice's biome, so the band is not empty and this is a change to WHAT fills it rather
than a new layer. `farSea` shows the pattern to copy, including the guard that emits the points the
projection gives and skips the ones that do not rise toward the horizon - it needs no sanity test and
its failure mode is a shorter walk. And the corner cap matters here in a way it did not for the sea:
past about 90 degrees the road leaves the frame, so a walk that continues a hard bend has to stop
rather than draw tarmac across the sky.

MEASURE IT AS THE SEA WAS MEASURED. Not as a picture: the tone step across the join, sampled either
side, swept inside single frames because this world cannot be compared across runs. The sea's seam
turned out to be a COLOUR rather than an angle, and the road has the same asymmetry waiting for it -
the band is painted before `drawHaze` and the drawn road after, so the tarmac will step at the join
for exactly the reason the water did.
