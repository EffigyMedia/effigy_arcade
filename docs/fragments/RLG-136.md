### RLG-136 — It precipitates too often - one lever over the whole board
- **RULED 2026-08-31T22:38:21-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-08-31, after twenty to thirty minutes of play: "it seemed to precipitate a lot. Maybe we
should reduce the overall chances of precipitation."

THE REPORT IS ARITHMETICALLY RIGHT AND THE NUMBER IS WORSE THAN IT SOUNDS. The table averages 0.366
across the seven places and the weather rolls every 35 to 80 seconds, so a twenty-five minute session
sees about twenty-six rolls and NINE OR TEN weather events. A dry road was the exception rather than
the rule, and the owner noticed it as soon as they played for long enough to see the distribution
rather than a sample.

ONE MULTIPLIER OVER THE WHOLE BOARD, at 0.55. The places keep their relationship to each other - a
swamp is still sixteen times wetter than a desert on the table, and measured through the engine's own
roll it still has weather on 37% of rolls against a desert's 2%. A per-place edit would have been
eleven decisions where the owner made one, and it is the shape [[RLG-109]] already chose for
`SNOW_FLOOR_K` and `CLIMATE_SWING`.

WHERE IT IS APPLIED IS THE PART THAT COULD HAVE GONE WRONG. Scaling `precip` inside `climateAt` would
have been the obvious place and would have dragged everything derived down with it:

    the taper thresholds read the derived rain and snow chances, so a tundra's snow would fall
    from 0.135 to 0.074 - under `WEATHER_KEEP` at 0.12 - and snow carried into a tundra would
    have started tapering, silently reversing an owner ruling

    the cloud base is `precip * 1.6`, so the skies would have cleared with the rain

So the scale is applied at the ROLL, in `rollWeather`, and only the FREQUENCY moves. An overcast day
with no rain in it becomes commoner as a result, which the sky model already calls the commonest sky
there is.

MEASURED, through the engine's own roll rather than the formula: a forest has weather on 31% of rolls
at the new scale against 52% unscaled. That is about five or six events in a twenty-five minute
session rather than nine or ten.

`API.precipScale` IS LIVE, because how much weather a session should carry is a judgment made by
playing rather than by reading, and this is the second time in one day that a distribution has needed
a longer sitting than a harness run to be judged.

ONE EXISTING CHECK HAD TO MOVE WITH IT AND THAT IS RECORDED RATHER THAN QUIETLY ADJUSTED. biome-test
asserted that a cold city snows on more than 0.25 of rolls; it was calibrated against the table's own
figures and now reads 0.167. The claim is unchanged in kind - a cold city snows and a warm one cannot
- and only the frequency behind it moved. biome-test is 86 of 86.

NOT JUDGED ON A DEVICE. Whether five or six events in twenty-five minutes is right, or still too
many, is exactly the judgment that produced this ruling and it needs another sitting of the same
length.
