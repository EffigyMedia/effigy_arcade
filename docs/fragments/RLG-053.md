### RLG-053 — Every lamp is the unlit bulb drawn again, lit - not its own geometry
- **RULED 2026-08-28T18:03:02-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28:** "The turn signal needs to illuminate like a brake light, but not as vibrantly.
Per our lessons learned, duplicate the unlit bulb as the lit bulb overlay so the dimensions and
locations are pixel perfect. This needs to happen across the board - every vehicle in the game:
headlights, brake lights, police light bar front right, front left, rear right, rear left, turn
signal lights."

**This is a technique ruling, and it applies to every lamp on every vehicle.** A lit lamp is the
UNLIT BULB DRAWN AGAIN, lit. It is never a rectangle with its own coordinates.

**The engine does the wrong thing today and it is easy to show.** `playerBrakes` at road.js:8229
computes its own geometry from the bounding box:

    const lw = box.w*0.265, lh = box.h*0.11;
    const ly = box.y - box.h*0.34;
    for(const lx of [left + box.w*0.135, left + box.w*0.60])

Not one of those numbers comes from wherever the car painter actually put the tail lamp. They are a
second, independent description of the same object. **So the lit lamp and the unlit lamp are two
drawings that happen to agree**, and they agree only for as long as nobody edits either one. Every
reskin is an opportunity for them to drift, silently, with no error and nothing failing.

This is the same class of fault the project already carries notes about - a badge drawn before the
band that then covered it, a fill that stopped 0.045h short and left a visible seam. The fix the
owner has ruled removes the class rather than the instance: if the lit lamp IS the unlit bulb, drawn
with a different fill, the two cannot drift, because there is only one of them.

**Scope, as the owner listed it.** Every vehicle: headlights, brake lights, the police bar as four
separately addressable lamps - front left, front right, rear left, rear right - and the turn signals
[[RLG-052]] will add. The bar's four corners being individually addressable is what lets a light bar
run a real pattern instead of pulsing as one blob, and it is what a stopped car with a cruiser
behind it will need in [[RLG-047]].

**"Like a brake light, but not as vibrantly"** is the intensity ruling for the indicator
specifically. The brake lamp currently sits at alpha 1.0 while braking against 0.34 for the running
light; the indicator belongs between them, and the exact value is a look decision for the owner on a
real device rather than a number to pick here.

**The size of this is the honest problem with it.** There are painters for supercars, road cars, the
cruiser, the super cruiser, and every traffic body, plus the rear-view and front sprites. This is not
one edit. **The first unit should be ONE vehicle end to end** - lamps declared once by the painter,
consumed by the lit overlay - so the shape is proved before it is repeated fifteen times. The record
should say which vehicle was done and which were not, at every step, so a half-converted engine is
never mistaken for a finished one.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T18:07:02-04:00** — **Amendment from [[RLG-052]], owner 2026-08-28.** The player's car declares indicator bulbs like every
other vehicle, and nothing ever lights them - there is no control and no automatic signalling.

**This is a useful constraint on the technique rather than an exception to it.** It forces the two
halves apart at the seam the owner already ruled: the painter DECLARES lamps, a separate overlay
CONSUMES them and lights the ones that are on. If the design cannot express "this bulb exists and is
never lit" without a special case in the painter, the split is in the wrong place. The player's
indicators are the cheapest available test of that, so **do them in the first vehicle converted.**
- **RULED 2026-08-28T18:09:39-04:00** — **Amendment from [[RLG-052]], owner 2026-08-28, replacing the previous amendment.**

The earlier note here said the player's indicators are declared and never consumed, and used that as
a test of the painter/overlay split. **The owner has since ruled that every vehicle's indicators are
wired to FUNCTION** - racers and the player included. Nothing triggers them on those two, but the
path works.

**The test this fragment wanted is therefore a better one than the one it had.** It is no longer
"can the design express a bulb that is never lit" - that is satisfied by doing nothing. It is:

> **every vehicle's lamps must be drivable by the same code, and the only difference between a
> traffic car and a racer is what asks the lamp to come on.**

So the first vehicle converted should have its indicators driven end to end and proved to light,
even though nothing in the game will ask them to. If lighting a racer's indicator needs code that
lighting a traffic car's does not, the split is wrong and it will be cheaper to find out on vehicle
one than on vehicle fifteen.

**This applies to the whole lamp set, not just the indicators.** Headlights, brake lights and the
four corners of the police bar are the same shape of thing: declared once by the painter, lit by a
consumer that does not care what kind of vehicle it is looking at. The police bar is the case that
will expose a bad seam fastest, because it is the only lamp set whose four members are addressed
independently.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T07:06:03-04:00** — **PART ONE BUILT in v0.9.22: the MATADOR, end to end, and only the MATADOR.**

**The seam, as the owner ruled it.** A painter DECLARES its lamps as functions that draw themselves
and take one argument - whether they are lit. `sprite()` runs each one unlit as it bakes the canvas
and keeps them on it. `lampsHere` runs the same function again, on the screen, through a transform
that maps the sprite onto the car as drawn. **There is one description and it is executed twice**, so
the lit lamp cannot be anywhere but exactly where the unlit bulb is, and a reskin moves both because
there is only one of them.

**The fault was worse than this fragment recorded. There were THREE descriptions of the player's
tail lamps, not two, and one of them was unreachable.**

| where | geometry | state |
|---|---|---|
| the sprite, from `paintCar` | the body's own tail art | the real one |
| `tailLights` | `0.265w x 0.11h` at `0.135` and `0.60` | live, for traffic |
| `playerBrakes` | the same numbers again | **dead - nothing called it** |
| inline in `drawPlayer` | circles at `p.x +/- 0.24w`, `p.y - 0.34h` | live, and what you actually saw |

`playerBrakes` is deleted rather than converted: converting it would have kept a second answer alive.
The inline copy remains ONLY for bodies that have not been converted, and its comment now says so.

**A fourth fault fell out of it.** The inline glow was drawn OUTSIDE the transform that draws the
car, so it did not lean with the car. The declared lamps are lit inside that transform, so they roll
with it - which nobody had noticed because the glow was a soft circle.

**Indicators exist and are wired, on this car, today.** [[RLG-052]]'s refined ruling is that every
vehicle's signals FUNCTION and only the driver differs. The MATADOR now carries an unlit amber bulb
at the outer end of each blade housing, `API.signal` lights it, and `blinkPhase` runs at about 1.5Hz
whether or not anything is signalling - so a lamp that comes on is already in step with every other
one on the road, which is what will make a line of traffic look like traffic. Nothing in the game
asks for it. That is the ruling, not an omission.

**`tools/lamp-test.py` is new, and the first two versions of it were bad in an instructive way.**

The obvious test is to light the lamp and diff the frame. It is not a test: the sky turns, the tail
glow follows the daylight and the car breathes, so **800 to 1,300 pixels of the car change between
two frames with the lamp OFF**, against a few hundred from the lamp. A control diff removes most of
that and not reliably - the same check reported **100% and then 35%** on consecutive runs, which is a
harness reporting weather.

The claim does not need the world. **The declaration IS the drawing**, so the harness runs it lit
into a blank canvas the size of the sprite and asks whether those pixels sit where the sprite's own
unlit bulb sits - with the bulb found by reading the BAKED SPRITE, never from a coordinate repeated
out of `road.js`:

| | x | y |
|---|---|---|
| the unlit bulb, read from the sprite | 0.845 - 0.882 | 0.679 - 0.738 |
| the lit lamp, from the declaration | 0.845 - 0.882 | 0.673 - 0.744 |

**And it bites.** Shifting only the LIT pass by 5% of the sprite - exactly what a reskin that moved
the art and left the glow behind would look like - moves it to `0.850 - 0.932` and the check fails.
A second assertion keeps the live path honest: asking for the indicator in the running game changes
pixels at that bulb.

**WHAT IS NOT DONE, and the ruling requires this to be written down.** Converted: the MATADOR tail
and its indicators. **Not converted: CREST, STALLION, FORMULA, the cruiser, the super cruiser, every
traffic body, the front sprites and the rear-view.** They still paint lamps straight into their
sprites with no declaration, and they are still lit by hand-placed geometry - `tailLights` for
traffic and the inline circles for an unconverted player body. `lamp-test` SKIPS rather than passes
when the chosen body is one of those, so a half-converted engine cannot read as a finished one.

**Still to do before [[RLG-052]] can be built:** traffic bodies must declare indicators, because that
is the vehicle that will actually signal. The seam is proved; the repetition is not.

**THE FORMULA CAR HAS NO INDICATORS. Owner's ruling, 2026-08-29:** *"The formula car does not need
turn indicators."* A single-seater has none, and that is what the car IS rather than something
unfinished. It is the ONE vehicle allowed to be missing them, `lamp-test` names it as the exception,
and naming it is what stops the exception spreading.

**Four corrections from the owner on the first sheet, all built:**

1. **The unlit bulb is a DARK amber and the lit one a BRIGHT amber** - not a near-black casing and a
   white core. It has to read as one lamp in two states, or a signal appears out of nowhere.
2. **On MATADOR the indicator IS the outermost chevron**, not an amber lamp bolted beside the
   cluster. A tail that reads as one design in the dark should read as one design lit.
3. **On STALLION the indicators are the outer two of the four rings.** Four rings is the car's
   signature and hanging a fifth shape off each end spoils it - and a real four-ring tail does
   exactly this.
4. **The sheet showed the fleet twice.** The garage SALOON and the traffic sedan come out of one
   painter with different paint. Rows are grouped by painter now and each says who drives it.

**`tools/lamp-test.py` checks the fleet without knowing a single colour**, and the first version of
that check was worthless. It compared BOUNDING BOXES of the lamp lit and unlit - and a box cannot
see one lamp of a pair sliding inside it. Deliberately shifting a saloon's left tail lamp by 3% of
the car went undetected. It compares PIXELS now, with one pixel of slack for antialiasing and a 2%
tolerance, and the same drift fails it at 50 stray pixels of 1,156.

**Still not converted, and this is now a short list:** the FRONT sprites (headlights) and the
rear-view. Every rear sprite in the game is done. The front is a separate painter with its own lamp
art and is the obvious next piece if headlights are ever to be driven by anything.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T07:43:18-04:00** — **FOUR MORE CORRECTIONS FROM THE OWNER on the second sheet, 2026-08-29, all built in v0.9.24.**

1. **STALLION: all four rings are brake lights, and the indicator is the DOT INSIDE the outer two.**
   The first attempt made the outer rings themselves amber, which was a misreading - it changed what
   the brake light IS. A four-ring tail carries its indicator in the middle of the outer pair, and
   doing it that way leaves the car's signature intact lit or dark.
2. **MUSCLE: four boxes a side, and the outermost box is the indicator.** It was three bars with an
   amber lamp beside them. Same idea as MATADOR's outermost chevron - the signal is part of the
   cluster rather than a shape stuck next to it.
3. **VAN: the indicator goes ABOVE the brake lights, and only there.** A van's corner cluster stacks
   upward.
4. **LORRY: the roof running lights illuminate as brake lights as well.** They are part of the tail
   declaration now rather than decoration painted once - which is what a lorry does, and it is the
   thing you actually see of one at night when it slows in front of you.

**And the duplicate rows the owner asked about were mine, not the engine's.** The sheet shown in
chat had been rendered BEFORE the grouping fix landed; the file written to disk was the grouped one.
The sheet now prints each row's painter signature - `rig:cop`, `car:SUPERCRUISER` - so two rows that
look alike in white can be told apart at a glance rather than read as a duplicate. **When a picture
is the deliverable, the version shown and the version saved have to be the same render**, and they
were not.

**The fleet is 15 distinct vehicles**, not 26: the garage SALOON and the traffic sedan are one
painter in two liveries, and eleven such pairs exist. The 26 figure is sprites, and it is the one
`lamp-test` counts.
