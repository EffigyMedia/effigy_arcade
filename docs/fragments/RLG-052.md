### RLG-052 — A traffic car signals before it merges, and sometimes does not
- **RULED 2026-08-28T18:03:02-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28:** "Before a traffic car merges, it turns on its turn signal for a couple
seconds. This gives players a broadcast that someone is about to merge. We should have a small
percentage chance of merging without using a turn signal, maybe 25% or 20%."

**What makes this good is the second sentence.** A signal on every car is a HUD element painted on
the world - it removes the reading rather than rewarding it. One car in four or five saying nothing
keeps the player watching the traffic instead of watching for indicators, and it makes the signal
information rather than a guarantee.

**It cannot be built before [[RLG-040]], and that is a design fact rather than a scheduling one.** A
signal has to come on BEFORE the move, for a couple of seconds. Today a lane change is a per-frame
lateral pressure that starts the instant something is in front and stops when it goes away - there
is no moment at which the car has decided to move and has not yet moved. There is nothing to signal.
RLG-040 introduces exactly that: a target lane, committed to, and finished. The indicator hangs off
that decision. Build it before, and the signal can only come on at the same time as the movement,
which broadcasts nothing.

**It needs [[RLG-053]] for the drawing**, which is the owner's ruling that a lit lamp is the unlit
bulb drawn again rather than its own rectangle.

**Open, and the owner should answer it:** does the player's own car have indicators, and are they
driven by steering or by a control? The ruling names traffic only. A player signal is a different
feature - it is an input, not a tell - and inventing one is not implied by this.

**One thing to check rather than assume.** The turn signal is a repeating flash, and the project has
a note about periodicity being audible and visible where it is not wanted. At distance and at 190mph
a blinking two-pixel lamp may read as the rendering pop the owner has separately reported in
[[RLG-041]]. Whatever else happens, this must not be built while that report is open and unexplained,
or the two will be confused for each other.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T18:07:02-04:00** — **Owner's answer, 2026-08-28, to the question this fragment parked.**

> "The player's car should have unlit turn signals, because every car should have turn signals, but
> the player doesn't really have a need to use them, so there shouldn't really be controls to engage
> them."

**So: the bulbs exist on the player's car and they never light. No control, no binding, no automatic
signalling from steering.**

**This is a ruling about the MODEL, not about the feature**, and it lands more neatly than a
compromise would have. Under [[RLG-053]] a lit lamp is the unlit bulb drawn again, so the two halves
are already separate things: the painter DECLARES a bulb, and a lit overlay CONSUMES it. The player's
car declares its indicators like every other vehicle and nothing ever consumes them. There is no
special case in the painter and no `if(isPlayer)` anywhere - the overlay simply never fires.

**It also settles what the feature is for.** An indicator here is a TELL, not an input. It exists so
the player can read what another car is about to do. Giving the player a control would have made it
an input as well, and a signalling mechanic nobody needs is a button that has to be taught, bound on
a touch device that has no room for it, and then ignored. [[RLG-002]] rules this game MOBILE ONLY,
touch is the input, and screen space is the scarcest thing there is.

**Consequence worth stating plainly:** the player's indicators are geometry that is drawn and never
illuminated. A later session finding unlit bulbs with no code path to light them should read this
ruling before concluding something is unfinished. **They are not a stub.**
- **RULED 2026-08-28T18:09:24-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28, refining the ruling above. This CORRECTS the previous entry's wording.**

> "Racers also don't have a need to engage their turn signals, but the vehicles should still have
> them. They should be wired to engage their turn signals, but racers shouldn't really have a reason
> to do so nor should the player."

and, on what "wired" means:

> "By wired, I mean the car's turn signals should be wired to function."

**So the mechanism is REAL AND WORKING on every vehicle in the game.** Racers get indicators, the
player gets indicators, traffic gets indicators, and on all of them the signals actually function.
What differs is only whether anything asks them to come on.

| | bulbs | wired to function | anything triggers them |
|---|---|---|---|
| traffic | yes | yes | **yes** - before a merge, 75-80% of the time |
| racers | yes | yes | no reason to |
| the player | yes | yes | no reason to, and no control |

**CORRECTION.** The previous entry said the player's indicators are "geometry that is drawn and
never illuminated" with "no code path to light them", and called them "not a stub". The first half
is now wrong and is withdrawn: there IS a working path on the player's car, the same one every other
vehicle has. What is true is that nothing currently triggers it. "Not a stub" was right for the
wrong reason - they are not unfinished work because they are FINISHED and unused, rather than because
they are decoration.

**This is the same shape as the owner's ruling in [[RLG-042]]**, and the consistency is worth naming
because it is now a pattern rather than a one-off: *traffic cars all have real stats as well, except
their drivers are the limitation.* The car is fully capable; the DRIVER decides. Indicators are that
rule applied to a lamp instead of to an engine. A design that made indicators a traffic-only feature
would be the same mistake as giving traffic fake stats - a capability faked at the level of the
class instead of decided at the level of the driver.

**What it rules out, concretely:** no `if(isTraffic)` around the indicator logic, and no separate
traffic-only lamp code. One mechanism, on every vehicle, driven by whoever is driving.

**It also makes a later feature nearly free, which is the test of whether the seam is right.**
[[RLG-047]] has a non-racer pulling over for the police. A car pulling onto the verge signals first,
because that is what pulling over IS - and under this ruling there is nothing to build for it. If
that turns out to need new lamp code, the seam was drawn in the wrong place.
