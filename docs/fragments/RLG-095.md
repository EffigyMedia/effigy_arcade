### RLG-095 — The city's windows switch on and off on their own
- **RULED 2026-08-31T07:32:49-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: "the city skyline doesn't have dynamic window lights like it used to."

IT NEVER HAD THEM BY DESIGN, AND THAT IS THE USEFUL PART OF THIS REPORT. The skyline has always been
two sprites - the buildings, and their windows on a separate sheet drawn over the top with one global
alpha following `nightFall()`. `DESIGN.md` describes exactly that and nothing more: the city lights
come on at dusk, burn through the night and go out by mid-morning, with a handful of cold blue
windows among the sodium orange. There is one alpha for every window in the city. No window has ever
switched on by itself.

SO WHAT THE OWNER IS REMEMBERING IS THE DEFECT IN [[RLG-094]], SEEN FROM THE SIDE WHERE IT LOOKED
GOOD. The window sheet was regenerated every six seconds along with the rest of the city, with new
window positions and a fresh warm-or-cold roll for each one. At a glance that reads as windows
switching on and off across the skyline - which is a thing cities do, which is why it was never
reported as a fault and was instead missed when it went.

THE LIVELINESS IS WORTH KEEPING. ITS SOURCE IS NOT. A city whose entire window pattern is replaced
ten times a minute is not a city where people are switching lights on; it is a different building
every time you look. The behaviour to build is the one the accident imitated: individual windows
changing state on their own slow clocks, on a silhouette that stays put.

WHAT THIS NEEDS, AND NONE OF IT IS BUILT:

  A PER-WINDOW CLOCK. Every window already exists as an entry in the plan - position, and now its
  warm-or-cold roll. A third field is what it is doing: a phase and a period of its own, hashed from
  the window's index so it is stable across a reload and costs no storage. The whole city must not
  breathe together, which is the failure mode the thruster note in RLG-012 already records: anything
  driven by one clock reads as a pulse rather than as a place.

  AND IT MUST NOT BE A PER-FRAME RANDOM. That reads as a rendering fault, which is the same lesson
  from the same fragment. A window turns on and STAYS on for minutes.

  THE SHEET CANNOT STAY ONE SPRITE, or at least not one drawn whole. A baked sheet is a single alpha
  by construction. Either the windows are drawn individually at their own alphas - 1024 pixels of
  the near band only, which is what carries windows at all - or the sheet is split into a few
  sub-sheets that can be lit against each other. The first is honest and the cost is unmeasured.

  IT INTERACTS WITH THE HOUR, NOT INSTEAD OF IT. `lampsOn()` still decides whether the city is lit
  at all. A window's own clock decides whether THIS window is on within that. At midday the city is
  dark whatever any window thinks.

MEASURE IT THE WAY THE SILHOUETTE WAS MEASURED. `skyline-test.py` already reads the sprite and
already asserts the SHAPE does not move; the question here is the opposite one, and it is the same
instrument turned round - do the lit pixels change over time while the silhouette does not. Both
assertions have to hold at once, which is what stops this ruling from being built by reintroducing
RLG-094.

NOT STARTED. Raised while RLG-094 was being closed, and deliberately left for its own unit: the
stable plan is what makes per-window state possible at all, so the order is not negotiable.
- **RULED 2026-08-31T07:48:09-04:00** — BUILT IN 0.10.19, AND THE HABIT IS ON THE WINDOW. Every window in the plan now carries three more
fields beside its position and its colour: a phase, a period between 26 and 190 seconds, and a duty
between 0.45 and 0.87. It is lit when its own cycle says so. A long period with a high duty is an
office block that stays on all night; a short one with a low duty is somebody up and down the stairs.

THE SPREAD OF PERIODS IS THE WHOLE POINT AND IT IS NOT DECORATION. A city where every window shares
one clock is a pulse, not a place - the same lesson RLG-012 wrote down about a thruster driven by a
single sine, and the same reason the plume there breathes on two waves of different periods rather
than jittering per frame. Measured across six minutes of window clock, the lit pixel count runs 210
to 264 against a peak of 264: it drifts, it never surges, and 65 of 1024 columns change between two
samples.

AND IT IS NOT A PER-FRAME RANDOM. A window turns on and STAYS on for tens of seconds. That is the
other half of RLG-012's note, and it is the difference between a city and a rendering fault.

THE SHEET IS REPAINTED WHEN THE PATTERN CHANGES, NOT EVERY FRAME. Drawing a hundred and twenty
windows individually, per tile, in two views, is about three hundred fills a frame for something that
changes a few times a second. `windowMask` walks the list and returns what is lit as a string; if
that differs from what is on the sheet, the sheet is repainted, and not otherwise. A repaint is
throttled to one every 0.35 seconds so a burst of windows changing in one frame cannot cost more
than a single one. The BUILDINGS sheet - the expensive one, with every shape in it - is never
touched by any of this.

IT IS DRIVEN FROM `drawSky` ON A FIXED FRAME STEP, NOT FROM THE STEP LOOP, and the second reason is
what decides it. The step loop returns early when the run is over or the car is wrecked, so a city
would freeze on the results screen. And the TITLE draws a skyline without running a step at all -
which is precisely why `skySmooth` beside it is already chased on a fixed step, with a note saying a
title screen and a run both call this at the same rate. A cycle of 26 to 190 seconds cannot show the
difference between a fixed step and a real one. A dead city on the menu would have been obvious.

THE HOUR STILL DECIDES WHETHER THE CITY IS LIT AT ALL. `lampsOn()` is untouched and still sets the
alpha the sheet is drawn at. A window's own clock only decides whether THIS window is on within
that, so at midday the city is dark whatever any window thinks. The check asserts the hour is dark
before it asserts anything about windows, so a run cannot report switching lights in a city nobody
can see.

MEASURED, AND BOTH HALVES ARE WATCHED FAILING. `tools/skyline-test.py` reads the LIT SHEET rather
than the plan - the sheet is what gets drawn, and reading the plan would be the harness asking the
engine to agree with its own idea of which windows ought to be on.

  The pattern moves: 65 of 1024 columns change across six minutes of window clock.

  The buildings do not: zero columns of silhouette move in the same samples. That assertion is what
  stops this ruling being satisfied by putting RLG-094's defect back, because regenerating the city
  would change the windows too.

  And with the clock HELD STILL at 120 seconds, six samples apart, zero columns change. Without that
  control the first assertion could have been reading noise.

WHAT A HARNESS CANNOT SAY. Whether the rate reads as a living city or as a twitch is the owner's
call. The three numbers that decide it are the period range, the duty range and the repaint
throttle, and all three are in `skylinePlanFor` and beside it rather than buried in an expression.
The accident this replaces repatterned the WHOLE city every six seconds, which is far busier than
what is built here - so if it now reads as too calm, that is the number to move and not the design.

NO MEASURABLE COST. Two pairs of fps-test runs at midnight, mine against the baseline: CITY 52.4-56.8
against 55.6-60.4 in the first pair and 56.0-59.6 against 55.6-58.0 in the second. The ranges overlap
in both and the direction FLIPS between them, which is the harness's own definition of no change.
FOREST, which has no windows at all, moved further than CITY did in the first pair - 45.6-53.6
against 54.4-60.0 - and came back higher than the baseline in the second. That is the machine, not
the change, and it is exactly why the harness prints the spread.
