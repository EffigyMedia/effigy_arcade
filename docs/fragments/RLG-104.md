### RLG-104 — A canyon, where the skyline is a wall
- **RULED 2026-08-31T10:50:57-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31, answering the question put in [[RLG-102]]: of the four gaps offered, canyon and
tunnel are the two to build.

WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT IS STRUCTURAL, NOT A PALETTE. Every place on the board has a horizon and a
skyline drawn as a distant silhouette band. A canyon has neither: rock walls close on both sides, the
skyline IS the wall, and the sky is a strip between two masses rather than the top two thirds of the
frame. That is the first place where `buildSkyline`'s premise - a band of shapes tiled along the
horizon - does not hold, and it is why this is worth building rather than a reskin of MOUNTAIN.

SO THE SKYLINE GENERATOR NEEDS A SECOND SHAPE OF ANSWER, and that is the real work. Today
`skylinePlanFor` produces a plan of free-standing objects with gaps between them; a canyon wall is
continuous, has no gaps, and rises out of the frame. Whether that is a new `kind` in the same
generator or a different painter for this place is the first decision, and it should be made by
looking at what `paintSkylineShape` can express rather than by reasoning about it.

IT IS ALSO THE NATURAL HOME FOR VERTICAL CURVATURE. The owner has twice ruled that the road is never
completely flat and that the biome drives how much it climbs. MOUNTAIN is 1.00 on `hill` and is the
only place that really uses the renderer's range. A canyon floor follows the water that cut it, so it
should be high on `bend` and moderate on `hill` - a road that turns constantly between walls, which
is a combination nothing on the board has.

AND THE WALLS ARE A CULLING QUESTION BEFORE THEY ARE AN ART QUESTION. Scenery outboard of the lamp
posts is [[RLG-073]], still unbuilt. A canyon wall is the largest possible instance of that: it is
outboard, it is enormous, and it is on both sides at once. Whatever RLG-073 decides about the far
scenery decides how a wall is drawn, so the same note that defers the coast's boats applies here.

DO NOT LET IT BECOME A CORRIDOR THE PLAYER CANNOT LEAVE. The corner cap in this engine is a renderer
limit rather than a taste one - past about 90 degrees the road leaves the frame - and a canyon invites
exactly the tight bends that reach it. The place has to stay driveable at the speeds these cars do.
- **RULED 2026-09-01T08:35:23-04:00** — BUILT 2026-09-01, v0.11.5. The canyon is on the board, and the capability it needed is
built once and deliberately rather than three times by accident.

THE GENERATOR NEEDED A SECOND SHAPE OF ANSWER, AND THE RULING SAID TO DECIDE IT BY LOOKING
AT WHAT `paintSkylineShape` CAN EXPRESS. Looking settled it: the painter can already draw
any polygon, and what could not be expressed was the PLAN. Every form walks the horizon
placing a free-standing shape and then a gap, and each shape rolls its own height with no
memory of its neighbour. Run that with the gap set to zero and the result is a comb of
unrelated columns.

SO A CONTINUOUS FORM IS PLANNED THE OTHER WAY ROUND. `SKY_CONTINUOUS` holds one table row
per continuous form and `contPlan` builds it: the crest is a bounded random WALK sampled at
a fixed step, and each plan entry carries the height at BOTH of its ends. A segment is then
a quad from its neighbour's crest to the next one, so the profile is connected by
construction rather than by the gaps happening to be zero. The bridge towers of RLG-112 and
the jungle canopy of RLG-113 are a table row each, and no code.

AND IT HAS TO CLOSE ON ITSELF, WHICH IS THE PART THAT WOULD HAVE BEEN MISSED. The skyline
sprite TILES across the frame. A walk that ends where it likes puts a cliff at the seam and
then repeats that cliff across the whole horizon. The last fifth of the walk is bent back
onto its own first sample, which costs one loop.

THE SEAM IS ALSO THE ONLY RULE THAT SEPARATES A WALL FROM A ROW OF OBJECTS, and that was
measured rather than assumed. The first version of the harness asserted the STEP alone -
that a continuous crest never jumps - and it went red on the controls: a peak line overlaps
its own neighbours by construction, so MOUNTAIN and TUNDRA have no gaps AND a small step,
and they are still rows of peaks. What only a closed walk can pass is the seam, because
nothing else plans the two ends of a tile together. MOUNTAIN misses it by 60 pixels. The
three rules together separate the canyon from every other place on the board, and no one of
them does it alone.

`skyRise` IS THE SECOND HALF OF THE PLACE AND IT IS NOT DECORATION. The skyline band was a
fixed eighth of the frame for every place, which is right for a horizon miles off and wrong
for a wall a few hundred feet away. The place states a multiple - 2.4 - and the scale is
uniform, so a taller band is a wider tile too. That is correct rather than convenient: a
nearer mass is bigger in both directions. The mirror takes the same multiple, or a canyon
behind you would be a distant ridge while the same wall ahead left the frame.

THE RELIEF HAD TO BE MADE CONTINUOUS TOO, AND THE FIRST CAPTURE IS WHY. Each falling face
was shaded by its own drop, so a segment's shading ended where the segment did - and the
wall came out as a row of upright panels, which is the row of objects this form exists to
stop, painted back on top of a continuous silhouette. A cap of ONE depth fixes it by
construction: both edges of the band are the crest itself, offset by the same amount
everywhere, so neighbours share an edge exactly.

TWO NAME BRANCHES WENT WITH IT, and both are the shape RLG-102 already had to remove when a
farmland was given a city. `buildSkyline` chose its silhouette base colour by testing
`B.name === 'TUNDRA'`; a place states `skyBase` now, so the canyon's red sandstone and the
tundra's ice are both stated rather than recognised. And `ROCKFACE` took a `snow` BOOLEAN,
which could only ever be grey rock or white rock; it takes a named `tone`, and the canyon's
walls are MOUNTAIN's own ranks in sandstone rather than a sixth set of art.

THE WALLS ARE THE SCENERY SYSTEM, NOT A SECOND ONE. The ruling called them the largest
possible instance of the far-scenery question in RLG-073, and warned that whatever RLG-073
decides about culling decides how a wall is drawn. Building them out of the ranks MOUNTAIN
already uses means they inherit that answer whenever it lands, instead of needing it now:
`out` 0.10 is where the cornfield stands, `outFar` 9.0 carries the ranks back until the
rock has no visible far edge, and density 0.98 over five ranks is what makes the
silhouettes overlap into one mass.

DRIVEABLE, AND THE RULING'S WARNING IS ANSWERED BY A NUMBER. Bend 0.90 against hill 0.40 -
the pair nothing else on the board has, and deliberately UNDER the mountain's 1.00, which
is the top of the range this renderer's corner cap allows. drive-test came back 39/39.

NOT JUDGED ON A DEVICE. tools/canyon-test.py is green and the capture is in the unit
record, but whether the strip of sky between the masses reads as a canyon at speed, whether
the far bands recede or merely go pale, and whether a road at 0.90 of bend between walls is
comfortable on a phone are all the owner's call.
