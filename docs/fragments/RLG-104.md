### RLG-104 — A canyon, where the skyline is a wall
- **RULED 2026-08-31T10:50:57-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31, answering the question put in [[RLG-102]]: of the four gaps offered, canyon and
tunnel are the two to build.

WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT IS STRUCTURAL, NOT A PALETTE. Every place on the board has a horizon and a
skyline drawn as a distant silhouette band. A canyon has neither: rock walls close on both sides, the
skyline IS the wall, and the sky is a strip between two masses rather than the top two thirds of the
frame. That is the first place where `buildSkyline`'s premise - a band of shapes tiled along the
horizon - does not hold, and it is why this is worth building rather than a reskin of MOUNTAIN.

SO THE SKYLINE GENERATOR NEEDS A SECOND SHAPE OF ANSWER, and that is the real work. Today
`skylinePlanFor` produces a plan of free-standing objects with gaps between them; a canyon wall is
continuous, has no gaps, and rises out of the frame. Whether that is a new `kind` in the same
generator or a different painter for this place is the first decision, and it should be made by
looking at what `paintSkylineShape` can express rather than by reasoning about it.

IT IS ALSO THE NATURAL HOME FOR VERTICAL CURVATURE. The owner has twice ruled that the road is never
completely flat and that the biome drives how much it climbs. MOUNTAIN is 1.00 on `hill` and is the
only place that really uses the renderer's range. A canyon floor follows the water that cut it, so it
should be high on `bend` and moderate on `hill` - a road that turns constantly between walls, which
is a combination nothing on the board has.

AND THE WALLS ARE A CULLING QUESTION BEFORE THEY ARE AN ART QUESTION. Scenery outboard of the lamp
posts is [[RLG-073]], still unbuilt. A canyon wall is the largest possible instance of that: it is
outboard, it is enormous, and it is on both sides at once. Whatever RLG-073 decides about the far
scenery decides how a wall is drawn, so the same note that defers the coast's boats applies here.

DO NOT LET IT BECOME A CORRIDOR THE PLAYER CANNOT LEAVE. The corner cap in this engine is a renderer
limit rather than a taste one - past about 90 degrees the road leaves the frame - and a canyon invites
exactly the tight bends that reach it. The place has to stay driveable at the speeds these cars do.
