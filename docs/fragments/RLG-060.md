### RLG-060 — Lightning, thunder and windshield wipers in heavy weather
- **RULED 2026-08-29T03:47:45-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-29, filed to the queue and NOT started:**

> "Lightning (VFX) / thunder (SFX) during heavy rain, which could be a modifier of the rain storm.
> We could even add animated windshield wipers during weather events.
>
> Lightning could either be a drawn strike in the distance and/or the screen flashing white for a
> frame or two."

**Three things, and the owner has already ruled on the hardest one.** Lightning is either a drawn
strike on the horizon or a white flash for a frame or two, **or both** - and "or both" is the right
answer, because they are different distances. A strike is weather happening somewhere else; a flash
is weather happening to you. A storm that only ever flashes has no location, and one that only ever
draws a bolt never startles.

**"A modifier of the rain storm" is the load-bearing phrase.** Lightning is not a new weather state.
The engine has `wet`, and heavy rain is the top of that range - so this is an intensity band within
weather that already exists, in the same shape as [[RLG-017]]'s ruling that weather ends with the
biome.

**Thunder follows the strike by the distance to it**, and that is free drama nobody has to author:
the delay between the flash and the crack IS how far away the storm is. If a bolt is drawn on the
horizon, its thunder is seconds late; if the screen flashes white, it is immediate.

**The wipers are a different feature wearing the same coat, and they are the risky one.** The
project has a standing note about periodicity being visible where it is not wanted, and a wiper is
the most periodic thing that could ever cross this screen - a moving occluder over the whole view,
forever, at a fixed rate. **Build it last, behind an option, and judge it on the device.** A wiper
that is right for ten seconds and maddening for ten minutes is the likely outcome and the harnesses
cannot see it.

**The flash is a hazard and must be treated as one.** A full-screen white flash at a random interval
is a photosensitivity trigger. It wants a ceiling on brightness and frequency, and it should be
possible to turn off - which makes it the second thing in this project to need a comfort option, and
worth designing as one rather than as a special case.

**Read before starting:** [[RLG-041]] is a rendering pop the owner reported and is not fully closed.
A screen-wide flash and a moving wiper are exactly the kind of thing that gets blamed for a
rendering fault, or hides one. Finish reading the pop before adding either.

**Also open:** whether lightning lights the ROAD - a bolt that flashes the sky and leaves the tarmac
unchanged reads as a filter over the picture rather than as light in the world. The day cycle already
tints the ground; a flash borrowing that path would be the cheap and correct version.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T03:51:11-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-29, adding a fourth thing to this family:**

> "Would it be hard to add simulated rain drops / snow flakes 'on the camera' during weather events?
> If we do this and it looks good enough (meaning I don't cut it), maybe put the option to turn this
> on or off as well."

**Not hard, and it is the cheapest item in this fragment.** `drawRain` already runs in screen space
after the world is drawn - 90 falling streaks with no projection, no depth and no interaction with
the road. Drops held on the lens are the same layer with a different motion: they sit still, drift
down slowly, and are re-seeded rather than falling through. There is nothing new to build
underneath it.

**It belongs here rather than in its own fragment because of the wipers.** A drop on the camera is
the thing a wiper wipes. Build the drops first and the wiper has a reason to exist and something to
act on; build the wiper first and it sweeps a clean screen. If the drops are cut, the wiper should be
cut with them.

**"If it looks good enough, meaning I don't cut it" is the ruling on how to judge it**, and it is
the right one for this feature: nothing in the harnesses can tell whether a lens effect reads as
weather or as dirt. It is an owner verdict on a device, and the option exists because the answer may
be "yes, and not always".

**The option is the same option as the flash.** Both are effects laid over the whole picture rather
than in the world, and a player who wants one off usually wants the other off. Two switches for the
same taste is two switches to find on a phone screen.

**One thing to get right, and it is the same trap as the wipers:** anything on the lens is FIXED to
the screen while everything else moves, which is exactly what makes it read as a windscreen - and
exactly what makes it read as a smudge on the display if the drops never change. Re-seed them, and
tie their density to `wet` and `snowy` so the effect arrives with the weather rather than being
present at a fixed strength whenever it rains.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T04:35:14-04:00** — **CORRECTION from the owner, 2026-08-29, to the wipers half of this fragment:**

> "I don't mean wipers for the game screen, only on the cars in the rearview. The screen droplets
> should fade soon after appearing."

**That is a much better feature than the one written down here, and it removes the risk this
fragment was most worried about.** The entry above treats wipers as a moving occluder over the whole
view, forever, at a fixed rate - the most periodic thing that could cross this screen - and says to
build it last, behind an option, and to judge it on the device. **None of that applies any more.**

**Wipers are a detail ON OTHER CARS, seen in the rearview.** A car behind you shows you its
windscreen, so its wipers are visible and they are a small moving detail in a small mirror. They
never cross the player's view, they cannot occlude anything the player needs, and they are one more
thing that makes the traffic look alive in the one place where you study it.

**And that lands it squarely in [[RLG-052]] and [[RLG-053]]'s territory rather than in weather VFX.**
A wiper is an animated part of a vehicle, like an indicator: declared by the painter, driven by
something that decides it is on. Under RLG-053's rule - a lit lamp is the unlit bulb drawn again -
a wiper is a part of the car's own drawing rather than an overlay invented for the mirror.

**The droplets fade soon after appearing**, which is also a correction: the entry above says to
re-seed them so they do not read as a smudge on the display. The owner has ruled the specific
behaviour - a drop arrives, sits, and fades - and that is both simpler than re-seeding and more like
what a windscreen does at speed.

**What survives from the original entry:** the drops are cheap and go in `drawRain`'s screen-space
layer, they need an option because the owner may cut them, and the lightning flash is a
photosensitivity hazard that wants the same switch. What is withdrawn is the wipers being a
full-screen effect at all.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T13:31:19-04:00** — BUILT, the first half: the wipers move and the lens gets wet.

THE WIPERS. Every vehicle has carried a `parts.wipers(g, t)` since the fronts were drawn - 0 parked,
1 at full extension - and the only thing that had ever called it was the fleet sheet. The mirror drew
them PARKED, every frame, in all weathers. All that was missing was something to turn.

ONE CLOCK FOR THE WHOLE ROAD, and that is a deliberate choice rather than a shortcut. Wipers on
different cars are not synchronised in life, but at sprite size a road full of blades each keeping
its own time reads as noise; one rhythm reads as weather. `wipePhase` runs at a rate set by how hard
it is raining - which is what a driver does with the stalk - and `wipeAt()` turns it into a stroke:
out, back, and a rest at the bottom, because a blade that never stops is a metronome.

RAIN ON THE LENS. Drops that land on the CAMERA rather than on the road: they arrive faster the
harder it rains and the faster you are going, they crawl, and THE WHOLE SCREEN CLEARS WHEN THE WIPER
PASSES. That last part is the one that makes rain feel like rain rather than like a filter over the
picture - the same clock drives both, so the lens clears on the stroke you can see on the car in
front.

The player's car has no windscreen in this view, so this is a lens rather than a screen. That is the
convention a chase camera uses, and it is what the owner asked for by name.

STILL OPEN on this fragment: lightning and thunder, and the two weather layers of RLG-023. The sky
overhaul (RLG-057) is the next thing the owner has queued and those belong with it.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T14:51:37-04:00** — THUNDER IS LOUD NOW, AND THE DELAY IS THE DISTANCE. Owner, 2026-08-29: "turn the volume for the
thunder way up", and then "we could approximate a random distance by having a random time range of
250 ms to 5000 ms."

THE LEVEL. It was 0.26 and 0.20 at the top of its range, which put it under `dead` at 0.26 and
`copDown` at 0.22 - ordinary event sounds. It is 0.46, 0.37 and 0.20 now, about 1.8x. The headroom
was counted rather than guessed: the master bus is 0.85 with no compressor after it, so the ceiling
is real and clipping is on the other side of it. The three beds do not peak together - the second
starts 0.12s after the first and the crack is a fifth of a second long - and the worst case through
the master is about 0.71, which leaves the engine and the tyres room to be heard under it.

ONE DISTANCE, AND EVERYTHING COMES OFF IT. The level and the delay used to be rolled separately -
`boltMag = rnd(0.45, 1)` and `thunderIn = rnd(0.8, 3.4)` - so they could disagree. A strike could
arrive at full volume five seconds after its flash, or barely register a quarter of a second later.
`rollStrike()` now rolls the distance once, and the delay, the loudness and the brightness of the
flash all follow from it. The floor is 0.38 rather than zero, because a storm you cannot hear at all
is not a storm at a distance, it is a storm that is not happening.

API.strike IS THE LIGHTNING AND NOT THE THUNDER, and this cost a wrong measurement before it was
noticed. It sets the flash and its magnitude and returns; the sound is scheduled seconds later by the
step loop from `thunderIn`. The first version of the loudness check struck, waited four seconds and
read whatever gains had accumulated - and the engine and the tyres use the same `sfx.noise`, so the
total came to 10.0 against a master bus of 0.85. That was not a measurement of thunder at all.

WHAT THE SOUND ASKS FOR, NOT WHAT THE GRAPH REPORTS. RLG-065 cost three attempts on that distinction:
a GainNode on a closed context reports a healthy value quite happily, and counting rebuild calls read
zero on a build that was working. The check wraps `AR.sfx.noise`, calls `snd.thunder` and unwraps
inside one evaluate, so it records exactly the gains thunder handed the synthesizer and nothing else.

AND BOTH CHECKS WERE WATCHED FAILING. The old gains were put back and the two loudness assertions went
red at 0.260 and 0.200, then the new values were restored. The distance checks run 400 rolls of the
real function the storm uses, because `boltNext` is 5 to 22 seconds between strikes and a harness
cannot wait that out. Measured: the full 250ms to 5s range is used, and the nearest 40 strikes mean
0.9-odd against the furthest 40, which is the correlation being asserted rather than assumed.

NOT VERIFIED HERE, AND THIS ONE CANNOT BE. A harness cannot listen. Whether thunder is now too loud,
or still not loud enough, is the owner's call on the device with the phone's own speaker.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T15:04:54-04:00** — THUNDER IS TWO SOUNDS AND DISTANCE TREATS THEM DIFFERENTLY. Owner, 2026-08-29: "we could split the
thunder into two sounds: a mid/high frequency crack, and a low/mid frequency rumble. Distance will
lower the volume of both but the mid/high frequency crack will be rolled off more heavily based on
distance."

WHY THAT IS RIGHT, and it is what air does to sound. High frequencies are absorbed over distance and
scattered by everything they pass; low ones are not. A strike overhead is a crack with a rumble under
it. The same strike a mile off is the rumble alone, and it is longer, because what reaches you has
come by several paths.

THE NUMBERS, MEASURED ACROSS THE RANGE. The crack falls as the nearness raised to 2.2 and the rumble
falls in a straight line:

  far 0.00   rumble 0.460   crack 0.300   rumble runs 2.80s
  far 0.25   rumble 0.390   crack 0.159   rumble runs 3.25s
  far 0.50   rumble 0.320   crack 0.065   rumble runs 3.70s
  far 0.75   rumble 0.250   crack 0.014   rumble runs 4.15s
  far 1.00   rumble 0.180   crack 0.000   rumble runs 4.60s

At half distance the crack keeps 22% of its close level and the rumble keeps 70%. Past two thirds the
crack is gone entirely. The rumble never drops below 0.18, because a storm you cannot hear at all is
not a storm at a distance.

THE HEADROOM STILL HOLDS. Peak sum is 0.46 + 0.36 + 0.30 = 1.12 against a master bus of 0.85 and no
compressor, so the worst case is 0.95. The three do not peak together in any case: the second bed
starts 0.12s in and the crack is a fifth of a second long.

THE TWO SOUNDS ARE TOLD APART BY THEIR FILTER, which is what makes them two sounds. The rumble is
lowpass and the crack is highpass. Reading the gains alone could not say which was which, and a check
that cannot name what it measured cannot tell a quiet crack from a quiet rumble.

THE HARNESS PINS THE TIME OF DAY NOW, and that came out of a real flake rather than a precaution. A
run started at DUSK drifts into the night branch part way through. There the ground is already dark,
so the rain darkening has almost no room to show, and the verge check went red on one run in six at a
margin of about three. Loosening the threshold would have hidden a real thing about the effect -
rain barely reads on the land at night - so the run is pinned to MIDDAY instead. Six consecutive
green runs since, across DESERT, MOUNTAIN and TUNDRA.

NOT VERIFIED HERE, AND IT CANNOT BE. A harness cannot listen. Whether the crack now reads as a crack,
and whether a distant strike still reads as thunder rather than as noise, is the owner's call on the
device.
