### RLG-109 — A place should hand over to a place it could plausibly be next to
- **RULED 2026-08-31T10:53:59-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: "As a rule for biome switching, we should try to not have unrealistic transitions,
like desert into tundra and vice versa. Maybe the way to do this is to assign biomes a temperature and
step between them on an axis I guess - what are your thoughts on that?"

THE INSTINCT IS RIGHT AND THE AXIS IS ALMOST ALREADY THERE. Every place already declares `rain` and
`snow`, and those two numbers ARE a climate position - snow is what cold means in this engine, and
rain is moisture. Laid out, they separate the places the owner wants separated without anything new
being invented:

    place       rain   snow
    DESERT      0.04   0.00      hot and dry
    TUNDRA      0.10   0.62      cold and dry
    COASTAL     0.34   0.00      mild and wet
    MOUNTAIN    0.30   0.34      cold and wet
    FOREST      0.42   0.06      mild and wet
    SWAMP       0.62   0.00      hot and wet
    CITY        0.38   0.10      no climate of its own

Desert to tundra is the largest jump on the `snow` axis on the board - 0.00 to 0.62 - which is exactly
the pair the owner named. That is a good sign that the numbers already know the answer.

BUT ONE AXIS IS NOT ENOUGH, AND THE REASON MATTERS. A single temperature line forces a TOTAL ORDER,
and a total order makes real neighbours impossible. Coastal to tundra is a perfectly ordinary piece of
the world - Norway, Iceland, the north Atlantic - and any ordering that puts desert and swamp at one
end and tundra at the other has to pass through the mild places to get anywhere. Two axes give
coast-and-cold for free; one axis forbids it.

AND SOME PLACES HAVE NO CLIMATE AT ALL. A city exists in every climate on earth. So do the two places
just approved: a tunnel is a hole in the ground and a canyon is rock, and neither says anything about
temperature. Forcing them onto the axis is the mistake that would make this feel arbitrary - a city
that cannot follow a desert is wrong in a way a player would notice faster than the thing being fixed.
The model needs a way for a place to say "anywhere".

SO THE RECOMMENDATION, AND IT IS ONE CHANGE TO ONE FUNCTION. Do not add a per-pair table. Ten places
is forty-five pairs, every new place makes somebody edit ten entries, and a table is a thing that goes
stale silently. Instead:

  DERIVE THE DISTANCE from `rain` and `snow`, weighting `snow` more heavily because temperature is
  what the owner actually noticed. That number already exists per place and needs no maintenance.

  WEIGHT THE PICK BY IT rather than forbidding jumps outright. `openBiome` and the biome roll choose
  uniformly from `BIOME_KEYS` today; choosing with a weight that falls off with distance keeps the
  world varied while making desert-to-tundra rare rather than illegal. A hard ban is brittle - it
  needs a fallback when nothing qualifies, and that fallback is where the arbitrary-looking choices
  come from.

  LET A PLACE DECLARE ITSELF NEUTRAL, one optional field, defaulting to not-neutral so nothing that
  exists changes. City, tunnel and canyon set it and can follow anything. That is the same shape
  `cover` took for the open sky: a tendency stated by the place, with everything else getting the
  ordinary behaviour for free.

IT ALSO MAKES THE TRANSITION LOOK BETTER, WHICH IS A SECOND ARGUMENT FOR IT. The crossing takes the
whole length of the drawn road: the two grounds blend across the band, the two skylines hand over by
sinking and rising, and the weather tapers between them. All three of those are interpolations between
two palettes, and they read better the closer the palettes are. So near-neighbour transitions are not
only more plausible, they are less work for the renderer to make convincing.

WHAT TO MEASURE. Not "is this transition realistic" - that is a judgement. What a harness can say is
the DISTRIBUTION: over a few thousand rolls, how often each pair comes up, and that the far pairs are
rare rather than absent. `biome-test` already samples the biome roll, so the instrument exists. The
check should also assert that every place is still reachable from every other by SOME route, or the
world quietly splits into two halves that never meet.

NOT STARTED. The owner asked for thoughts rather than for the change.
- **RULED 2026-08-31T11:05:25-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-08-31: "let's re-derive each biome's stats based on its climate. For example, if city is
truly neutral then it should have a .5 rain and snow. If tundra is cold and dry, then it should have a
lower chance for snow and an even lower chance for rain but still have a small baseline snow coverage
on the ground maybe like .2. Let's re-derive the entire biome list, including the new ones."

ONE CORRECTION FIRST, AND IT IS THE WHOLE REASON THIS NEEDS WRITING DOWN. "City is neutral, so 0.5
rain and 0.5 snow" would not make a city neutral - it would make it the snowiest place on the board.
`rain` and `snow` are not a position on a scale; they are PROBABILITIES that this place's weather roll
lands on rain or on snow. A city at snow 0.5 snows half the time, everywhere, all year. And cover is
derived from their sum, so it would also be permanently overcast: (0.5 + 0.5) * 1.6 clamps to the
0.75 ceiling.

THREE DIFFERENT QUANTITIES WERE BEING SPOKEN OF AS ONE, AND SEPARATING THEM IS THE FIX:

  WHERE A PLACE SITS IN THE CLIMATE - what decides which places can be next to each other. This does
  not exist yet and is what the owner is reaching for.

  HOW OFTEN IT RAINS OR SNOWS THERE - `rain` and `snow`, which exist and are probabilities.

  WHAT IS PERMANENTLY ON THE GROUND - `snowFloor`, which exists and is TUNDRA's alone at 0.50. This is
  the "small baseline snow coverage" in the owner's own sentence, and it is already a separate field.

SO THE POSITION IS NEW AND THE REST IS DERIVED FROM IT. Two axes, because one forces a total order and
a total order forbids coastal-and-cold, which is an ordinary piece of the world:

    temp   0 coldest, 1 hottest
    damp   0 driest,  1 wettest

And then, with no hand-tuned weather numbers left anywhere:

    P         = damp * 0.75                          how often it precipitates at all
    snowShare = clamp((0.50 - temp) / 0.50, 0, 1)    how much of that falls as snow
    snow      = P * snowShare
    rain      = P * (1 - snowShare)
    snowFloor = clamp((0.25 - temp) / 0.25, 0, 1) * 0.60

THE DERIVED TABLE. `cover` is the open-sky multiplier from the coastal work and stays a stated field,
because how much sky a place shows is about its shape rather than its climate - a forest is closed in
by trees whatever the weather.

    place      temp  damp  |  rain  snow  snowFloor  cover  |  today
    DESERT     0.95  0.05  |  0.04  0.00    0.00      1.00  |  0.04 / 0.00
    SWAMP      0.80  0.85  |  0.64  0.00    0.00      1.00  |  0.62 / 0.00
    CANYON     0.70  0.15  |  0.11  0.00    0.00      1.00  |  new
    FARMLAND   0.60  0.45  |  0.34  0.00    0.00      0.45  |  new
    COASTAL    0.55  0.50  |  0.38  0.00    0.00      0.45  |  0.34 / 0.00
    FOREST     0.45  0.70  |  0.47  0.05    0.00      1.00  |  0.42 / 0.06
    CITY       0.45  0.50  |  0.34  0.04    0.00      1.00  |  0.38 / 0.10
    MOUNTAIN   0.15  0.60  |  0.14  0.31    0.24      1.00  |  0.30 / 0.34
    TUNDRA     0.05  0.20  |  0.02  0.14    0.48      1.00  |  0.10 / 0.62
    BRIDGE      -    0.60  |  0.34  0.00    0.00      0.45  |  new
    TUNNEL      -     -    |  0.00  0.00    0.00       -    |  new, no sky

SEVEN OF THE NINE LAND WITHIN A FEW HUNDREDTHS OF THE NUMBERS ALREADY IN THE GAME, and that is the
strongest argument that this is a derivation rather than a rewrite. The values were hand-tuned one at
a time over weeks, and a two-axis model reproduces almost all of them.

TUNDRA IS THE ONE THAT REALLY MOVES, AND IT MOVES THE WAY THE OWNER ASKED. Snow falls from 0.62 to
0.14 and rain from 0.10 to 0.02, because a cold DRY place precipitates rarely - it is the ground cover
that makes it read as tundra, not constant snowfall. That is the owner's sentence turned into numbers:
"a lower chance for snow and an even lower chance for rain, but still a baseline coverage on the
ground". The derived floor is 0.48; the owner offered 0.2 as a guess. Keep it derived and tune the
0.60 coefficient if the ground reads too white - a number that falls out of the model can be moved in
one place, and a number typed into one biome cannot.

MOUNTAIN ALSO DROPS ITS RAIN, from 0.30 to 0.14, for the same reason: a cold place's precipitation is
mostly snow, and today's table has it doing both at once.

NEUTRAL IS A FLAG, NOT A MIDPOINT. City, tunnel and bridge state that they have no climate of their
own and may follow anything. That is what the owner meant by neutral and it is a different field from
`temp`. A city still needs weather numbers, so it keeps a temperate position for those - it is only
its ADJACENCY that is unconstrained. Canyon is given real numbers rather than the flag: a canyon is
genuinely an arid landform, and desert and mountain are the neighbours it should have.

A TUNNEL HAS NO WEATHER AT ALL, which falls out for free rather than needing a rule. There is no sky
above it, so rain 0 and snow 0 are facts about a roof rather than a climate. What happens to weather
already falling when you enter one is a real question and is left to [[RLG-105]].

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE. `hill` and `bend` are untouched - they are terrain rather than climate, and
nothing in the owner's request bears on them. And `cover` stays stated rather than derived for the
same reason: a forest is closed in by trees at every temperature.

NOT BUILT. This is the table the owner asked to see. Nothing in `BIOMES` has been changed, and the
adjacency rule this table exists to serve is still unbuilt as well.
- **RULED 2026-08-31T11:13:11-04:00** — OWNER, 2026-08-31, adjusting the table above: "truly neutral biomes should have an adjustable temp
each time that instance of that biome is generated, and instead of having a separate rain and snow
chance we could just call it precipitation and it's based off of the temperature. So we have a
temperature and a precipitation chance - we derive the whole table with that in mind."

THIS IS BETTER THAN WHAT IT REPLACES, AND IT REMOVES A FIELD RATHER THAN ADDING ONE. `rain` and `snow`
stop being stored at all. A place states how OFTEN it precipitates and how WARM it is, and what falls
out of the sky follows from the two. Two numbers per place, down from two probabilities plus a damp
axis.

AND IT DELETES AN IMPOSSIBLE STATE THAT EXISTS TODAY. MOUNTAIN currently declares rain 0.30 AND snow
0.34 - two independent rolls that sum to 0.64, so the place is asked twice whether it has weather. One
precipitation chance asked once, then split by temperature, cannot express that and does not need to.

THE MODEL, COMPLETE:

    stated per place    temp        where its temperature sits, 0 coldest to 1 hottest
                        tempVary    how far a given instance may roll either side of it
                        precip      how often it precipitates at all
                        cover       the open-sky multiplier, which is about shape not climate

    rolled per instance temp        temp + rnd(-tempVary, +tempVary), once, when the place opens

    derived             snowShare = clamp((0.50 - temp) / 0.50, 0, 1)
                        snow      = precip * snowShare
                        rain      = precip * (1 - snowShare)
                        snowFloor = clamp((0.25 - temp) / 0.25, 0, 1) * 0.60
                        cloudBase = precip * 1.6 * cover

`cover` NOW DERIVES FROM ONE NUMBER INSTEAD OF TWO. It was `(rain + snow) * 1.6`, and `rain + snow` is
exactly `precip`. So the cloud calculation gets simpler as a side effect rather than needing a change.

THE TABLE, RE-DERIVED:

    SWAMP      0.80  0.08   0.64  |  0.64  0.00   0.00   1.00

    CITY       0.45  0.45   0.38  |  varies with the roll   1.00

WHAT A ROLLED TEMPERATURE ACTUALLY BUYS, shown on the city, which is the case the owner named:

    this city rolls   temp 0.05  |  rain 0.03  snow 0.35  floor 0.48    a city in the cold
    this city rolls   temp 0.45  |  rain 0.34  snow 0.04  floor 0.00    a temperate city
    this city rolls   temp 0.85  |  rain 0.38  snow 0.00  floor 0.00    a city in the heat

One entry in the table, three places to drive through, and nothing in the code names any of them.

AND THE `neutral` FLAG DISAPPEARS, WHICH IS THE BEST PART OF THIS. The previous entry proposed a
boolean saying "this place may follow anything". It is not needed: `tempVary` says the same thing
better and by degrees. A place with a narrow range - tundra, desert - can only sit near places of its
own temperature. A place with a wide one - city, tunnel, bridge - can sit anywhere, because it can
roll a temperature that fits wherever it lands. Neutrality stops being a special case and becomes a
number, which is the shape everything else in this table already has.

THE STRONGER VERSION, AND IT IS ONE LINE FURTHER. If a neutral place rolls its temperature TOWARD the
place it is following rather than uniformly within its range, then neutral places become the
connective tissue of the world: a city between a desert and a mountain rolls cool, and the crossing
either side of it is short in temperature terms. That is [[RLG-109]]'s adjacency problem partly solving
itself - the awkward transitions get a place to happen in.

WHERE THE ROLLED VALUE HAS TO LIVE. `BIOMES` becomes a table of RECIPES rather than of places: what a
city is like in general. The instance - this city, with its rolled temperature and the rain, snow and
floor that follow - has to be stored where the current place is stored, and computed once when the
place opens rather than per frame. `openBiome` already exists and already rolls the sea's side per
instance, so there is a precedent and a home for it.

AND THAT IS A REAL REFACTOR, NOT A TABLE EDIT. `B.rain` and `B.snow` are read by `rollWeather`,
`rollSky`, `weatherTaper` and `biome-test`, and every one of them currently reads the RECIPE. They all
have to read the INSTANCE instead, or a place will roll cold and rain anyway. That is the part of this
that can go quietly wrong, and it is the reason to build it as its own unit with the biome checks
watched failing first.

STILL NOT BUILT. Nothing in `BIOMES` has changed.
