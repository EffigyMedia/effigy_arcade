### RLG-082 — The anchored relative spacing of UI elements
- **RULED 2026-08-30T08:55:05-04:00** — REQUESTED, NOT STARTED. Owner, 2026-08-30, mid-session and explicitly for later: "fix the anchored
relative spacing of UI elements."

WHAT IS BEING SAID, as far as the sentence itself carries. The elements of the interface are placed
against anchors - the top of the stage, the safe area, the bottom of the glass - and the SPACING
between them relative to those anchors is wrong. That is a layout fault, not a taste request: the
word is "fix".

WHAT IS NOT KNOWN AND MUST BE ASKED BEFORE ANY OF IT IS BUILT: which elements, on which screens, and
on which machines. Four cabinets and a launcher each have their own furniture, the shell owns the
room (`--stage-h`, `--safe-top`) and each machine paints inside it, and a fix in the wrong one of
those two places is the fault this project has already recorded: a machine that defines `--stage-h`
in its own `:root` silently discards the shell's calculation, because the shell's stylesheet is
appended during parse and a later `:root` wins on source order.

THE OWNER SHOULD BE ASKED FOR A CAPTURE, or for the screen's name. A description of a spacing fault
is the class of report that has already cost this project three wrong fixes in a row on the mirror's
eye height, where each fix was made from a written description and the picture settled it in one
look.

RELATED BUT NOT THE SAME: [[RLG-027]] is menu polish, and its scope has never been agreed either.
If the answer to "which screens" turns out to be the menus, these two are one job.

DO NOT START THIS. It is recorded because the owner said to record it.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T11:52:14-04:00** — THE SURFACE IS THE DRIVING HUD, on the owner's answer to the question this fragment said to ask.
Which ELEMENTS was still not stated, so the first thing done was to measure the HUD at three
viewports and photograph it, rather than to guess.

AND THE PICTURE ANSWERED IT. At 390 wide the HUD is right. At 820 the wheel, the pedals, the bottle
and the dials are half as big again - they scale with `--ark-ui`, which the shell publishes and which
runs 1 to 1.8 - while TIME and DISTANCE are the same 26 pixels they are on a phone, stranded in
corners that have moved two hundred pixels further out. That is precisely "the anchored relative
spacing of UI elements": the readouts are anchored to corners that move and sized in a space that
does not.

THE SHELL ALREADY SAID SO. `uiScale`'s own note: "Games opt in by scaling their own overlay by it -
the shell cannot do it for them without knowing what is a control and what is the game itself." This
cabinet opted its controls in and left its readouts behind. The fault is half a job, not a wrong
number.

NOTHING WAS RESIZED. Every figure is the size it already was multiplied by the scale everything
beside it already used, so a phone at scale 1 is unchanged to the pixel and only a larger screen
moves. That is the test of this kind of fix: if the phone changes, the fix was a redesign.

ONE THING IS DELIBERATELY NOT SCALED, and it is the exception that shows the rule. The top row clears
the rear-view mirror by 58 pixels, and the mirror is drawn on the CANVAS at a fixed 44 tall - it does
not grow with the interface. So that clearance is measured against the glass rather than against the
scale, and scaling it would have opened a gap that grows for no reason. An anchored distance follows
what it is anchored TO.

MEASURED: a readout is 26.0px at scale 1 and 46.8px at scale 1.8, where 46.8 is 26 times 1.8.
Falsified by taking the scale off one rule: 26.0 and 26.0.

AND ONE THING SEEN IN THE SAME CAPTURES AND NOT FIXED: at 820 by 1180 the pedal pads run off the
bottom edge of the frame. The cluster's scale is chosen from the SHORT edge and nothing caps it
against the available height, so on a tall narrow window the controls grow past the floor. That is a
different decision - it is about the cluster rather than the readouts, and capping a scale changes
how the controls feel on every large screen - so it is recorded here rather than done. The capture is
what makes it obvious, which is the second time today a capture has found the thing next to the thing
being fixed.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T16:35:35-04:00** — A BIGGER MIRROR, AND IT WAS NEVER CENTRED. Owner, 2026-08-30: "make the rearview mirror larger and
centered on the top of the screen."

THE REASON IT WAS OFF-CENTRE LOOKED DELIBERATE, which is why it is worth recording. `viewShift` moves
the whole forward view left to make room for the thumb cluster in the bottom right, and the mirror's
x was computed from it - so the glass sat 8.5 per cent of the screen left of the middle, hanging off
a windscreen that is symmetrical. A mirror is mounted on the SCREEN rather than in the world: it does
not move when the view does. Dropping `viewShift` from its x is the whole of the centring.

LARGER AT THE SAME SHAPE. 0.62 of the width capped at 250 becomes 0.80 capped at 340, and the height
is derived from the width at the 5.68:1 the glass already had rather than being a second number that
has to be kept in step.

AND THE HUD MEASURES ITSELF AGAINST THE GLASS NOW, which is the part that generalises. The row under
the mirror cleared it with a hardcoded 58 - six of margin, a 44-pixel mirror and eight of gap, three
numbers collapsed into one, in a different file from the two that decide them. So a bigger mirror
would have silently overlapped the readouts. The engine publishes `--mirror-h` and `--mirror-top`
when the size changes, and both driving games read them.

ONE MORE THING HAD TO MOVE FOR THAT TO BE MEASURABLE. The HUD's own top padding was safe-area plus
twelve, and the row's clearance was measured from THERE - so the gap between the glass and the text
was the sum of two paddings and could not be asserted. The HUD's top padding is the safe area alone
now and the row owns the whole distance from the top of the stage. The gap is then exactly what it is
designed to be: 9.8 pixels at a UI scale of 1.231, where 8 times the scale is 9.85.

FALSIFIED BY PUTTING THE HARDCODED 58 BACK: the gap reads MINUS eight - the readouts overlapping the
bottom of the glass - and the check fails. Worth noting that a looser bound would not have caught it:
the first version of this check allowed any gap from 0 to 40 and passed the old number, which is the
failure mode of a comfortable tolerance. It agrees with whatever is there.

AND THE HARNESS STOPPED SLEEPING. The bottle-collapse check waited a fixed 600ms for the dials to
finish a 0.18-second transition and still failed about one run in eight. It waits for the position to
STOP MOVING now, which is the thing it actually needs to be true. Five consecutive runs, all 46.8 px.
