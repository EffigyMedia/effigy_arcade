### UNT-141 - The deck runs to the horizon instead of stopping in mid-water

- Routing class: `feature` -> **claude-sonnet-5**, effort **medium**. Bias 1. Implementation to a spec that already answers the design questions. ([[RLG-092]])
- Opened for: [[RLG-112]]
- Opened by `unit.py`. **The identity exists before the first edit** ([[RLG-097]]), so the snapshot has somewhere to go.
- **CLOSED 2026-09-01T14:06:00-04:00** - The deck runs to the skyline instead of stopping in mid-water. It is the same gap RLG-101 is about and it has always been there - the road stops 12.6 pixels short of the vanishing point - but on land the far field fills it with more ground and nobody reads it as an end, where on a bridge the STRUCTURE stops in the middle of a stretch of water. The technique is RLG-093's: no extrapolation and no second construction, just the same walk asking proj the same questions in the same steps, so a shorter deck is the failure mode rather than a wrong one. The tarmac, the kerb and both rails are carried; the lane markings are not, because a dashed line has to keep its phase. The tarmac's colour is one function with two callers now, because the join is exactly where two derivations stop agreeing. THE CHECK READS THE WALK RATHER THAN THE PIXELS and that was decided after trying the other way: the skyline's own headlands are dark silhouettes sitting exactly where the deck's tip is, and a scan of the vanishing column read 353, 356, 358 and 397 on four runs of one unchanged build. It closes 8.4 pixels by sampling and the rest by construction, because a road converging on a vanishing point is a point there. Green on bridge-test, drive-test and smoke-test.
