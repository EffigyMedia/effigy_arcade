### RLG-094 — The skyline was a different city every six seconds
- **RULED 2026-08-31T07:32:10-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31, twice, correcting the scope the second time: the skyline parallax jitters and
pops, in the FORWARD view and the REAR-VIEW.

IT IS NOT A PARALLAX FAULT, AND THE WORD "PARALLAX" IN THE REPORT IS WHY IT SURVIVED SO LONG. Two
sessions had already smoothed the offset: `skySmooth` chases `bendPx(pos + 30000)` frame to frame,
after an earlier version teleported the city every time `curvatureAt()` stepped from one segment to
the next. Both of those were real fixes to a real stepping problem, and neither could touch this,
because the city was not moving wrongly. It was being rebuilt as a DIFFERENT CITY.

THE MECHANISM, and it is two correct decisions meeting. `buildSkyline()` generated the plan - where
every building, peak and tree stands, how wide, how tall, and which of a tower's windows exist -
from `Math.random()`, inline, every time it ran. That was harmless while the sprite was built once.
Then RLG-080 made the silhouette take the hour's light, and it did so the right way: the tint is
mixed in at BUILD time, because a tint pass over the frame had already been tried and painted a band
across the sky. Baking the colour means the sprite is a sprite of one HOUR as well as of one place,
so it is keyed by an hour bucket - one fortieth of a 240-second day.

Which means `buildSkyline()` runs EVERY SIX SECONDS. Ten times a minute, for as long as the game is
running, the entire horizon was replaced by a different horizon - and in both views at the same
instant, because the windscreen and the mirror draw from the same cache. Neither ruling is wrong on
its own. The fault is that they share a function.

WHAT THE PLACE LOOKS LIKE AND WHAT COLOUR IT IS AT THIS MOMENT ARE NOT THE SAME THING. The first
belongs to the biome and must never change while the car is in it; the second belongs to the hour
and must change all day. They were fused in one function, so the only way to get a new colour was to
get a new city. `skylinePlanFor` holds the plan, cached per biome and built once; `buildSkyline`
paints that plan in the current hour's colours. RLG-080 is untouched - the tint is still baked, and
there is still no wash over the frame.

SPLITTING THEM IS WHAT MAKES THE FAULT UNREPEATABLE RATHER THAN FIXED. The shapes now have nowhere
to come from except the cached plan, so a later change to the painting cannot reach them by
accident. That is the whole reason for a second function rather than a seeded random inside the
first: a seed can be forgotten, a structure cannot.

AND A WINDOW'S COLOUR WENT WITH IT. Whether a lit window burns amber or cold blue was rolled at
PAINT time, so it was re-rolled with every rebuild - a lit city changed the colour of every window
it had, ten times a minute. The roll moved into the plan, beside the window it belongs to.

MEASURED. `tools/skyline-test.py` walks ten hours of the day on one pinned biome and reads the
silhouette as a PROFILE - the topmost opaque row in each of the sprite's 1024 columns, which is the
outline a player sees against the sky, rather than a pixel count two different cities could share.
Zero of 1024 columns move across the whole day. The colour travels 85 to 92, from rgb(42,27,44) to
rgb(52,33,48).

THE CHECK ASSERTS THE COLOUR STILL MOVES, and that is not a courtesy. Without it, "never rebuild the
sprite" passes every shape assertion in the file and is the exact fault RLG-080 was raised to
remove - a skyline holding the colour of whatever moment it was first drawn at, through dusk,
sunset and midnight.

AND IT ASSERTS THE RUN CROSSED SEVERAL HOUR BUCKETS, which is the difference between this file and
one that passes on both builds. The fault does not exist inside a bucket: the broken engine returns
a cached sprite there and is perfectly stable. A check that sampled twice in six seconds would have
gone green on the defect.

FALSIFIED BY REINTRODUCING THE DEFECT, NOT BY REVERTING THE ENGINE. Reverting cannot falsify this
file, because the instrument that reads the silhouette does not exist on the broken build - the run
dies with `R.skyBucket is not a function`, which is not a check going red. `API.forgetSkylinePlans()`
drops the plan cache between samples, which is precisely what the old engine did on every rebuild.
With it, 1024 of 1024 columns move.

WHAT IS NOT SETTLED. The owner's report said "jittering AND popping". This is the popping, and it is
certain. Whether a jitter remains in the forward view's chased offset is NOT measured here and is not
claimed to be fixed. `API.skyTrace()` - the trace hook UNT-088 left behind before it was abandoned -
is the instrument for that question and it has never been read.

AND THE DYNAMIC WINDOW LIGHTS THE OWNER MISSES WERE THIS BUG. Reported the same day: "the city
skyline doesn't have dynamic window lights like it used to." The design has only ever drawn ONE
window sheet at ONE global alpha following the clock, so no window has ever switched on by itself.
What was moving was the sheet being regenerated every six seconds with new window positions and new
colours - the defect, seen from the side where it happened to look good. The liveliness was real and
worth keeping; its source was not. See [[RLG-095]].
