### RLG-128 — Scenery in the mirror faded in as it receded, which is inside out
- **RULED 2026-08-31T19:42:11-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: "the scenery that moves away from the car in the rearview mirror, I think that alpha fades inverted from how it should. It looks like it starts alpha and then becomes opaque as it moves away, and it should be an inversion of the forward view where the alpha is at the outer bounds of the vision - so in the rearview mirror the scenery should be opaque and become alpha as it reaches the draw distance."

EXACTLY RIGHT, AND THE CAUSE IS ONE EXPRESSION. The fade was driven by the object's WIDTH:

    const mFade = Math.min(1, Math.max(0, (mCap - mw2) / (mCap * 0.20)));

A NEAR object is BIG, so `mCap - mw2` goes to zero and it drew nearly transparent. A FAR object is SMALL, so the ratio clamps to fully opaque. And in a mirror everything only ever RECEDES - you cannot approach a thing behind you - so every tree faded IN as it went away. Backwards twice over: the opposite of the windscreen, and the opposite of what air does.

THIS EXACT MISTAKE WAS MADE ONCE BEFORE IN THE FORWARD VIEW, and the note left on the lamp posts records it word for word: "`globalAlpha = fade` made the whole post see-through for most of its life, since `fade` is the distance ramp and only reaches 1 right in front of you. A steel column is not translucent at any distance. The ramp is now only used to fade a post IN at the far edge of the draw distance, where it would otherwise pop."

So the mirror uses the windscreen's own shape now, the same ramp and the same factor of four - `Math.min(1, clamp(1 - away, 0, 1) * 4)`. Solid everywhere, easing away only over the last quarter of the reach. `mAway` is 0 just behind the car and 1 at the draw edge, which is the forward `fade` measured backwards, because that is what a mirror is.

WHAT THE OLD RAMP WAS RIGHT ABOUT IS KEPT. Anything wider than `mCap` is culled outright so a tree cannot fill a pane 44 pixels tall, and without an ease an object that has just receded under that cap POPS IN at a fifth of the glass. The arrival band is 6 per cent of the cap now rather than 20: long enough that it arrives, short enough that it is never the see-through object the owner saw.

MEASURED: 0.995 alpha in the near third of the glass and 0.315 at the draw edge. With the old expression restored, 0.986 near and 1.000 at the edge - solid at the horizon, which is the complaint stated as a number.

AND THE CHECK TOOK THREE TRIES, WHICH IS WORTH RECORDING BECAUSE THE FIRST TWO LOOKED FINE. Asserting "the near objects are solid" over a distance bucket passed WITH THE FAULT PRESENT at 0.986, because only the handful of objects sitting just under the size cap were transparent and the bucket drowned them. Narrowing to the WIDEST objects then failed on the FIXED build at 0.041 - and correctly, because the widest object on the road is the one that has just arrived under the cap and is deliberately faint. Asking it to be solid asserts against the arrival ease rather than against the fault.

WHAT DISCRIMINATES IS THE FAR END. There must BE a fade at the draw distance, and the far end must be dimmer than the near end. Both fail with the fault and pass without it, and neither can be satisfied by the arrival ease.

`MIRROR_BACK` is named now rather than being 34,000 written in three places that all have to agree - where the glass starts its walk, where its horizon band is measured, and where the scenery fades away.

NOT VERIFIED FROM HERE: whether a quarter of the reach is the right length to fade over. It is the windscreen's own number, which is the best available argument for it, and only the device can say whether it reads the same in a pane this small.
