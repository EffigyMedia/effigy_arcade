### RLG-058 — A vehicle's collider must match the sprite you can see
- **RULED 2026-08-28T22:49:23-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28:**

> "We have to make the vehicle colliders true to their sprite size. It's hard to tell."

**"It's hard to tell" is the symptom and it is the part to design against.** The complaint is not
that a number is wrong in the abstract - it is that the player cannot predict a hit from what is on
the screen. Anything that ends with the drawn car and the hit agreeing is a fix; anything that only
makes the numbers tidier is not.

**What is actually there today, read rather than assumed.**

| | drawn at | collides at |
|---|---|---|
| traffic | `c.w`, 0.26 to 0.32 by body | the same `c.w` |
| a rival | `r.w`, 0.265 for every one of them | the same `r.w` |
| **the player** | **0.265**, hard-coded in `drawPlayer` | **0.26**, hard-coded at every hit test |

So the lateral half is already sprite-true for everything except the player, and the player is out
by 2% - which is small, and is NOT nothing when the ruling is that the hit should match the picture.

**Two things are worse than that 2%, and they are probably what the owner is feeling.**

1. **The player's collider is one number for all six cars.** A MATADOR and a TUNER are visibly
   different widths on the screen and hit identically. That is the same fault [[RLG-042]] found in
   the gearbox - a shared constant standing in for a per-car property - and the same ruling applies:
   the car is what it is, and the number should come from the car.
2. **Length has no sprite to be true to.** `c.len` is 380 to 520 by body type and the player's is
   the constant 380, and NONE of them is derived from anything drawn. A billboard sprite has a width
   and a height on the screen and no depth at all, so "true to the sprite" cannot be read literally
   for the z axis. **This needs the owner's ruling**: a car's on-road length is a real thing the
   player can see from behind, and the honest options are to derive it from the body's real
   proportions or to keep it as a per-car figure in the fleet table - which is [[RLG-055]]'s
   territory, and would land there naturally.

**A hit test between two cars uses the sum of the two half-widths**, which is geometrically right
and should stay. The `+ 0.05` margins in `laneClear` and `laneSpeed` are the AI's judgment of a safe
gap, not a collider, and are not in scope here.

**Not scope, and worth saying because it is the obvious next thought:** giving the cars real
rectangles, rotation or per-corner contact. The road is drawn from billboards and the collision is
an axis-aligned overlap on two numbers. Making the two numbers honest is this ruling; replacing the
model is not.

**Measure it the way [[RLG-040]] was measured.** The instrument is a harness that drives at a known
lateral offset and reports the offset at which a hit is registered, against the sprite's drawn width
at the same distance - `API.spriteWidthAt` already exists and returns exactly that. A number for
"the hit happens N% wider than the car looks" is what tells anyone whether this is fixed.
- **RULED 2026-08-30T11:13:29-04:00** — ONE WIDTH, AND IT IS THE ONE YOU CAN SEE. The player was DRAWN at 0.265 of the road and COLLIDED at
0.26, hard-coded separately at three hit tests - so the car was struck about two per cent narrower
than it looks, and each of the three had to be remembered independently. The drawn width wins,
because the picture is what the player is predicting from, and "it's hard to tell" is a complaint
about prediction rather than about a number.

A FOURTH AND A FIFTH SITE TURNED UP WHILE DOING IT. `API.spriteWidthAt` - the very function this
ruling names as the reference for "how wide is the car drawn" - carried its own copy of 0.265, and so
did the tyre marks the car lays under braking. Both read the constant now. That is five places one
number was written out, which is the shape of the fault rather than an aside.

THE MEASUREMENT IS THE PART WORTH KEEPING, and it took three attempts to make it mean anything.

FIRST IT MEASURED THE INVULNERABILITY WINDOW. Walking the player outward in two hundred small steps
found an edge at 0.025 against an expected 0.223: a hit sets nine tenths of a second of immunity, so
every step after the first read as a miss and the walk "found" the edge immediately. Parking a car
clears that window now, and a binary search costs fourteen readings instead of two hundred.

THEN IT MEASURED A FULL DAMAGE GAUGE. With the sedan's search run first, every truck reading came
back a miss and the search converged on zero - because damage caps at 100 and the panel was already
full. The gauge is cleared before each staged collision.

AND THEN IT WAS STILL CIRCULAR, WHICH IS THE ONE THAT MATTERED. The edge is the SUM of two
half-widths, so measuring against one car proves only the sum, and a check that reads `PLAYER_W` back
out would pass on any value at all. TWO cars of known and different widths separate the sum into its
parts: a truck at 0.320 collides at 0.2416 and a coupe at 0.260 at 0.2169, and the difference pins
the scale, which turns either edge into the player's own width. 0.2648 measured against 0.2650 drawn.

FALSIFIED AT THE TOLERANCE IT NEEDS. Put the old 0.26 back in the traffic hit test and the same
measurement reports 0.2597 and fails. The bound is 0.002 rather than something comfortable, precisely
because the defect being guarded against is a gap of 0.005.

NOT DONE, AND BOTH ARE THE OWNER'S. Every player car is still drawn at one width, so a lorry and a
roadster are the same width in your hands while traffic already varies 0.26 to 0.32 by body - that is
twenty-six numbers and it belongs with the fleet table in [[RLG-055]]. And length has no sprite to be
true to, because a billboard has width and height and no depth at all.
