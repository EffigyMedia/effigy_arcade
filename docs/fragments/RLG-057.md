### RLG-057 — The sky carries the weather: cloud cover from clear to overcast, and storm and snow skies
- **RULED 2026-08-28T22:43:53-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-28, filed to the queue and NOT started:**

> "Matching sky weather effects - storm clouds and snow clouds. Along with normal clouds too that
> are transparent enough to let the moon or sun kind of bleed through, but sometimes opaque too. So
> sometimes crystal clear skies, and sometimes heavily overcast - the whole range in between, with
> weather based effect modifiers."

**Three things are being asked for and they are worth keeping apart.**

1. **Cloud cover is a RANGE, not a state.** Crystal clear at one end, heavy overcast at the other,
   and everything between. That rules out a set of three sky presets with names.
2. **Cloud is translucent, and sometimes it is not.** The sun or the moon bleeds through thin cover
   and is hidden by thick cover. So the light source and the cloud layer have to be drawn in a
   relationship rather than as two independent layers, and cover has to modulate what the sky body
   contributes to the scene.
3. **The sky matches the weather on the road.** Storm cloud with rain, snow cloud with snow. The
   engine already has `wet`, `snowy` and `settle`, and [[RLG-017]] already ruled that weather ends
   with the biome - so the sky is a consumer of a weather state that exists, not a new system with
   its own opinion.

**"Weather based effect modifiers" is the part to put back to the owner before anything is built.**
It could mean the sky's own look changes with the weather, which is the sentence above and needs no
ruling. It could also mean the weather changes the DRIVING - grip, visibility, draw distance - which
is a different feature, touches [[RLG-048]]'s wet-slip ruling, and is not implied by a sentence about
clouds. Ask which.

**Not scheduled.** This is polish and the owner filed it as such. It sits behind the ruling set
already in flight.

**Read before starting:** the day cycle is four minutes and owns dusk, night, dawn and midday, and
[[RLG-051]] added a TIME control that sets where it starts. A cloud layer that ignores the phase will
fight it. And [[RLG-041]] is open - a translucent moving layer over distant vehicles is exactly the
sort of thing that gets blamed for a rendering pop, or hides one.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T13:42:49-04:00** — BUILT. The sky has cover, and the cover is a range.

`cloud` runs from clear to overcast and is a WEATHER VARIABLE rather than a consequence of rain. That
distinction is the whole ruling: an overcast day with no rain in it is the commonest sky there is,
and a sky that only goes grey when it is raining reads as a filter that comes on with the drops. Rain
and snow RAISE the cover - it does not rain out of a clear sky - and it drifts on its own the rest of
the time, on a roll against the biome, so a desert is usually clear and a tundra usually is not.

`storm` is how BLACK the cover is, and it follows the rain rather than the cloud. That is what
separates a storm from an overcast afternoon, and it is why heavy rain is dark while heavy snow is
bright: snow falls out of a pale sky.

THE SUN AND THE MOON BLEED THROUGH because the cover is drawn AFTER them. A body behind cloud is not
switched off; it is a bright patch with grey over it, and how much of it survives is how heavy the
cover is. Drawing the cloud first and the bodies second would have been easier and would have put a
sun on top of an overcast sky.

LIGHTNING AND THUNDER, which is the rest of RLG-060. A strike belongs to a storm and nothing else -
heavy rain under heavy cover, and never in snow. The flash is two or three strikes in quick
succession rather than one clean pulse, because a single white frame reads as a rendering fault, and
it lights the CLOUD rather than the ground: what a driver sees is the sky going white for a moment.

Thunder follows at a distance of 0.8 to 3.4 seconds, which is the part that makes it weather rather
than a light. It is three overlapping beds of low noise with long tails - a single burst reads as a
door slamming - and the crack only survives on a near strike, because distance eats the high end
first. NOT VERIFIED BY EAR: a harness cannot listen. The owner has to say whether it lands.

The instrument is `sky()`, `setSky()` and `strike()`, so a storm can be photographed rather than
waited for.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T13:52:16-04:00** — SETTLED SNOW IS TWO LAYERS NOW, not one slab. Owner, 2026-08-29: the cover was a single overlay
filling everything from the horizon to the bottom of the screen at one strength, and it looked wrong
because snow does not lie like that.

What you see from a car is two different things, and they behave differently:

  THE FAR FIELD is a bright band under the horizon, where the ground is nearly edge-on and every
  flake you can see is stacked into one surface. It is the brightest part of a snowy landscape and it
  fades DOWNWARD as the ground turns to face you.

  THE GROUND IN FRONT OF YOU is seen from above at a shallow angle, so the snow on it reads as a wash
  rather than as a wall. Strongest at the bottom of the screen, fading UPWARD.

THEY OVERLAP ACROSS MOST OF THE PLANE, and that took a second pass. The first attempt had the far
band stop where the near one started and the join was a visible line across the grass - the same
fault as the single slab, moved down the screen. Each now runs well into the other's half and reaches
zero inside it, so there is nowhere for an edge to be.

The verge itself was always right: it is whitened per segment where the road is drawn, so it follows
the geometry. These two layers are the atmosphere over it.

`setSnow()` forces both, so the cover can be photographed rather than driven to.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T13:58:53-04:00** — SNOW ACCUMULATES NOW. Owner, 2026-08-29: "a snow coverage increase where over time it gets more and
more slick and the white overlay becomes more and more opaque."

`settle` used to CHASE the fall - it eased toward `wet` and stopped there - so a light flurry could
never whiten the ground however long it lasted, and a heavy one reached its ceiling in a few seconds
and then held. That is a level rather than an accumulation, and it is why the ground looked the same
after ten minutes of snow as after ten seconds of it.

It integrates now. How fast it builds is how hard it is falling; where it stops is the ground being
covered, not the storm's intensity. A long light fall and a short heavy one arrive at the same white
by different roads, which is what snow does. Measured under heavy snow: 0.145 after five seconds,
0.417 at fifteen, 0.96 at thirty-five - about forty seconds to cover the ground completely, and three
or four minutes for a flurry to do the same. That difference is what a driver should feel between
weather passing through and weather setting in.

MELT IS NOT THE SAME NUMBER. Snow lies: with nothing falling it goes at a twentieth of the rate it
arrived. What takes it away quickly is the place turning warm - `settleMelt`, set when the biome
underneath can no longer hold it - because driving out of a tundra into a desert should not leave the
ground white for a minute and a half.

AND DEEP SNOW IS SLICKER THAN SNOW. Settled cover now costs more grip than the fall does: a road with
an inch on it is the hazard, and the flakes in the air are only how it got there. Measured across the
same run, grip fell from 0.578 to 0.340 as the ground whitened.

THE FLOOR IS DELIBERATE, at a third of dry grip. Below that a car does not feel slippery, it feels
broken, and weather should change how you drive rather than take the car away.

THE OVERLAY FOLLOWS FOR FREE, which is the point of having built the two layers first: both read
`settle`, so the far field and the near wash both deepen as the ground covers, without either
knowing anything about weather.

WHY THAT IS PROBABLY THE FAULT, and it is a reading rather than a finding: the owner said one layer
should FOLLOW THE GROUND LAYER. A gradient over the whole screen does not follow anything - it is the
same slab with a soft edge on it. Following the ground means being drawn WITH the ground: the verge
polygons the road pass already emits, per segment, which is where `settle` already whitens the grass.
The second layer, "between the ground layer and under the horizon", then sounds like the far field
BEYOND the verge - the land between the end of the drawn road and the horizon line - which is not
drawn as geometry at all today.

WHAT TO DO FIRST, next session: ask the owner what is wrong with it rather than guessing again. Two
readings fit the same words and they lead to different work, and this fragment already carries one
wrong guess.

DO NOT ASSUME THE COLOUR OR THE OPACITY IS THE PROBLEM. The accumulation behind it - snow building
over time and the road getting slicker with it - was measured and is not what was rejected. The
rejection is the OVERLAY.
