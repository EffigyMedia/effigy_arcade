### RLG-151 — Stars belong to the clear night sky, and a tundra has a small chance of an aurora
- **RULED 2026-09-01T20:16:22-04:00** — Owner, 2026-09-01: "Can the clear night sky have stars? The tundra should have a small chance
to have an aurora borealis."

THE SKY ALREADY HAS STARS, WHICH CHANGES WHAT THE FIRST HALF IS ASKING. `drawSky` puts 40 of
them out once `nightFall` passes 0.25, each twinkling against `dist`. So the question is not
"add stars" - it is that the owner has been driving at night and has not been reading them as
stars. Three candidates, and they are all cheap to check before anything is built:

    THEY ARE NOT GATED ON THE CLOUD. `cloud` is drawn after them, so heavy cover does paint
    over them - but their own alpha does not know about it, so a clear night and a half
    overcast one put out the same forty. The owner's word is CLEAR, and that is the lever:
    stars should be a property of there being nothing in the way.

    FORTY IS FEW, and their placement is a lattice - `(i * 137.5) % W` across and `(i * 61) %
    (horizon * 0.72)` down. On a 480-pixel screen that is a regular scatter of single pixels,
    which reads as noise rather than as a sky.

    AND THE BLOOM SITS OVER THEM. The horizon glow is painted after the stars at 0.35 of its
    strength even at midnight, so the lower sky - where a driver is actually looking - is
    washed before the stars are seen.

MEASURE WHICH ONE IT IS BEFORE CHANGING ANY OF THEM. A capture at `--hour 0.25` with the cover
pinned clear, and the same with it pinned overcast, answers it in two pictures. RLG-041 is the
standing lesson about diagnosing a device report from a guess.

THE AURORA IS THE NEW THING, AND IT IS A MOMENT RATHER THAN A PLACE. A tundra is a place and it
is always there; an aurora is not, so it belongs with the things that are ROLLED for a stretch
rather than stated in the table - the same shape the sea's side, the crop's field and the
window's habit all have. One roll, held for a run of road, read by the painter.

    WHAT IT NEEDS FROM THE OWNER, and it is one question: how small is "a small chance"? A
    tundra is one place in eleven and a run passes through a handful of places, so a 1-in-10
    aurora is a thing a player might see once in twenty runs. That may be too rare to be worth
    building or exactly rare enough to be worth seeing - the owner's call, and RLG-142's
    temperature step was blocked on the same kind of question.

    AND IT IS A NIGHT THING ONLY. It has to ride `nightFall` the way the stars do, or a green
    curtain appears over a lit tundra at noon.

    IT SHOULD ALSO WANT A CLEAR SKY, for the same reason the stars do - which makes the two
    halves of this note one mechanism seen twice, and is the argument for doing them together.

WHERE IT GOES. `drawSky` after the stars and before the bloom, so the bloom washes it the way
it washes everything else at the horizon. The canyon's continuous skyline shows the shape a
band drawn across the sky takes; an aurora is that walk again with a colour ramp and a slow
drift, and [[RLG-057]] already holds the owner's ask for a sky that is a range rather than a
switch.
