### RLG-061 — Should vehicles be drawn further down the road?
- **RULED 2026-08-29T03:47:46-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-29, asked as a question rather than a ruling:**

> "I also feel like the cars should be drawn further away forward possibly?"

**The number, so the question can be answered rather than debated.** The road is drawn to
`DRAW * SEG` - 150 segments of 200 units, so 30,000 units. Against the engine's own scale that is
about **a sixth of a mile, or 280 metres**. A car appears out of nothing at that line, and on a
straight road at 190mph it arrives about five seconds later.

**So the instinct is sound and there is a measurement behind it:** a real sight line down a motorway
is a mile or more, and this one is a city block.

**Why it is a question and not just a bigger number.**

- **Cost.** Every extra segment is a slice painted, a bucket, and every sprite in it offered to the
  painter. This is the hot loop and it runs on a phone.
- **The draw edge is where the remaining pop lives.** [[RLG-041]] measured every surviving flicker at
  about 30,000 units, where the sprite bucket range runs to `DRAW+1` and the road pass walks to
  `DRAW`. Moving the edge moves that pop rather than removing it, unless the edge itself is fixed
  first.
- **A car at that distance is about one pixel wide.** `drawSprite` refuses anything under 1.2 pixels,
  so drawing further forward only helps if the sprite is legible when it arrives. Beyond some
  distance the answer is a haze band rather than a car.
- **The horizon already does work.** The haze and the skyline are drawn to hide the edge; a longer
  road changes how that reads.

**What would settle it, and it is cheap:** raise `DRAW`, measure the frame time on the device at
several values, and look at the far cars. Frame cost is a number this project can take; whether the
road reads better is the owner's call on a phone, and nothing here can answer it.

**Related and not the same:** the owner also said the flicker is not noticed in the rearview, while
adding that the eye is on the road ahead. The mirror has its own level-of-detail fade
([[RLG-020]]) and its own draw distance, so a fix forward does not automatically travel backward.
