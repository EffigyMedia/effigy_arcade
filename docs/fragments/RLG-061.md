### RLG-061 — Should vehicles be drawn further down the road?
- **RULED 2026-08-29T03:47:46-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-29, asked as a question rather than a ruling:**

> "I also feel like the cars should be drawn further away forward possibly?"

**The number, so the question can be answered rather than debated.** The road is drawn to
`DRAW * SEG` - 150 segments of 200 units, so 30,000 units. Against the engine's own scale that is
about **a sixth of a mile, or 280 metres**. A car appears out of nothing at that line, and on a
straight road at 190mph it arrives about five seconds later.

**So the instinct is sound and there is a measurement behind it:** a real sight line down a motorway
is a mile or more, and this one is a city block.

**Why it is a question and not just a bigger number.**

- **Cost.** Every extra segment is a slice painted, a bucket, and every sprite in it offered to the
  painter. This is the hot loop and it runs on a phone.
- **The draw edge is where the remaining pop lives.** [[RLG-041]] measured every surviving flicker at
  about 30,000 units, where the sprite bucket range runs to `DRAW+1` and the road pass walks to
  `DRAW`. Moving the edge moves that pop rather than removing it, unless the edge itself is fixed
  first.
- **A car at that distance is about one pixel wide.** `drawSprite` refuses anything under 1.2 pixels,
  so drawing further forward only helps if the sprite is legible when it arrives. Beyond some
  distance the answer is a haze band rather than a car.
- **The horizon already does work.** The haze and the skyline are drawn to hide the edge; a longer
  road changes how that reads.

**What would settle it, and it is cheap:** raise `DRAW`, measure the frame time on the device at
several values, and look at the far cars. Frame cost is a number this project can take; whether the
road reads better is the owner's call on a phone, and nothing here can answer it.

**Related and not the same:** the owner also said the flicker is not noticed in the rearview, while
adding that the eye is on the road ahead. The mirror has its own level-of-detail fade
([[RLG-020]]) and its own draw distance, so a fix forward does not automatically travel backward.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T04:34:53-04:00** — **MEASURED 2026-08-29, and it CORRECTS a claim I made twice without measuring it.**

The owner, from the device:

> "I still see the flicker and a lot of traffic pops in much closer than them being 1 pixel."

**The owner is right and I was wrong.** [[RLG-041]] and this fragment both say a car at the draw
distance is "about one pixel wide". I inferred that from the painter refusing anything under 1.2
pixels - which is the size at which a sprite STOPS being drawn, not the size at which one ARRIVES.
Those are different questions and I answered the wrong one.

**`API.spriteWidthAt` has been in the engine the whole time. Asked properly:**

| distance ahead | sprite width |
|---|---|
| 34,000 | 6.0 px |
| 32,000 | 6.3 px |
| **30,200 - the bucket edge, where a car may first be drawn** | **6.7 px** |
| 30,000 - the drawn road ends | 6.8 px |
| 26,000 | 7.8 px |
| 20,000 | 10.1 px |

**A car does not fade in at one pixel. It appears at nearly seven, on a 480-pixel-wide screen.**
That is a seventieth of the width of the picture arriving from nothing, and it is plainly visible -
which is what the owner is reporting.

**The projection is much flatter than I assumed**, and that is the reason: doubling the distance from
20,000 to 40,000 does not halve the width, because a sprite's scale is dominated by the road's
perspective rather than by raw distance. So drawing further forward buys less than it looks like it
should, and the pop at the edge is bigger than it looks like it should.

**WITHDRAWN: "a car there is about one pixel wide", and with it the argument that the draw-edge pop
in [[RLG-041]] is too small to matter.** It is 6.7 pixels and it matters.

**Two ways out, and they are not exclusive.**

1. **Fade a sprite in over the last stretch of the drawn road.** Cheap, no extra geometry, and it
   turns a pop into an arrival. It does nothing about how near the road ends.
2. **Draw the road further.** The owner's original instinct, still costed as a question above: more
   slices and more sprites per frame, on a phone.

**The fade should be tried first**, because it is the one that fixes the reported symptom rather than
the underlying distance, and because it makes the second one cheaper to judge: with a fade in place,
a shorter draw distance is far less objectionable, and the decision about `DRAW` can be made on how
far you can SEE rather than on how badly things arrive.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T05:20:54-04:00** — **THE FADE IS BUILT, in v0.9.19. The draw distance question is NOT answered and stays open.**

A sprite in the last sixth of the drawn road is faded in rather than switched on. Measured from the
engine's own numbers:

| distance ahead | sprite width | drawn at |
|---|---|---|
| 30,200 - the bucket edge | 6.7 px | **alpha 0** |
| 30,000 - the road ends | 6.8 px | alpha 0 |
| 28,000 | 7.2 px | alpha 0.42 |
| 26,000 | 7.8 px | alpha 0.83 |
| 25,200 and nearer | 8.0 px + | alpha 1 |

**So a car now arrives over about 4,800 units instead of appearing at a seventieth of the screen
width.** It costs one multiply per sprite and no geometry.

**This is the cheaper of the two ways out and it does not settle the other one.** The road still
ends at 30,000 units - about a sixth of a mile - and the owner's instinct that cars should be drawn
further forward is still unanswered. What the fade changes is the BASIS for answering it: with a pop
at the edge, the draw distance was being judged on how badly things arrived. Now it can be judged on
how far you can see, which is the question actually being asked.

**And the measurement above is the argument for judging it on a device rather than here.** The
projection is far flatter than raw distance suggests - 20,000 units gives 10.1 pixels and 34,000
still gives 6.0 - so pushing `DRAW` out adds slices and sprites every frame for a small change in
what is legible. Whether that reads as "further down the road" or merely as "more haze" is not
something any harness can report.
