### RLG-092 — The weather falls in the mirror too
- **RULED 2026-08-31T00:04:57-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: weather effects should be shown in the rear view - not the drops on the glass, but the rain and the snow themselves.

THE DISTINCTION IS THE WHOLE INSTRUCTION AND IT IS THE RIGHT ONE. A droplet on the lens belongs to the windscreen the player is looking through; it has no business in a picture of what is behind the car, because the mirror is a separate piece of glass and the drops on THAT would be a different effect nobody asked for. Falling precipitation is weather in the world, and the world is what the mirror shows.

IT FOLLOWS THE SAME RULING THE MIRROR ALREADY OBEYS. RLG-079 said the mirror must show the world state it is in - biome, road conditions, scenery - and everything in there asks the SAME functions the windscreen asks rather than similar ones. Rain and snow are the part of the world state that never got carried across, so this finishes that job rather than starting a new one.

AND THE PANE IS 44 PIXELS TALL, which is what makes it a design question rather than a copy. The forward view's rain is drawn for a 900-pixel screen; the same density and the same streak length in a strip that size would read as static rather than as weather. Expect the count and the length to need their own numbers, and expect the fps question to come with them - the mirror's scenery already carries a note about a forest costing four frames a second in there.

MEASURE IT THE WAY THE HOUR WAS MEASURED: the check should ask whether the mirror gets brighter or busier when the weather turns, against a dry frame of the same scene, rather than counting particles. A count is an implementation; a difference in the picture is the effect.
- **RULED 2026-08-31T02:09:40-04:00** — BUILT IN 0.10.17, AND THE SHAPE OF IT IS THE PART WORTH KEEPING. The particles came out of
`drawRain` and into `paintPrecip`, which takes a rectangle. The windscreen calls it with the whole
frame and the mirror calls it with the glass. That is the same move RLG-079 made for `skyStops`,
`groundBase` and `groundTone` - one definition and two callers - and it is made for the same reason:
a second copy of the rain is a thing that drifts out of step with the first, and nobody notices until
a screenshot shows two weathers.

EACH PANE OWNS ITS OWN PARTICLES, and that is not an optimisation. A particle carries its position
and the painter advances it, so a single shared field would be stepped twice a frame and fall at
double the rate the moment the mirror was drawn. Two arrays, one painter.

THE LEAN FLIPS, AND IT IS THE ONE THING THE MIRROR DOES DIFFERENTLY ON PURPOSE. Rain falls straight
down; the streak leans because the CAR is moving through it. Look the other way down the same road
and it leans the other way. Everything else the mirror does differently, it does because the pane is
small.

THE THREE NUMBERS THAT ARE THE MIRROR'S OWN. `MIRROR_RAIN_N` at 20, `MIRROR_RAIN_LEN` at 0.17 of the
pane's height, `MIRROR_RAIN_FALL` at 2.4 times the forward view's rate. The glass measures 340 by 60
at a 480-pixel viewport, so the forward view's ninety particles put one every twelve pixels, and its
streak of 3.5 per cent of a 900-pixel screen is two pixels long in there. Everything horizontal - the
flake radius, the stroke width, the sideways drift - was already written against the pane's own
width, so it follows the glass with no knob of its own.

THE FALL RATE IS A JUDGEMENT AND IT IS WORTH SAYING WHY. Rain in a mirror is at the same angular rate
as rain in a windscreen, so the physically true pixel speed is the forward view's: 63 pixels a frame
at speed, across a pane 60 pixels tall. That crosses the glass in under one frame and is invisible.
2.4 is a compromise - fast enough to read as falling, slow enough to be seen at all - and it is a
tunable rather than an expression for exactly that reason.

AND THE CHECK DOES NOT MEASURE THE OBVIOUS THING. "The mirror looks different when it rains" was
ALREADY TRUE before this was built: RLG-079 wired the wet tarmac and the settled snow into the shared
surface code, so that check would have passed on the old engine and reported a feature that was not
there. `tools/mirror-rain-test.py` compares particles against NO particles in one and the same wet
scene. `API.mirrorRain({n: 0})` removes the feature through the public interface - zero means off,
deliberately - so the harness can run the identical assertion with the weather switched out and
require it to go red. It does: 0.0000 per cent of the pane against a 0.400 per cent threshold, with
rain lighting 1.77 per cent and snow 0.89 per cent.

THE INSTRUMENT CAUGHT TWO OF ITS OWN FAULTS AND BOTH LOOKED LIKE THE FEATURE WORKING.

  Standing water fills for as long as it rains, at about 0.054 a second, and a wet road is BRIGHTER
  in the distance because it reflects the sky. So a road still filling gets brighter between two
  samples all on its own. That read as 12.5 per cent of the pane - three times what the rain itself
  lights. `pool` and `settle` are pinned at their ceiling now, where neither can move.

  And PARKING THE CAR IS WHAT MAKES TRAFFIC SPAWN BEHIND IT. Below 42 per cent of top speed for two
  seconds the engine starts feeding cars in from the rear, faster the slower you are, so a harness
  that stops the car to hold the scene still has asked for vehicles to arrive in the one view it is
  measuring. Two of them lit 21.9 per cent of the pane. The dry control caught it - and it had read
  0.0000 on the run before, which was a scene that happened to be empty at that moment rather than a
  stable one. A sweeper clears the road every frame, the sampling takes a MEDIAN rather than a
  maximum so one contaminated frame cannot carry the answer, and a run reports how many it removed.
  Ten to eleven per run.

WHAT THIS CANNOT SAY. Whether twenty particles in a pane 60 pixels tall reads as weather or as
static is a judgement on a device, and so is whether a streak leaning the wrong way looks right
rather than merely correct. `--shots` writes the glass enlarged so the owner can judge from a
picture; the captures at rest read as weather in both rain and snow. NOT SEEN AT SPEED and not seen
on a phone.

AND `weather-test.py` IS FLAKY, WHICH IS NOT THIS UNIT'S DOING. Four runs on the baseline and four
on this change: both sides fail one or two checks, and WHICH checks vary run to run - the road
against the land, the band under the horizon, the far road against the near, the sky above the
horizon. The same headline failure appears in three of four on each side. It was measured on both
arms before this change was believed innocent of it. It wants its own unit.
