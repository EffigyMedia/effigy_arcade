### RLG-092 — The weather falls in the mirror too
- **RULED 2026-08-31T00:04:57-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: weather effects should be shown in the rear view - not the drops on the glass, but the rain and the snow themselves.

THE DISTINCTION IS THE WHOLE INSTRUCTION AND IT IS THE RIGHT ONE. A droplet on the lens belongs to the windscreen the player is looking through; it has no business in a picture of what is behind the car, because the mirror is a separate piece of glass and the drops on THAT would be a different effect nobody asked for. Falling precipitation is weather in the world, and the world is what the mirror shows.

IT FOLLOWS THE SAME RULING THE MIRROR ALREADY OBEYS. RLG-079 said the mirror must show the world state it is in - biome, road conditions, scenery - and everything in there asks the SAME functions the windscreen asks rather than similar ones. Rain and snow are the part of the world state that never got carried across, so this finishes that job rather than starting a new one.

AND THE PANE IS 44 PIXELS TALL, which is what makes it a design question rather than a copy. The forward view's rain is drawn for a 900-pixel screen; the same density and the same streak length in a strip that size would read as static rather than as weather. Expect the count and the length to need their own numbers, and expect the fps question to come with them - the mirror's scenery already carries a note about a forest costing four frames a second in there.

MEASURE IT THE WAY THE HOUR WAS MEASURED: the check should ask whether the mirror gets brighter or busier when the weather turns, against a dry frame of the same scene, rather than counting particles. A count is an implementation; a difference in the picture is the effect.
