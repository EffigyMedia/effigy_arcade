### RLG-122 — Traffic and rival engine sounds may be pinned rather than derived from their revs
- **RULED 2026-08-31T15:34:50-04:00** — TRACKED, NOT STARTED. Owner, 2026-08-31, from the device: "I'm not sure the engine sounds of all the traffic cars and maybe the race opponents are real and derived by their RPMs, because it seems to me that it's just a bunch of pinned engine sounds - there's no revving, there's no increasing decreasing based on speed acceleration, etc."

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN WITHOUT MEASURING, and it should be checked rather than trusted: the player's engine note is derived properly - `snd.drive` takes a rev ratio and maps it across better than three octaves, 58Hz to 470Hz, and the gearbox steps it on every shift. The OTHER cars go through `snd.traffic`, which is a different path, is called on every fifth frame rather than every frame, and takes a list of vehicles rather than a rev figure. Whether it derives a pitch per car from that car's own speed, and whether it has any notion of a gearbox or a rev range at all, is the question - and the owner's ear says it does not.

THE ONE PHYSICS RULING BEARS ON THIS. Every car in the fleet already runs the same torque curve and the same gear model, and `aiGearFactor` computes a rival's rpm from its speed against its own gearing. So the number the owner is asking to hear ALREADY EXISTS for every rival: nothing has to be invented, and the work may be as small as passing it to the sound rather than deriving a pitch from raw speed.

WHAT TO MEASURE FIRST, and it must be measured rather than read: a sound test cannot listen, but it can read the Web Audio graph. RLG-065's lesson applies exactly - a level read off a node is not evidence the node is heard, and the question worth asking is which context it belongs to. The experiment is a car accelerating hard through its gears with the harness reading the frequency its voice is carrying, and the check is whether that frequency STEPS at each shift and climbs between them. A pinned sound reads as one number that never moves; a derived one draws the sawtooth every rev counter draws.

AND THE TRAP: `snd.traffic` runs every fifth frame for cost reasons, and the note beside it says the smoothing on `setTargetAtTime` means nothing changes perceptibly between one frame and the next. That is true of a slowly varying value and false of a shift, which is a step. If the pitch is derived and still sounds pinned, the rate rather than the derivation may be the fault.

QUEUED AT THE END OF THE CURRENT QUEUE at the owner's instruction. It belongs after RLG-098 and RLG-099, which are the two screech rulings, because all three are the same subject - a sound taking its pitch from the wrong number - and RLG-098 is already ordered before RLG-099 for that reason.
