### RLG-093 — The sea meets the horizon instead of stopping at it
- **RULED 2026-08-31T00:08:35-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: the ocean drawn into the background - can the angle it is drawn at be interpolated from the slice segment's ocean, so it runs to the horizon seamlessly and reads as one body of water.

THE SHAPE OF THE PROBLEM IS THE ONE THE GROUND ALREADY HAD. The road geometry does not reach the horizon, so there is a band between the furthest drawn slice and the skyline that has to be filled by something else. For the LAND that gap is already handled - groundBase paints the far verge's colour rather than the near one, with a note about a flat fill never matching a gradient and a seam appearing wherever the drawn slices begin. The sea is the same gap with a different surface, and it was given the simpler treatment.

SO THE ANSWER IS PROBABLY NOT A NEW EFFECT. It is to make the background water take its edge from the LAST DRAWN SLICE rather than from a fixed line: the shoreline in the drawn geometry runs at an angle set by the bend and by which side the water is on, and the background should continue that angle to the horizon instead of meeting it square. That is an interpolation between two things the engine already knows.

AND THE SIDE CAN CHANGE WHILE YOU LOOK AT IT. rollSeaSide picks which side the water is on per place, so during a biome crossing one end of the draw can be coast and the other not. Whatever is built has to read the segment's own biome the way the scenery pass already does, or the background will show a sea the foreground has stopped having.

MEASURE IT AS A SEAM, NOT AS A PICTURE. The check that fits is the one the ground band wanted: sample a column of pixels across the join and ask whether there is a step. A screenshot shows whether it looks right today; a seam measurement shows whether it stays right when the bend, the hour or the weather move.
