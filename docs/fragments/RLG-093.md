### RLG-093 — The sea meets the horizon instead of stopping at it
- **RULED 2026-08-31T00:08:35-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31: the ocean drawn into the background - can the angle it is drawn at be interpolated from the slice segment's ocean, so it runs to the horizon seamlessly and reads as one body of water.

THE SHAPE OF THE PROBLEM IS THE ONE THE GROUND ALREADY HAD. The road geometry does not reach the horizon, so there is a band between the furthest drawn slice and the skyline that has to be filled by something else. For the LAND that gap is already handled - groundBase paints the far verge's colour rather than the near one, with a note about a flat fill never matching a gradient and a seam appearing wherever the drawn slices begin. The sea is the same gap with a different surface, and it was given the simpler treatment.

SO THE ANSWER IS PROBABLY NOT A NEW EFFECT. It is to make the background water take its edge from the LAST DRAWN SLICE rather than from a fixed line: the shoreline in the drawn geometry runs at an angle set by the bend and by which side the water is on, and the background should continue that angle to the horizon instead of meeting it square. That is an interpolation between two things the engine already knows.

AND THE SIDE CAN CHANGE WHILE YOU LOOK AT IT. rollSeaSide picks which side the water is on per place, so during a biome crossing one end of the draw can be coast and the other not. Whatever is built has to read the segment's own biome the way the scenery pass already does, or the background will show a sea the foreground has stopped having.

MEASURE IT AS A SEAM, NOT AS A PICTURE. The check that fits is the one the ground band wanted: sample a column of pixels across the join and ask whether there is a step. A screenshot shows whether it looks right today; a seam measurement shows whether it stays right when the bend, the hour or the weather move.
- **RULED 2026-08-31T08:46:49-04:00** — BUILT IN 0.10.21, AND THE RULING'S OWN DIAGNOSIS WAS WRONG. That is the part worth keeping. The
fragment supposed the background met the horizon at the wrong ANGLE and said so twice - "the angle it
is drawn at", "meeting it square" - and the angle turned out not to be what anybody could see. The
fault was a COLOUR.

THE ANGLE WAS BUILT FIRST, BECAUSE THAT IS WHAT THE RULING SAID. Two versions of it:

  A TANGENT AND A QUADRATIC, WHICH MEASURED WORSE. The slope of the shoreline as it leaves the drawn
  road, taken from the last two slices, used as the control point of a curve to the vanishing point.
  At the far end of the draw two neighbouring slices are a fraction of a pixel apart vertically, so
  the slope is a small number divided by a smaller one. On a 13-pixel band it put the bend at 13
  times the edge's own curvature where the straight line it replaced sat at 2. AN UNSTABLE ESTIMATE
  OF A QUANTITY IS WORSE THAN NOT USING IT.

  THEN NO ESTIMATE AT ALL, WHICH IS WHAT SHIPPED. The band walks on in the same steps the road walks
  in, asking `proj` and `roadsideAt` the same questions the drawn slices ask, until it reaches the
  horizon. There is no second construction, so there is nothing for a first one to disagree with, and
  the join stops being a join. It needs no sanity guard either - the note in the code records an
  earlier extrapolation that was wrong a third of the time and whose guard was false on a climbing
  road, so the band was skipped and the coast ran out at the skyline. Walking forward emits the
  points the projection gives; a point that does not sit between the horizon and the shore already
  drawn is simply not added, and the failure mode is a shorter walk rather than a missing sea.

AND THEN IT WAS MEASURED, AND IT HAD FIXED NOTHING VISIBLE. Across 13 bands in 40 places the straight
line creased by AT MOST 2.0 times the edge's own curvature, which is the quantisation of the reading
itself. The bands are 19 to 32 pixels tall in practice; over that distance a straight line and the
true shoreline are the same picture. The change is kept because it is the right construction and it
is what the owner asked for, and the record says plainly that it changed nothing that could be seen.

THE FAULT IS THAT THE BAND IS HAZED AND THE DRAWN SEA IS NOT. The band above the furthest drawn slice
is painted inside `drawSky`, BEFORE `drawHaze()`, so the distance wash falls on it. The road's own sea
is painted after and gets none. And `seaTone` had no distance term at all - one flat colour from the
bumper to the end of the draw, where the LAND has receded properly through `groundTone` since RLG-059.
So the water changed colour at the join: measured up to 45 in summed RGB, rgb(64,80,103) above the
line against rgb(49,73,81) below it, a pale blue meeting a dark green-grey. That is what "reads as one
body of water" was asking for.

SO THE SEA RECEDES NOW, toward the haze's own colour, from the same function `drawHaze` asks - one
definition, two callers, for the same reason the sky and the ground already work that way. The far
band asks for the tone WITHOUT the recession, because the wash is painted onto it rather than mixed
into it, and asking for both would haze it twice and put the seam back the other way round.

THE AMOUNT WAS MEASURED AND NOT CHOSEN, AND THE METHOD IS THE PART TO REUSE. This world cannot be
compared across runs: the road is generated per load, the day is always turning, and the sea's own
colour follows the hour - so two runs differ in three things at once, and RLG-062 exists because a
claim was already made and withdrawn on exactly that. `API.seaHaze` moves the value live, so six
values were read from the SAME band at the SAME hour, over six bands and four hours each:

    value   median step at the join   worst   samples under 10
     0.00            21.88             40.70        4 of 15
     0.15             9.43             17.93        9 of 16
     0.25             2.46              8.34       16 of 16
     0.34             9.08             29.63        8 of 16
     0.45            20.52             52.28        5 of 15
     0.60            36.39             65.08        2 of 13

A clean minimum with the curve rising on both sides, which is what makes 0.25 a measurement rather
than a fit. 0.00 is what shipped before this.

VERIFIED ON THE GATE ITSELF: median 21.94 with the recession removed against 1.57 with it, and 10 of
10 samples under the seam threshold against 4 of 12. `coast-test` passes three runs of three, so the
shoreline's own checks did not move.

FALSIFIED BY REMOVING THE RECESSION, NOT BY REVERTING. Reverting cannot falsify this: `API.seaHaze`
does not exist on the old build. Setting it to zero IS the old behaviour - the sea flat-coloured at
every distance while the band above it takes the haze - and the check goes red there.

WHAT THE CHECK DELIBERATELY DOES NOT ASSERT. The shape. `seajoin-test.py` reports the crease
measurement for both constructions and gates on neither, because a check that cannot fail is not a
check and this one demonstrably cannot: where the band is shallow the edge reader finds no unbroken
run at all. What it does assert structurally is that the shoreline IS carried on - `farSea().walked`
counts the continued points - so the construction cannot silently revert.

NOT SEEN ON A DEVICE. A tone step of 22 in summed RGB is small on a desktop panel and the owner
reported this by eye on a phone, which is the machine that can say whether it now reads as one body
of water.
