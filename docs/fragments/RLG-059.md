### RLG-059 — The biome builds the world: roadside scenery beyond the lamps, and the biome sets the vertical curvature
- **RULED 2026-08-29T03:47:10-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-29, filed to the queue and NOT started:**

> "As part of the 'new biomes road slices generate toward me so there is an immersive transition',
> I'd like to experiment with spawning biome based scenery the sides of the road just beyond the
> light posts. So thick forest in forest, large rock faces in mountain, buildings in city, the
> occasional cactus in desert. And the biome should probably also drive the magnitude of the road's
> vertical curvature - mountains on one extreme and desert > city on the other. Never completely
> flat but much less so."

**Two features, and they are not the same size.**

1. **Roadside scenery, biome by biome**, standing beyond the lamp posts: thick forest, rock faces,
   buildings, the occasional cactus. This is content in the draw loop, and the lamps already prove
   the placement works - `idx % 8 === 0` puts a post at a segment and the projection does the rest.
2. **The biome sets the road's vertical curvature.** Mountains at one extreme, desert and city at
   the other, **and never completely flat**. This is one number driven by an existing state, and it
   is by far the cheaper of the two.

**The owner's ordering of the flatter end is worth keeping exactly as stated:** desert is flatter
than city, and neither is flat. Written as `desert > city` in the sense of "desert is the flatter
extreme", and it should be confirmed which way round it was meant before it is built - the sentence
reads both ways and the difference is visible in play.

**It hangs off [[RLG-022]]**, which the owner named: biome sweeps toward the player so the transition
is immersive. Scenery that changes with the biome is the same idea seen from the roadside, and both
want the sweep to exist first or the change will happen as a hard edge at a segment boundary.

**Read before starting, and this is the real constraint.** The road is drawn far to near, and a
sprite is only ever a billboard emitted into the segment it stands on. Scenery beyond the lamps is
therefore more sprites per segment, at the widest part of the frame, every frame - and this project
has just spent two units on what the sprite pass does at a crest ([[RLG-041]]). **Measure the frame
cost before committing to a density**, and expect the answer to differ on the phone.

**The vertical curvature half touches the corner cap**, which is a renderer limit rather than a
taste one: past about 90 degrees the road leaves the frame. The vertical equivalent has the same
character - there is a magnitude past which a crest hides the road entirely - so mountains want a
measured ceiling rather than a chosen one.

**Not scope:** a new biome. The four exist; this changes what they look like and how they drive.
