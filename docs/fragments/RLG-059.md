### RLG-059 — The biome builds the world: roadside scenery beyond the lamps, and the biome sets the vertical curvature
- **RULED 2026-08-29T03:47:10-04:00** — **Owner, 2026-08-29, filed to the queue and NOT started:**

> "As part of the 'new biomes road slices generate toward me so there is an immersive transition',
> I'd like to experiment with spawning biome based scenery the sides of the road just beyond the
> light posts. So thick forest in forest, large rock faces in mountain, buildings in city, the
> occasional cactus in desert. And the biome should probably also drive the magnitude of the road's
> vertical curvature - mountains on one extreme and desert > city on the other. Never completely
> flat but much less so."

**Two features, and they are not the same size.**

1. **Roadside scenery, biome by biome**, standing beyond the lamp posts: thick forest, rock faces,
   buildings, the occasional cactus. This is content in the draw loop, and the lamps already prove
   the placement works - `idx % 8 === 0` puts a post at a segment and the projection does the rest.
2. **The biome sets the road's vertical curvature.** Mountains at one extreme, desert and city at
   the other, **and never completely flat**. This is one number driven by an existing state, and it
   is by far the cheaper of the two.

**The owner's ordering of the flatter end is worth keeping exactly as stated:** desert is flatter
than city, and neither is flat. Written as `desert > city` in the sense of "desert is the flatter
extreme", and it should be confirmed which way round it was meant before it is built - the sentence
reads both ways and the difference is visible in play.

**It hangs off [[RLG-022]]**, which the owner named: biome sweeps toward the player so the transition
is immersive. Scenery that changes with the biome is the same idea seen from the roadside, and both
want the sweep to exist first or the change will happen as a hard edge at a segment boundary.

**Read before starting, and this is the real constraint.** The road is drawn far to near, and a
sprite is only ever a billboard emitted into the segment it stands on. Scenery beyond the lamps is
therefore more sprites per segment, at the widest part of the frame, every frame - and this project
has just spent two units on what the sprite pass does at a crest ([[RLG-041]]). **Measure the frame
cost before committing to a density**, and expect the answer to differ on the phone.

**The vertical curvature half touches the corner cap**, which is a renderer limit rather than a
taste one: past about 90 degrees the road leaves the frame. The vertical equivalent has the same
character - there is a magnitude past which a crest hides the road entirely - so mountains want a
measured ceiling rather than a chosen one.

**Not scope:** a new biome. The four exist; this changes what they look like and how they drive.

- **RULED 2026-08-29T13:53:53-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-29, adding to the queue and reordering it: "the additional aspect of the weather
overhaul that gates the type of weather based on the biome... I suppose that work could be done with
the biome overhaul. I think the biome overhaul should come before the scenery because it is going to
drive it better."

THE ORDER IS NOW: the biome overhaul, then the scenery, then the gearbox gate.

The reason is the owner's and it is the right one. Scenery is a biome's own furniture. Deciding what a
biome IS - what falls on it, what grows on it, what stands beside the road - and then drawing it, is
one decision made once. Drawing the scenery first and overhauling the biomes underneath it afterwards
means choosing the same thing twice and throwing the first answer away.

WHAT ALREADY EXISTS, so the overhaul starts from the truth rather than from a plan: every biome
carries a rain and a snow probability and the weather step rolls against them, so WEATHER IS ALREADY
GATED BY BIOME - a desert has a four per cent chance of rain and none at all of snow, and a tundra
snows more often than not. The cloud cover added under RLG-057 reads the same two numbers, so an arid
place is usually clear and a cold one usually is not.

What does not exist is everything else a biome should decide: its scenery, its skyline, how its cover
behaves beyond a single tendency, and whether the weather has any relationship to the biome you are
driving INTO rather than the one you are standing in. That last part is RLG-022 - the biome sweeping
toward the player - and it belongs inside this work rather than beside it.

WHAT EACH BIOME GETS, and the differences between them are structural rather than cosmetic:

  CITY      buildings at various heights, WITH LIT WINDOWS AT NIGHT. The consistency requirement is
            explicit: the windows must agree with the skyline drawn behind them, so the two cannot be
            separate ideas of what a lit building looks like.
  FOREST    thick forest, both sides of the road.
  MOUNTAIN  rock faces and large slopes that cut into the frame and rise up and out, IN SEVERAL
            LAYERS, so the road reads as passing between them rather than past them. This is the only
            one of the four that is explicitly multi-layer.
  DESERT    sparse cactus. The word the owner used is "just" - it is the empty one, and its emptiness
            is what makes the others read as full.
  TUNDRA    NOT SPECIFIED. The owner named four and said "I forget if we have other biomes". There
            are five. Tundra needs its own answer before this is finished, and it is the owner's.

"JUST LIKE THE LIGHT POSTS" IS THE MECHANISM AND IT IS ALSO THE PROBLEM. The lamps are drawn per
segment, walking toward the camera, which is the model the owner is pointing at. They also POP IN,
which is [[RLG-073]]: they are not clipped or culled the way the cars are. Scenery written against the
lamps as they stand today would inherit that fault five times over, and there would then be five
things to fix rather than one.

SO THE CULL COMES FIRST. The owner listed it last - "as for the clipping/culling, we need all those
slice drawn, scenery objects, including the light posts to use the same system that clips and culls
vehicles relative the ground" - but building scenery onto a system that pops means writing every
scenery type twice. The deliverable does not change; only the order does.

STILL OPEN FROM THIS FRAGMENT'S EARLIER RULING: the biome driving the road's vertical curvature, and
which way round "desert > city" was meant. The owner has not answered that and it is untouched by this
statement.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T15:35:21-04:00** — THE LAMPS ARE CITY SCENERY. Owner, 2026-08-29: "the light posts are probably specifically only city
scenery. So the cities scenery would be the light posts and building buildings to make it feel like

That changes what the lamps ARE. They are drawn today on every eighth segment regardless of place, so
a desert at midnight is lit by street lighting and a forest has a lamp standard every eighth segment
of nowhere. Under this ruling they become one of CITY's two scenery objects, alongside the buildings,
and they stop existing in the other four biomes. The night in a forest or a desert then goes properly
dark, which is a consequence worth stating because it is a large change to how those biomes look and
it was not asked for in those words.

THE GROUND COLOUR IS STRICT PER BIOME. Owner, same message: "we need to make sure that each biome is
strict about the color of the ground." Read alongside the far-field ruling in [[RLG-022]] - the band
under the horizon matching the foreground and sitting slightly darker - this says the ground is the
biome's signature and must not be approximate. Today the night and golden-hour branches of the ground
fill are FLAT HEX CONSTANTS shared by all five biomes: `#12251a` at night and `#2f4a2c` at golden
hour, whatever place you are in. So a desert at night is the same green-black as a forest at night.
That is the first thing this ruling breaks, and it is in the same code the snow cover touches.

TUNDRA IS SNOW-COVERED BEFORE ANY SNOW FALLS. Owner, 2026-08-29, answering the question this fragment
raised about the fifth biome:

  "let's make the tundra automatically snow covered whether it's snowing or not. So let's assume that
   base tundra is 50% snow coverage and then if it does snow then it just increases from there like
   normal. The scenery and skyline would be just like the mountains, except the tundras mountains will
   be white and the scenery rock faces cliff faces and mountain sides would also be white to match
   with the ground."

  a FLOOR, not a level. `settle` sits at 0.5 in tundra and accumulation builds from there in the
  ordinary way. The unwind recorded in [[RLG-057]] must stop at the floor rather than at zero, and
  leaving the biome must release it - which is the same `settleMelt` route a desert already uses.

  ITS SCENERY IS MOUNTAIN'S, PAINTED WHITE. Rock faces, cliff faces and mountain sides in layers, and
  its skyline the same, all white to agree with the ground. So tundra is not a sixth set of art; it
  is mountain's set with a palette. That is worth saying plainly because it halves the work.

  AND THE GRIP FOLLOWS, WITHOUT ANYTHING BEING ADDED. `wetGrip()` already costs `settle * 0.30`, so a
  floor of 0.5 makes tundra permanently slipperier than anywhere else - about 15% of dry grip gone
  before a flake falls. That is a consequence of the ruling rather than a separate decision, and it
  should be measured rather than assumed once it is built.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T22:56:28-04:00** — THE TUNDRA'S SNOW FLOOR IS BUILT. `snowFloor` is 0.50 on TUNDRA and absent everywhere else - absent
rather than written as zero, because a place either lies under snow or it does not.

IT CROSSES ON THE WEATHER'S BAND, NOT THE GROUND'S. Driving into a tundra, the white should arrive
with the cold rather than with the colour of the verge, so the floor blends over the 72-segment
weather band rather than the 18-segment colour one. Leaving lowers it the same way, which means the
melt that follows is the ordinary unwind and not a special case.

THE CLAMP IS NOT `Math.max(floor, settle)`, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS THE WHOLE POINT. That form yanks
the level UP to the floor the instant the floor rises, so arriving in a tundra would snap the ground
white in one frame - the exact switch this run of work exists to remove. Two separate mechanisms
instead: a decay that stops at the floor when the ground is already above it, and a gentle climb for
arriving below it. Measured, the climb runs 0.027 to 0.317 over three seconds, about four seconds
from bare ground to half cover.

THE GRIP FOLLOWED WITH NOTHING ADDED, as predicted when this was recorded. `wetGrip` already costs
`settle * 0.30`, so the floor takes about 15% of dry grip in a tundra before a flake falls. Measured
against a dry desert in the same run.

TWO FALSIFICATIONS WERE NEEDED, AND THE FIRST ONE FAILED TO FAIL. Deleting the decay clamp left every
check green, because the CLIMB pulls the level back within a sample interval - the floor is
implemented by two mechanisms and removing one leaves the other doing the job. That is redundancy
rather than a defect, but it means the first attempt was aimed at one line instead of at the feature.
Deleting `snowFloor` from the biome table is the real defect state, and it turns four checks red.

AND ONE CHECK SWITCHED ITSELF OFF WHEN ITS SUBJECT WENT MISSING. "Nothing takes it below the floor"
compared against the floor the ENGINE reported. With `snowFloor` deleted that read zero, so the
assertion became "at least -0.02", which is true of anything. It compares against 0.48 now - the
owner's own number, written into the check - so it cannot be neutralised by the fault it exists to
catch. This is the same shape as the environment's `RVW-025` finding 1, arrived at independently:
a guard that asks the code under test what its subject is has no way to tell absent from broken.

A THIRD MEASUREMENT WAS CONTAMINATED BY THE GAME WORKING CORRECTLY. The unwind reading first showed
settle FALLING to 0.536 and then CLIMBING to 0.554, which looked like the floor holding it. It was
fresh snow: a tundra has a 0.62 snow chance and the weather timer had fired. The reading holds the
sky dry for its duration now, and the assertion is that nothing takes the ground BELOW the floor at
any point rather than that it rests on it exactly - because a tundra putting snow back is the game
working, not the check failing.

AND THE UNWIND RATE HAD TO BE REAL. Setting the level directly and then measuring gave a slope of
0.006 a second - the fallback for a run that has never seen snow - which moves the level by 0.025
over the whole reading and reaches no floor at all. A floor and no floor were indistinguishable. It
snows first now, so the rate the unwind uses is the rate a fall actually deposited at.

STILL UNBUILT FROM THIS RULING: the tundra's scenery and skyline, which are mountain's painted white.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T23:36:41-04:00** — THE SCENERY SYSTEM IS BUILT, WITH TWO BIOMES IN IT. Forest and desert first, deliberately: they are
the two extremes the owner described - "thick forest of trees on either side" against "just a sparse
quantity of cactuses" - so building both at once proves the system carries a range rather than one
look. City, mountain and tundra declare nothing yet and therefore draw nothing, with no branch
anywhere saying so.

FOUR DECISIONS, AND EACH ONE IS LOAD-BEARING:

  ONE SPRITE PER KIND, BUILT ONCE. A thick forest is a couple of hundred trees in frame. Painting
  each as a path every frame is a couple of hundred path builds sixty times a second; drawing a
  cached bitmap is a scaled `drawImage` the browser resolves in hardware. The skyline already works
  this way, so this is the existing pattern rather than a new one.

  PLACEMENT IS A HASH OF THE SEGMENT INDEX. This is the one that would have been a real defect. The
  index IS the world position, so hashing it puts an object somewhere and leaves it there for as long
  as that segment exists, storing nothing. `Math.random()` per frame would reshuffle the entire
  roadside every frame - the forest would boil, and it would have looked like a rendering fault of
  some other kind entirely.

  THEY GO THROUGH `crestGate`, which is the whole of [[RLG-073]] and the reason the cull was done
  before this rather than after. Measured over three roads: 5,763 clipped of 386,607 asked. Written
  against the lamps as they were a day ago, every one of five scenery types would have inherited the
  pop and needed fixing separately.

  AN OBJECT BELONGS TO ITS SEGMENT'S BIOME, not to the car's. `bioAt(idx)`, so the trees stop and the
  cactus starts at the same place on the road where the ground colour changes. Under a global `biome`
  the roadside would switch under the car while the ground ahead was still the old place.

SEEN, NOT ASSUMED. Both were captured and looked at rather than reasoned about, which is the lesson
[[RLG-079]] cost four rounds to learn. The forest reads as a wall of conifer receding to the
treeline; the desert reads as empty with occasional saguaro, which is what makes the forest read as
full.

WHAT IS NOT RIGHT YET, said plainly: the objects sit in a band near the horizon because anything
placed at 1.35 road widths out is off the side of the screen once it is close. That is correct
perspective and it may still be wrong as a picture - a real forest has trunks at the edge of the
frame. The owner's verdict on the device decides whether it needs a second, nearer layer.

STILL UNBUILT: city buildings with lit windows and the lamps becoming city-only; mountain rock faces
in layers; tundra as mountain painted white. And the owner has since asked for the skylines
themselves to be more detailed, once the scenery is done.
- **RULED 2026-08-29T23:48:08-04:00** — THE CITY IS BUILT, AND THE LAMPS BELONG TO IT. Owner, 2026-08-29: "the light posts are probably
specifically only city scenery. So the cities scenery would be the light posts and building buildings
to make it feel like you're in the city."

THE BUILDINGS ARE TWO SHEETS FROM ONE DESCRIPTION, and that is the part worth recording. The owner
required the roadside windows to agree with the skyline's - "for consistency with the skyline" - so
the same technique is used: a dark window grid baked into the building, and a second sheet carrying
ONLY the glass, drawn over the top with `lighter` at an alpha that follows the same clock the street
lamps use. Both sheets walk `CITY_WINDOWS`, one function, rather than holding two lists of
coordinates. A lit window that does not line up with the hole it shines out of is the fault that
shape exists to make impossible.

WHICH OFFICES ARE OCCUPIED IS A FUNCTION OF THE WINDOW, not of chance. `(r*7 + c*13 + i*5) % 11 < 4`
leaves about a third of them dark. A random call would have re-rolled every frame and the city would
have flickered like a fault.

THE LAMPS NOW ASK THE SEGMENT'S BIOME, so the lighting ends where the city ends rather than under the
car - the same rule the ground colour and the roadside objects follow.

AND FOUR BIOMES ARE NOW DARK AT NIGHT. That is a large change to how they look and nobody asked for
it in those words; it follows from the ruling. A forest at midnight has nothing in it but the moon,
and it reads as a genuinely different place from the city rather than as the same road with different
verge colours. Captured and looked at rather than assumed.

THE OCCLUSION HARNESS WENT RED, AND THE FIX WAS TO CHANGE THE HARNESS, WHICH IS A SHAPE TO BE CAREFUL
WITH. It pinned itself to FOREST to guarantee scenery, and asserted that lamps go through the crest
gate. The moment the lamps became city-only that assertion read `asked=0`. Nothing was broken - the
harness was measuring a thing the ruling had just moved. It is pinned to CITY now, which is the only
place with both kinds. Written down rather than quietly repinned, because "the check went red and the
answer was to edit the check" is exactly how a real regression gets waved through.
