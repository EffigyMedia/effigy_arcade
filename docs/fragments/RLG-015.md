### RLG-015 — Hardpoint gets its sound: four held layers that follow the ship, and the mechanic becomes audible
- **RULED 2026-08-28T13:22:39-04:00** — - **What was actually missing was not effects. It was that nothing was continuous.** Every sound in
  this cabinet was a one-shot, so **the ship had no engine**: you could fly a first-person sim from a
  bridge and hear only the shots you fired. And because the engine was silent, **the mechanic the
  whole combat model is built on could not be heard at all** - engines dark, everything else
  destroys, and the moment a target went dark sounded like a menu blip, because it literally reused
  `snd.ok()`.

- **Four held layers now track the ship every frame**, with the one-shots sitting on top of them:

  | Layer | What it follows |
  |---|---|
  | `engine` | pitch rides SPEED, level rides the THROTTLE |
  | `burn` | noise, and only above 100% - the sound of the thing that overheats you |
  | `air` | hull air on the bridge, ventilation aboard a boarded ship |
  | `alarm` | a pulse that appears with heat, and a lower slower one for a failing hull |

- **Pitch rides speed and level rides the lever, deliberately.** The ship then sounds like it is
  catching up with what you asked for, which is exactly what it is doing.

- **SILENCE IS ONE OF THE SOUNDS.** When the engines cut, the engine layer goes to zero over a
  0.55-second spin-down and only air is left - and the air gets LOUDER, because there is nothing over
  it any more. That hole is the loudest thing in the game and it cost a level change rather than a
  new effect. Measured: engine 0.128 to 0.002, air 0.035 to 0.055.

- **The bed reads the game in three tiers.** A bed that does not know what is happening is wallpaper.
  The bridge is sparse and low; **hunted adds a heartbeat on the off-beat** - nothing gets louder,
  something is simply there that was not; a boarding drops the low end for something tighter, because
  you are inside a hull rather than flying one. The title has its own slower bed, because a menu that
  plays combat music has already spent the moment the run should open on.

- **Nine events that were silent now have sounds**, and the three engine events got their own rather
  than sharing: theirs going dark is a long spin-down, yours cutting is harder and closer, and the
  relight is a starter turning over. Also added: the hunter arriving, losing him, the hull breach,
  being repelled, dying, the airlock cycling after the clamps, and footsteps.

- **Footsteps are paced by DISTANCE, not by time.** A step every N milliseconds keeps walking while
  you stand against a wall pushing into it, which is the moment a player is listening hardest.
  Accumulating distance actually moved means a blocked player is a silent one, and a heavier kit
  takes longer strides for free.

- **`tools/audio-test.py` reads the Web Audio graph, because a sound test cannot listen.** Every held
  layer is a GainNode and an OscillatorNode with live values, so "does the engine get louder when you
  open the throttle" has a number behind it. This matters more than it would for one-shots: a missing
  one-shot is obvious the first time you play, but **a held layer that has quietly stopped tracking
  the throttle still sounds like a game with an engine drone**, and nobody notices for a month.

- **THE TEST WAS WRONG THREE TIMES BEFORE THE CODE WAS, and each was a real lesson.**
  1. It called `snd.step` forty times in a tight loop and read the graph immediately. Everything came
     back frozen. `setTargetAtTime` ramps from the AUDIO clock, so forty calls in one instant all
     re-target from the same moment. **Push the state, wait real time, then read.**
  2. It gave the spin-down 520ms and called a 0.55-second fade a failure of the code. Engines do not
     stop like a switch; the check now gives it the time it was designed to take.
  3. It read the alarms as a single snapshot. **Both alarms are square pulses, so one reading lands
     wherever the phase happens to be** - it reported the heat alarm as silent while passing on the
     hull alarm beside it, which is a check that would have missed a genuinely dead alarm. Measured
     as a peak now, and settled first, because a peak taken across a transition is a peak of the
     previous state decaying.

- **Proof.** `audio-test` 9/9 with the numbers printed. `pack.sh` all gates, 42 files;
  `smoke-test` 6/6.
