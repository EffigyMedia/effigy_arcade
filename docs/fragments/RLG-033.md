### RLG-033 — Redline: racers need more intelligence
- **RULED 2026-08-28T17:17:01-04:00** — **SCOPE, owner's ruling 2026-08-28.** The fragment was a title and nothing else, so the scope was
put to the owner as a question rather than inferred. The owner chose all four parts offered. They
are recorded here in the order they will be built, and they are FOUR UNITS, not one.

**1. Measure it first, the way [[RLG-029]] was measured.** Nothing is changed until there are
numbers describing what the rivals do today: overtakes attempted and completed, how long a rival
sits stuck behind slower traffic, whether it holds a line or drifts back to a lane centre the
moment a gap clears, and how much of that varies between the eleven cars. This comes first because
the rest of this fragment is a set of impressions until it exists, and because RLG-029 has just
shown what an unmeasured impression is worth: the tow was believed to be weak, and it was not
weak - it was arithmetically unreachable, and no amount of tuning would have found that.

The instrument follows the same rules that made `band-test.py` trustworthy: a control arm, a held
initial condition rather than a waited-for one, a seeded `Math.random` so two arms race the same
grid, repeats because one run is weather, and columns that are readings rather than explanations.

**2. Racing lines and overtaking.** Today a rival only slows for what is in front of it and leans
away from it - `want = Math.min(want, blocking car * 0.98)` and a `dodge` term - and then drifts
back toward its lane centre as soon as nothing is pressing. That is avoidance, not an overtake. A
rival should pick a side, commit to it, and finish the move.

**3. Defending and blocking.** A rival being caught does nothing about it. One that is being closed
on should be able to cover the inside line. **This one needs care and is the most likely of the
three to be got wrong:** a rival that defends against the player is a difficulty increase applied
by proximity, which is rubber banding wearing a different hat. The measurement in part 1 is what
tells the difference afterwards, and the band it must not quietly duplicate is the one RLG-038 has
just made real.

**4. Mistakes and personality.** Every rival drives to identical rules and differs only in top
speed and in the small skill spread on `base`. Per-car error rates and aggression, so the field
behaves like eleven drivers rather than one driver run eleven times.

**What is NOT in scope**, because nobody asked for it: changing the size of the field, changing the
race distance, giving rivals anything the player does not have, and any tuning of the rubber band
itself - that is RLG-038's, and it is settled and measured.

Parts 2, 3 and 4 are each a unit and a commit of their own. None of them starts before part 1 has
run and its numbers are in the record.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T17:25:43-04:00** — **PART 1 MEASURED, no change made.** `tools/racer-test.py` is new; evidence [[EVD-007]]. Three
paired runs of 40 seconds, eleven rivals each, `Math.random` seeded so both arms race the same grid
and the same traffic. The control arm forces the rivals' lateral term to zero at the point of use -
in the server, in memory, no file touched - so the scan that computes `dodge` still runs and still
sets `want` from the blocking car, and only the steering is removed.

| | dodge off | dodge on |
|---|---|---|
| passes per minute | 8.95 | **35.62** |
| share of time off a lane centre | 0% | **61%** |
| share of time held up by a slower car ahead | 46% | **53%** |
| mean speed | 122mph | **95mph** |

**The steering is load-bearing, and I expected it not to be.** The hypothesis written into the
harness before it ran was that rivals are carried past slower cars by raw speed and the lateral term
is decoration. That is wrong: the dodge produces four times the overtakes. The prediction is
recorded here because it was wrong, and because the harness would have been built the same way
either way - which is the only reason its answer is worth anything.

**The control is not vacuous.** `off-centre` reads 0% with the dodge off and 61% with it on. That
is the patch landing, measured, rather than asserted.

**But the steering is expensive, and this is what part 2 has to fix.** With the dodge on, rivals are
27mph slower and MORE obstructed, not less - held up 53% of the time against 46%. A car that steers
should be getting out of trouble; these are steering into it. The shape of the fault is in the code
and now has a number against it: `dodge` is a per-frame lateral RATE that lasts exactly as long as
something is in front, and the moment the pressure goes the car drifts back toward
`LANE_X[r.lane]` - a lane index that the dodge never updates. So a rival leans out, gets far enough
across to unblock its scan, stops leaning, and is pulled back into the lane it was always in. It
does not commit to a lane and it does not finish a move. 61% off-centre is that behaviour: not
overtaking, and not in a lane either.

**This is the same fault the owner reported from the traffic side on 2026-08-28** - that a merging
car moves a fraction of a lane rather than fully into the next one. Tracked as [[RLG-040]]. The two
are worth fixing as one idea even though they are two code paths: a lane change should have a
TARGET lane and be finished, rather than being a pressure that decays.

**What this measurement CANNOT tell you, and part 4 must not assume it can.** The spread row is
large - passes per minute has a standard deviation of 16.88 against a mean of 35.62, and speed 49
against 95. That is NOT evidence of personality. Eleven rivals are strung out over miles of road
with different amounts of traffic around them, so situation and car identity are confounded in every
one of those numbers, and this design cannot separate them. Part 4 needs a measurement that holds
the situation constant - the same trick [[RLG-029]] used - before anyone claims the field does or
does not have character.

**Not measured here:** defending, because nothing in the engine does it yet, so there is nothing to
take a baseline of. Part 3 starts from zero by definition.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T17:31:19-04:00** — **CORRECTION to the part 1 ruling above, same day, before it was committed.** Two of its claims do
not survive a second run and are WITHDRAWN.

The ruling above was written from the first paired run. The evidence run [[EVD-007]] repeated it with
the same settings and disagreed:

| measure, dodge off -> dodge on | first run | [[EVD-007]] | holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| passes per minute | 8.95 -> 35.62 | 6.18 -> 29.44 | **yes** |
| off a lane centre | 0% -> 61% | 0% -> 59% | **yes** |
| held up by a slower car | 46% -> 53% | 46% -> 47% | no |
| mean speed | 122 -> 95mph | 85 -> 86mph | **no, and it reverses** |

**WITHDRAWN: that the steering costs the rivals 27mph, and that it leaves them more obstructed.**
One run showed both, the other showed neither, and the speed figure changed direction. Neither is
evidence of anything.

**STANDS: the steering is worth four to five times the overtakes, and rivals spend about 60% of
their time off a lane centre against 0% with the lateral term removed.** Both reproduce closely
across two independent sessions.

The lesson is narrower than the project's existing one and worth writing down separately.
`--repeats 3` was already in the harness because one pair is weather, and it was not enough: the
three pairs WITHIN a session agreed with each other and the two sessions did not. Between-session
variance is larger than within-session variance here, because a session fixes the seeded grid at
its start and everything downstream inherits it. A number from this harness is not established
until it has been seen in two separate invocations, and the report cannot tell you that on its
own - it only ever sees one.

The reading of the fault does not change, because it never rested on the withdrawn numbers: 60% off
a lane centre with the dodge on, and 0% with it off, is a car that leans out of its lane and is
pulled back without finishing anything. That is [[RLG-040]]'s shape from the rivals' side.
- **RULED 2026-08-28T20:44:10-04:00** — **THIS FRAGMENT IS NOW LOAD-BEARING, by the owner's ruling of 2026-08-28:** *"Why do the NPCs need to
have quicker or faster cars? Just make them race well."*

[[RLG-042]] part 2 found that rivals had been accelerating about three and a half times harder than
any car the player can buy, and fixed it. The field is measurably slower as a result - recovery from
40% to 90% of pace went from 3.82s to 13.5s in v0.9.8. The owner was offered three ways to give it
back and **rejected all the ones that involve better cars.**

**So parts 2, 3 and 4 of this fragment are no longer polish on a field that was already fast enough.
They are the whole of what makes a rival competitive.** Racing lines and committed overtakes,
defending, and per-driver mistakes and personality are the compensation, and there is no other.

**Part 1's measurement is the baseline for that, and it is already in hand:** the steering is worth
four to five times the overtakes, and rivals spend about 60% of their time off a lane centre - they
lean out, unblock their own scan, and are pulled back to a lane index the dodge never updates. That
is the first thing to fix and it is shared with [[RLG-040]].

**Sequence, and the reason for it.** Part 2 first: a rival that finishes an overtake is worth more
than one with a personality, and every later part is easier to judge once cars commit to a line.
Part 3 next, with the warning this fragment already carries - defending because the player is close
is rubber banding under another name. Part 4 last, and it needs a measurement design that holds the
situation constant, which part 1 explicitly could not do.

**Measure against part 1's numbers each time.** `racer-test.py` exists and its control arm works;
passes per minute and time off a lane centre are the two figures to move. And the lesson recorded
there stands: nothing from that harness is established until two separate invocations agree, because
a session fixes its seeded grid at the start and between-session variance is the larger kind.
