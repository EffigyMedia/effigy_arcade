### RLG-096 — The skyline chase was measured in frames, so the device decided it
- **RULED 2026-08-31T08:03:17-04:00** — THIS IS THE OTHER HALF OF THE OWNER'S REPORT. They said the skyline was "jittering and popping".
[[RLG-094]] was the popping - the whole city rebuilt as a different city ten times a minute - and it
was certain. This is the jitter, and unlike the popping it was found by MEASURING rather than by
reading, because nothing about it is visible in the source without asking what a frame is worth.

WHAT THE TRACE SAID FIRST, AND WHY IT WAS NOT THE ANSWER. `API.skyTrace()` - the hook UNT-088 left
behind before it was abandoned - was read over a 14-second drive covering 86,000 units. The INPUT to
the parallax, `bendPx(pos + 30000)`, steps: median change 0.099 pixels a frame, worst 2.189, which is
22 times the median. The chased value takes that down to a worst of 0.554. So the chase is doing its
job and a half-pixel step is not what anybody calls jitter. That measurement was nearly written up as
"no jitter remains".

THE FIRST READING OF IT MEASURED NOTHING AT ALL, AND SAID SO ONLY BECAUSE IT WAS ASKED TO. `setSpd`
was called once and the car was never driven: the step loop bled the speed off in a frame or two,
`pos` did not advance, and `bendPx(pos + 30000)` returned the same number for 542 consecutive frames.
A perfectly still trace reads exactly like a perfectly smooth one. The instrument now refuses to
report unless the car actually travelled.

THE FAULT IS IN THE CHASE ITSELF, NOT IN WHAT IT IS CHASING. It was
`skySmooth += (skyWant - skySmooth) * 0.045` - a fixed fraction PER FRAME. That is a time constant of
about 22 frames: 0.37 seconds at 60fps, 0.18 at 120. How fast the city answers a corner was decided
by the refresh rate of the device it happened to be running on, and the owner's phone is not this
machine.

AND AN UNSTEADY RATE IS WORSE THAN A WRONG ONE. The frame rate is not constant inside a single run -
`fps-test` measures FOREST between 45 and 60 on one unchanged build - so the chase sped up and slowed
down as the scenery thickened. A horizon that surges and lags for no reason visible on screen is a
jitter, it is not reproducible without the reporter's frame rate, and it would have survived any
number of sessions smoothing the input.

MEASURED, AND THE SIZE OF IT IS THE PART WORTH KEEPING. Displace the chase by 100 pixels with the car
parked, wait a second and a half of WALL CLOCK, and read what is left:

              fixed fraction per frame      corrected to seconds
   61 fps               1.45                        1.42
   16 fps              33.12                        1.23

Twenty-three times further behind at 16fps than at 61, against no meaningful difference after the
fix. A phone dropping frames in a forest was getting a visibly different skyline from the same road.

THE TUNED NUMBER DID NOT MOVE. `chase(k, dt)` restates a per-frame fraction as a per-second rate, so
0.045 still means exactly what it meant at 60fps and every value tuned under the old form keeps its
meaning. That is what makes this a fix rather than a retune, and it is why no screenshot had to be
re-judged.

THE STEP IS MEASURED IN `drawSky` RATHER THAN TAKEN FROM THE GAME LOOP, because `drawSky` runs on the
TITLE as well, where no step loop runs at all. That was the original reason for a fixed fraction and
it is a real constraint, not an oversight - the answer is for the function to measure its own elapsed
time, which it now does once a frame and shares with the city's window clock so there is one clock
and not two.

AND THE STEP IS CLAMPED AT AN EIGHTH OF A SECOND, WHICH IS A DESIGN DECISION AND NOT A ROUNDING. A
backgrounded tab comes back with a gap of seconds in it, and an unclamped chase would snap the city
across the glass on the frame the player returns - the exact pop this work exists to remove. Below
8fps the chase is deliberately NOT time-correct: a game running that slowly has lost more than a
horizon, and a lagging skyline is the right failure there.

THE CLAMP FAILED A CORRECT BUILD BEFORE IT WAS UNDERSTOOD. `skychase-test.py` first throttled to
4.7fps, which is past the clamp, so the slow run was measuring the CEILING and reported a
frame-counting chase on an engine that had already been fixed. The harness reads `API.skyStepMax()`
now and refuses to measure below it with a margin - so the check can never again report the clamp as
a defect, and the number it refuses at comes from the engine rather than from a copy.

FALSIFIED BY REINTRODUCING THE DEFECT, NOT BY REVERTING. Reverting cannot falsify this file either -
the instrument that displaces the chase and reads its target does not exist on the old build.
`API.skyChaseFrames(true)` puts the fixed per-frame fraction back, and with it the two residuals
differ by 96 per cent.

A SECOND FAULT IN THE TRACE, FOUND THE SAME WAY. `skyTrace` reported `want` as the raw
`bendPx(pos + 30000)`, but the chase converges on `-bendPx * 0.55` - a different number. The first
version of the check measured its residual against `want` and read 34 left of 100 after a second and
a half of a chase that should have been at 1.9, AND PASSED. The trace publishes `target` now, which
is the value the chase actually approaches.

AND ONE FLAKE IN [[RLG-095]]'s OWN CHECK WAS FIXED HERE, in the same commit, because gating this unit
is what found it. The frozen-clock control went red on about one run in three: setting the window
clock did not stop it, so each sample landed at the time it was set to PLUS however long the read
took, and a window sitting on the edge of its cycle flipped between two samples that were meant to be
identical. `API.holdWindowClock` stops the clock, the whole window section is now sampled at exact
times, and three consecutive runs are clean. It is recorded here rather than split off because it is
a correction to an instrument built one commit earlier, discovered by this unit's own gate run, and
two lines of it are interleaved with this unit's edits in the same file.

NOT SEEN ON A DEVICE, AND THIS IS THE ONE MOST LIKELY TO SHOW THERE. Every number above comes from a
desktop browser held near 60fps or throttled on purpose. The owner's phone is the machine where the
fault actually lived, and it is the only place that can say whether the jitter is gone.
