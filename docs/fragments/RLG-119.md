### RLG-119 — Grip should decide how sharply a car answers the wheel, not only how wide it is pushed
- **RULED 2026-08-31T12:58:41-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31, on the brake and grip measurement: "Should grip not only affect being pushed on the
corner, but also your steering rate? A formula should handle extremely well and a lorry could handle
poorly."

YES, AND THE CODE CONFIRMS THE GAP RATHER THAN THE MEASUREMENT DOING IT. `grip` is consulted in
exactly two places - the braking rate, and `cornerG`, which decides how hard a bend pushes the car
wide. The rate at which a car answers the wheel is in neither:

    const grip  = (1 - Math.exp(-14*dt)) * (1 - carry*0.72);
    const want2 = clamp((targetX - playerX) * grip, -4.2*dt, 4.2*dt);

Those two numbers - the 14 and the 4.2 - are the same for every body in the game. A LORRY places
itself across the road exactly as sharply as a FORMULA car. The only thing that moves them is the
weather, through `carry`, and that is a road condition rather than a vehicle.

SO A LORRY TODAY IS SLOW, HEAVY, BAD AT STOPPING AND PUSHED WIDE IN CORNERS - and turns like a racing
car. Three of the four axes already say "lorry"; the fourth says nothing at all, which is why the owner
noticed.

THE ONE REAL RISK IS THAT THIS TOUCHES EVERY CAR THE PLAYER WILL EVER DRIVE, and it is the most
felt-per-line change in the engine. The two constants are the whole of how the game handles. Move them
per body and every car changes at once, including the three the owner has already judged on the
device. So it should ship on its own, be measurable before and after, and be the only thing in its
build.

THE SHAPE THAT FITS WHAT IS ALREADY THERE. `4.2` is a ceiling on lateral speed - how fast the car can
be placed - and `14` is how quickly it converges on the ask. Grip should scale the ceiling: a formula
car gets there faster, a lorry cannot. Scaling the convergence as well would make a low-grip car
feel LAGGY rather than heavy, which is a different and worse sensation, so the recommendation is the
ceiling alone until the ceiling has been felt.

AND IT MUST NOT SIMPLY MULTIPLY BY `grip`, because grip runs 0.66 to 2.05 across the fleet. The formula
cars would place themselves twice as fast as today and the lorry a third as fast, which is not a
handling model, it is two different games. A compressed curve - something like the square root of the
ratio to a mid-fleet reference - keeps the ordering the owner asked for while keeping every car
driveable. The reference and the compression are both tunables with committed defaults.

ONE PHYSICS FOR EVERY CAR STILL HOLDS, and this does not breach it. The rule forbids a branch that
names a vehicle class to get a behaviour; it does not forbid a stat the whole fleet shares from having
one more consequence. Grip already decides two things and would decide three, from one number per car,
with no branch anywhere.

MEASURE IT THE WAY THE PUSH WAS MEASURED, and the instrument mostly exists. `brake-test.py` already
drives nobody: it puts a car on a constant bend and reads the steering ask. The steering-rate
experiment is the same harness with an input - hold a fixed steering command from a known position and
time how long the car takes to cross a lane. That IS a driver, so it has to be a steady one rather than
the drive-test autopilot, whose sawing is what RLG-055 warns about.

AND THE CORNERING HALF OF RLG-055 IS NOT SETTLED YET, which matters for the order. Two cars - the CREST
and the APEX - are pushed wider than their grip says they should be, and nothing yet says whether that
is the engine or the experiment. Adding a third consumer of `grip` before that is understood would
make it harder to diagnose, not easier.
