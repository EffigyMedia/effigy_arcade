### RLG-119 — Grip should decide how sharply a car answers the wheel, not only how wide it is pushed
- **RULED 2026-08-31T12:58:41-04:00** — Owner, 2026-08-31, on the brake and grip measurement: "Should grip not only affect being pushed on the
corner, but also your steering rate? A formula should handle extremely well and a lorry could handle
poorly."

YES, AND THE CODE CONFIRMS THE GAP RATHER THAN THE MEASUREMENT DOING IT. `grip` is consulted in
exactly two places - the braking rate, and `cornerG`, which decides how hard a bend pushes the car
wide. The rate at which a car answers the wheel is in neither:

    const grip  = (1 - Math.exp(-14*dt)) * (1 - carry*0.72);
    const want2 = clamp((targetX - playerX) * grip, -4.2*dt, 4.2*dt);

Those two numbers - the 14 and the 4.2 - are the same for every body in the game. A LORRY places
itself across the road exactly as sharply as a FORMULA car. The only thing that moves them is the
weather, through `carry`, and that is a road condition rather than a vehicle.

SO A LORRY TODAY IS SLOW, HEAVY, BAD AT STOPPING AND PUSHED WIDE IN CORNERS - and turns like a racing
car. Three of the four axes already say "lorry"; the fourth says nothing at all, which is why the owner
noticed.

THE ONE REAL RISK IS THAT THIS TOUCHES EVERY CAR THE PLAYER WILL EVER DRIVE, and it is the most
felt-per-line change in the engine. The two constants are the whole of how the game handles. Move them
per body and every car changes at once, including the three the owner has already judged on the
device. So it should ship on its own, be measurable before and after, and be the only thing in its
build.

THE SHAPE THAT FITS WHAT IS ALREADY THERE. `4.2` is a ceiling on lateral speed - how fast the car can
be placed - and `14` is how quickly it converges on the ask. Grip should scale the ceiling: a formula
car gets there faster, a lorry cannot. Scaling the convergence as well would make a low-grip car
feel LAGGY rather than heavy, which is a different and worse sensation, so the recommendation is the
ceiling alone until the ceiling has been felt.

AND IT MUST NOT SIMPLY MULTIPLY BY `grip`, because grip runs 0.66 to 2.05 across the fleet. The formula
cars would place themselves twice as fast as today and the lorry a third as fast, which is not a
handling model, it is two different games. A compressed curve - something like the square root of the
ratio to a mid-fleet reference - keeps the ordering the owner asked for while keeping every car
driveable. The reference and the compression are both tunables with committed defaults.

ONE PHYSICS FOR EVERY CAR STILL HOLDS, and this does not breach it. The rule forbids a branch that
names a vehicle class to get a behaviour; it does not forbid a stat the whole fleet shares from having
one more consequence. Grip already decides two things and would decide three, from one number per car,
with no branch anywhere.

MEASURE IT THE WAY THE PUSH WAS MEASURED, and the instrument mostly exists. `brake-test.py` already
drives nobody: it puts a car on a constant bend and reads the steering ask. The steering-rate
experiment is the same harness with an input - hold a fixed steering command from a known position and
time how long the car takes to cross a lane. That IS a driver, so it has to be a steady one rather than
the drive-test autopilot, whose sawing is what RLG-055 warns about.

AND THE CORNERING HALF OF RLG-055 IS NOT SETTLED YET, which matters for the order. Two cars - the CREST
and the APEX - are pushed wider than their grip says they should be, and nothing yet says whether that
is the engine or the experiment. Adding a third consumer of `grip` before that is understood would
make it harder to diagnose, not easier.
- **RULED 2026-08-31T16:55:04-04:00** — BUILT in v0.10.31, and it ships alone as this ruling required.

THE SHAPE IS THE ONE THIS RULING RECOMMENDED. `STEER.rate` is the ceiling on lateral speed - how fast the car can be PLACED - and grip scales it. `STEER.snap`, the convergence on the ask, is NOT scaled, because a low-grip car should feel HEAVY rather than LAGGY: scaling the convergence would make the car answer late instead of slowly, which is a different and worse sensation.

AND IT IS COMPRESSED, WHICH IS WHY THE FLEET IS STILL DRIVEABLE. `grip` runs 0.42 to 2.05, so a raw multiply would be 4.9 to 1 - two different games, as this ruling warned. `STEER.bite` is the exponent on the ratio to a mid-fleet reference and 0.5 is a square root, which compresses that to 2.2 to 1 across the whole fleet. The reference is the ROADSTER at 1.20: the car a fresh install starts in and the one the owner has felt most, so AT THE REFERENCE THE RATE IS EXACTLY WHAT IT HAS ALWAYS BEEN and the starting car does not change at all.

THE CLAMPS ARE A RAIL AND NOT A TUNING, and the first set of them was wrong in an instructive way. At 0.72 the floor caught the LORRY, the VAN, the PICKUP and the CAB and flattened all four to the same rate - which throws away the ordering at the bottom of the fleet, and the bottom of the fleet is exactly where the owner asked for it. They are 0.58 and 1.32 now, wide enough to bind on nothing the fleet actually declares. They exist so a body with a wild or missing `grip` cannot make itself undriveable.

ONE PHYSICS FOR EVERY CAR STILL HOLDS. The rule forbids a branch that names a vehicle class to get a behaviour. This is one number every car already declares, given a third consequence, with no branch anywhere. Grip decided the braking rate and `cornerG`; it decides three things now.

MEASURED, THE WAY THIS RULING ASKED. `tools/brake-test.py` gained a third experiment: the car is put on one side of the road, asked to go to the other, and timed. `API.setTarget` moves the ASK alone - `setLane` moves the ask and the car together, which is right for placing a car and useless for measuring how long it takes to get anywhere. THERE IS NO DRIVER IN IT: the ask is set once and held, so nothing steers and nothing corrects, which is this ruling's own warning about the drive-test autopilot. Every one of the eleven driveable bodies measures its declared ceiling to 0.0 per cent, ordered by grip with no inversions, spanning 3.30 to 5.49 lanes a second.

THE MEASUREMENT WAS WRONG THREE TIMES BEFORE IT WAS RIGHT, and each way is worth keeping.

  TIMING A SHORT CROSSING measured a blend of two things. The car is only rate-limited while
  the ask is far away - `want2` is `(targetX - playerX) * snap * dt` clamped to `rate * dt`,
  so the clamp stops binding once the gap falls under `rate / snap`, about a third of the
  road - and the end of any crossing is decided by the convergence rather than by the
  ceiling. The measured stretch runs from -1.00 to +0.70 with the ask held at 1.15, which is
  entirely inside the clamped region for every body in the fleet.

  TAKING THE PEAK PER-FRAME SPEED was worse and read 12 to 62 lanes a second against a
  ceiling of 4.2. The probe's rAF and the game's rAF are different callbacks and the game
  steps a FIXED timestep out of an accumulator, so a frame may advance the world twice or not
  at all. Dividing movement by the wall-clock gap between two probe samples measures the
  phase between two loops.

  DERIVING A CLOCK FROM `pos` was the third, and it read every car ABOVE a ceiling it cannot
  exceed, worst by 29 per cent. `pos` advances at `spd`, and `spd` was only being re-pinned
  once per PROBE frame - the world steps many times in between and the car coasts down
  through all of them, so the distance covered under-reported the time that had passed.

  WHAT WORKS is `API.simTime()`, the seconds the world has actually been stepped, with the
  crossing points interpolated between samples. Both this experiment and the cornering one
  measure against it now, and the cornering half of [[RLG-055]] was resolved by the same fix.

NOT VERIFIED FROM HERE, AND THIS IS THE MOST FELT-PER-LINE CHANGE IN THE ENGINE. Whether the spread is right, whether a lorry at 41 per cent below the reference is heavy or simply broken, and whether a formula car at 31 per cent above is sharp or twitchy. Three of the four cars the owner has already judged on the device - the ROADSTER, the TUNER and the MUSCLE - now handle differently from the build they judged, and only the ROADSTER is unchanged. Every number is a row in `STEER` with a committed default, so a verdict costs one number each.
