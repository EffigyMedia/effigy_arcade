## Executive Summary

The product is in good health and the machinery around it is in better health than the
record of it. Every gate passes, the build is clean, the standalone exit route still
works, and the four cabinets boot and run. Nothing found here is Critical and nothing
found here blocks a release.

The most serious finding is not in the code. **The fragment store's status field has
drifted from reality**: twenty-six rulings that the changelog says shipped are still
marked `requested`, and there is no written rule for when a multi-part ruling becomes
`built`. This cost real time on the day of the audit — two of the ten items in the
owner's own queue turned out to be already built, and were only discovered to be so by
reading the code. A store whose statuses cannot be trusted is a store that has to be
re-read in full every time it is consulted.

Two intermittent test failures are recorded rather than dismissed. One is known and
already ruled on (the corridor guarantee, RLG-037). The other is new and points at a real
seam in the coast: on a steeply falling or hard-bending road, the shoreline drawn in the
far band and the shoreline drawn in the road slices disagree by up to 57 pixels.

**The contract this audit was judged against is not a design document.** This project has
none of its own: `docs/reference/PRIVATEER.md` is Hardpoint's, frozen and pre-rename. So
correctness was judged against `CLAUDE.md`'s architecture rules and invariants, the
fragment store's rulings, and the changelog. That is stated here so a later reader does
not mistake this for a design-conformance audit.

## High Priority Issues

### AUD-001 — The fragment store's status field does not track reality
**Area:** G, documentation and process · **Severity:** High

**Evidence.** Of 83 rulings, 42 read `requested`, 40 `built`, 1 `in-flight`. Cross-checking
against the changelog: **26 rulings that the changelog cites as shipped are still marked
`requested`** — RLG-004, 022, 024, 028, 029, 033, 037, 039, 040, 050, 053, 056, 058, 059,
060, 061, 062, 063, 065, 069, 071, 073, 077, 078, 080, 082.

**Impact, measured on the day.** Two of the ten items in the owner's agreed queue —
RLG-064 (traffic de-spawn) and the brake/grip measurement RLG-055 was blocked on — turned
out to be **already built**, and that was discovered only by reading the code and running
the harness. A third of a working day's queue was planned against a record that was wrong.

**The underlying gap is a missing rule, not laziness.** Several of those 26 are legitimately
still open: RLG-059 is a long-running epic that shipped nine times today and still has work
in it. Others — RLG-004 for one — are finished and simply never flipped. Nothing says when
a multi-part ruling becomes `built`, so the honest default has been to leave it alone.

**Recommendation.** Rule on the meaning first, then reconcile. Either (a) `built` means
"every part of this ruling has shipped", and epics get closed and superseded by narrower
rulings, or (b) add a `partial` status. Then a one-pass reconciliation of the 26, and a
check in `unit.py close` that asks when a unit's `caused_by` ruling is still `requested`.

**Effort.** The rule is a conversation. The reconciliation is an hour. The check is small.

## Medium Priority Issues

### AUD-002 — The coast's two shorelines disagree on a steep or bending road
**Area:** A, correctness · **Severity:** Medium

**Evidence.** `coast-test.py` failed 2 of 8 consecutive runs at 0.9.96, both times on the
same pair of checks:

```
FAIL  the sea reaches the horizon             [left 4268 of 23520 px, threshold 20%]
FAIL  the water's edge is a line, not a staircase   [largest step 57.00 against a mean of 3.45]
```

**Reading.** A 57-pixel step in a shoreline whose average step is 3.45 is a discontinuity,
not a rough edge. The far band under the horizon draws its shoreline from the road's
VANISHING POINT (`W/2 + viewShift + bendPx`), while the drawn slices draw theirs from each
slice's own projected centre plus the roadside offset. Where the road falls away steeply —
both failures had an unusually tall gap, 23,520 px against a typical 4,000 — the two
constructions meet at an angle and the join shows.

**Impact.** Visual, intermittent, on one biome. Not a crash and not a gameplay fault. It is
the kind of seam a device report would describe as "the water jumps".

**Recommendation.** Make the far band's shoreline continue the drawn slices' line rather
than being derived independently — take the furthest slice's shore and its neighbour's, and
carry that line up. That was tried in v0.9.85 and rejected for a good reason (the guard was
untrue on a climbing road), so the fix is the same idea without the guard: extrapolate from
two slices and clamp, rather than switching construction.

**Effort.** Small, but it needs the capture loop rather than reasoning — this is the third
time the coast has been got wrong by reasoning about it.

### AUD-003 — The corridor guarantee fails about one run in four
**Area:** A, correctness · **Severity:** Medium · **Already ruled on: RLG-037**

**Evidence.** `traffic-test.py` at 0.9.96: `narrowest corridor 0.306 lane units (need 0.34)`
and `1/180 samples blocked`. The thread records 0.463, 0.378, 0.342 and 0.300 across earlier
runs. Today's two runs gave 0.306 and 0.326.

**Impact.** The gate is red about a quarter of the time on an unchanged build. That is worse
than a missing check, because a check that cries wolf gets ignored — and this one is guarding
the promise that there is always a way through the traffic.

**Recommendation.** Not a threshold change; the thread is explicit that the number must not
be moved. Either the guarantee is absolute and the engine must enforce it, or it is
statistical and the check should assert a distribution over N runs rather than a floor on
one. That is the owner's call and it is what RLG-037 is for.

### AUD-004 — The thumb cluster runs off the bottom edge on a tall screen
**Area:** F, experience · **Severity:** Medium on a tablet, Low if phones only

**Evidence.** Captured at 820×1180 during RLG-082's work: the pedal pads extend past the
bottom of the frame. The shell's `uiScale` picks the scale from the SHORT edge
(`short / 390`, capped at 1.8) and nothing caps it against the available HEIGHT, so a tall
narrow window grows the controls past the floor.

**Impact.** On a portrait tablet the brake and accelerator are clipped. The product is
declared phone-first and portrait; whether a tablet is in scope has never been ruled.

**Recommendation.** Rule on whether tablets are a target. If they are, cap the scale by
height as well as by short edge — one line in `uiScale`, and it changes how the controls
feel on every large screen, so it wants the owner's eye.

### AUD-005 — The launcher's own stores break the documented key shape
**Area:** B, architecture · **Severity:** Medium

**Evidence.** `CLAUDE.md` states every persisted key is `effigyarcade.<scope>.<what>.v1`,
with `launcher` a reserved scope, and gives the table. The launcher writes three keys that
have no scope segment at all:

```
index.html:618  effigyarcade.sort.v1
index.html:672  effigyarcade.favs.v1
index.html:902  effigyarcade.prefetched.v1
```

None appears in the documented table, and none is covered by `smoke-test.py`'s `saves()`
check, which tests `effigyarcade.inter.*`, `effigyarcade.interstate.audio.v1` and
`effigyarcade.launcher.audio.v1` — the shapes that DO conform.

**Impact is smaller than it looks, and worth stating precisely.** `Arcade.save.clearAll`
erases anything with the `effigyarcade.` prefix, so the whole-arcade erase does reach them.
What cannot reach them is `Arcade.save.clear(id)`, which is per-machine and correctly should
not. So there is no live data-loss bug — what is wrong is that the documented invariant is
false, and the guard that exists to keep it true does not cover the three keys that break it.

**Recommendation.** Either rename them to `effigyarcade.launcher.<what>.v1` with a one-time
migration, or document them as the launcher's own exception. Then add them to `saves()`,
because the rule in `CLAUDE.md` says a new store must go into that check and these three
never did.

### AUD-006 — Fifteen of the launcher's eighteen attract painters are dead
**Area:** B, dead code · **Severity:** Low

**Evidence.** `games.js` uses four attract names (`grid`, `road` twice, `hunt`). `index.html`'s
`draw` map holds eighteen: `fold2, dive, maze, blocks, scope, ranks, fold, drain, scaffold,
fibre, radar, ice, strata, profile, soap` are unreferenced — the cabinets of Tiny Arcade,
which is a separate and parked repository.

**Impact.** Roughly a thousand lines of unreachable drawing code in the one file where
`CLAUDE.md` records that a stray `}` silently kills every rule below it. It is not a defect;
it is surface area in a fragile place.

**Recommendation.** Delete them, or move them to a `docs/reference/` file if they are wanted
as a source for future cabinets. A fifth machine would want one of them at most.

### AUD-007 — `overBrow` has seven unreachable lines

**Impact.** None at runtime. It reads as an oversight to anyone who has not read the three
comments.

**Recommendation.** Delete the body and keep the note, or delete the function and its three

## Missing Documentation

- **This project has no design document of its own.** `docs/reference/PRIVATEER.md` is
  Hardpoint's, frozen, and pre-dates the rename; `DESIGN.md`, `DRIVING.md` and `REFACTOR.md`
  are Tiny Arcade's, inherited and frozen. The contract is `CLAUDE.md` plus the fragment
  store. That works — the store is unusually good — but it means there is no single statement
  of what any of the four machines is meant to BE, and the CLAUDE.md itself says each is
  "meant to be built out into a full multi-scene game" with "nothing of that built yet".
- **No technical reference exists for the launcher**, which is the only wholly new subsystem
  in this repository and the one this audit found two findings in.

## Coverage Statement

**Covered:** the four cabinets' boot and run paths; the launcher's architecture boundary,
storage keys, attract map and stylesheet integrity; the driving engine's collision, lamp,
gate, HUD, audio-routing and biome/coast paths; the build and standalone routes; the fragment
store's link integrity and status accuracy; the changelog's link integrity; the never-commit
list; the harness suite's runnability and its intermittency; frame rate across all seven
biomes; brake and grip across sixteen bodies.

**Not covered, and stated so a partial pass is not read as a clean bill of health:**

- **Quietus and Hardpoint were audited only at the boot-and-paint level.** Their internal
  logic, save shapes and content were not read. The driving games got the depth here because
  that is where the day's work was and where the engine is shared.
- **No device.** Every figure in this report comes from a headless desktop browser. Rendering,
  audio mix, on-device layout and feel are not judged.
- **Security and privacy were checked only at the level this product has**: no keys, tokens
  or credentials exist in the repository, no licensed material is committed, and the
  never-commit list is enforced (0 files matching the zip or scratch patterns are tracked).
  There is no network call at launch and no user data leaves the device, so there is no trust
  boundary to audit beyond `localStorage`.
- **Dependency management** is not applicable: no toolchain, no package manifest, no
  third-party runtime code.
- **Scalability at 10x** is not meaningful for a static site with four pages.
